Employment IP Audit

One-time review. Must happen before FlashFusion signs any client contract. Not optional.
Your employer is an MSP. FlashFusion is AI enablement consulting. Close enough to adjacent that a broad IP clause in your employment agreement could create a cloud on FlashFusion's assets — especially any tools built using skills you developed at work, or any time a client might overlap. Needs a clean read before money changes hands.

What Your Attorney Is Looking For

  • !IP Assignment Clause — "All inventions" vs. "inventions related to employer's business." Narrower = better for you.
  • !Work-for-Hire Language — Does it claim anything created using employer equipment or on employer time?
  • !Non-Compete — You believe there isn't one. Verify exact language.
  • !Moonlighting / Outside Employment — Does it require disclosure of outside business activity?
  • !Non-Solicitation — Restricts you from soliciting employer's clients? Understand the scope.
  • →Illinois Employee Patent Act (765 ILCS 1060/2) — State law exempts IP developed entirely on your own time, without employer equipment, not related to employer's business. Know if this applies.

Brief for Attorney

EMPLOYMENT IP REVIEW — ATTORNEY BRIEF I am a full-time employee at a managed IT services firm. I am forming FlashFusion LLC, a B2B AI enablement consultancy. I believe I have no non-compete clause but need the exact language confirmed. Questions for review: 1. What does the IP assignment clause cover — is it broad ("all inventions") or narrow ("inventions related to employer's business")? 2. Does the clause reach work done entirely on my own time using my own equipment? 3. My employer is an MSP (managed IT / service desk). My business is B2B AI enablement consulting. Is there a colorable argument that these are related enough to create an IP claim issue? 4. Do I have any disclosure obligation before taking paying clients? 5. Are there non-solicitation restrictions I need to structure around? 6. Does the Illinois Employee Patent Act (765 ILCS 1060/2) protect any of my pre-formation work from employer claims? Please advise before I execute IP Assignment Agreements or sign any client contracts under FlashFusion LLC. Note: Two internal tools were built for the employer with employer knowledge. Please advise how this affects my IP position.

What to Do Now, Before the Attorney Review

  • →All FlashFusion work on personal devices only
  • →No FlashFusion work during employer's work hours
  • →No FlashFusion tools built using employer systems, credentials, or licenses
  • →No FlashFusion client that is a current employer client

Co-Creator Agreement — Chaos Club Co.

For your friend who's going to school to teach. Draft before any content is created together. Attorney reviews before signing.
The most common way these fall apart: one person does more work, one person needs to stop, or both people disagree on direction. The agreement exists so that conversation isn't also a legal crisis. The key questions to answer before you draft: What does each person contribute? What does each person own? What happens if one person exits?

Questions to Settle Before This Gets Drafted

  • ?Is your friend a co-founder (equity) or a contractor (paid per deliverable)?
  • ?Does your friend contribute content/curriculum expertise only, or also platform/technical work?
  • ?If your friend exits mid-build, who owns the curriculum they helped create?
  • ?What's the revenue split, if any? Is your friend being paid, taking equity, or both?
  • ?Does their school program have any claim on work they produce while enrolled?

CLC Partnership — Attorney Brief

Before you sign anything with College of Lake County. Institutions are notorious for IP grabs in partnership agreements.
Institutions — especially community colleges — have standard partnership agreement templates that are written entirely in their favor. Common landmines: they claim ownership of curriculum developed "in connection with" the partnership, they require exclusivity, they can terminate for any reason. You need eyes on the contract before you sign anything, even an MOU.

Questions to Raise with Your CLC Contacts Before Formalizing

  • ?What type of agreement does CLC typically use — MOU, licensing agreement, vendor contract, or something else?
  • ?Does CLC claim any IP on curriculum or tools developed in partnership with them?
  • ?Are you being brought in as a vendor, a partner, or something informal (guest lecturer, advisor)?
  • ?Is there an exclusivity expectation — i.e., can you offer this curriculum through other channels?
  • ?Does CLC have a procurement process that requires contracts go through legal/admin vs. direct relationship?

Brief for Attorney — CLC Agreement Review

CLC PARTNERSHIP — ATTORNEY BRIEF I am the founder of Chaos Club Co. LLC, an educational platform. I am in discussions with contacts at College of Lake County (CLC) about a partnership to develop and deliver educational content through or in collaboration with the institution. I need you to review any agreement CLC presents before I sign, and to advise me on the following: 1. IP OWNERSHIP Does the agreement contain any clause that assigns or licenses curriculum, content, platform, or tool IP to CLC? I need to retain ownership of all IP I bring in and all IP I create. 2. EXCLUSIVITY Does the agreement restrict me from offering this curriculum or platform through other channels (other schools, direct to consumer, online)? 3. TERMINATION What are the termination provisions? Can CLC terminate unilaterally? What happens to IP and work product on termination? 4. REVENUE If any revenue-sharing or licensing fee structure is proposed, review the terms and advise on market reasonableness. 5. INDEMNIFICATION What does CLC require me to indemnify them for? Assess exposure given that I am a single-member LLC. 6. PRE-SIGNING CHECKLIST Before I sign, I need written confirmation that: (a) Chaos Club Co. LLC retains all IP (b) No exclusivity without my explicit written consent (c) Clear termination terms with IP protection (d) Indemnification is reasonable and insurable If CLC presents a standard MOU rather than a formal contract, advise whether it creates any binding obligations I should be aware of before proceeding.
Even if the conversation with your CLC contacts is informal right now — don't let it get to "we shook on it" before the agreement is reviewed. Relationships at institutions are separate from what the institution's standard paperwork says.

Educational Content Licensing Framework

Who owns the curriculum. How it can be used. Required once you're producing content with your co-creator and distributing through CLC or other channels.
Three layers here: (1) Chaos Club Co. LLC owns all curriculum. (2) CLC (or any partner institution) gets a limited license to use it — not ownership. (3) Your co-creator contributed under a work-for-hire arrangement, so their contributions already belong to the LLC. This framework documents that cleanly.
