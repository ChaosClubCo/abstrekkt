# Memoir Arc — A 12-Essay Series

> Working spec. Titles are scaffolding; rewrite to fit your real beats.
> Cross-post distribution: kylerosebrook.com (canonical) → LinkedIn (adapted) → newsletter (when ready).

---

## What this series is for

A 12-essay run that produces, by the end:

1. **A first-pass memoir manuscript** — each essay is a chapter draft that's been pressure-tested by readers.
2. **An audience that already trusts your voice** — the people who'll buy the eventual book are the ones reading these essays now.

Engagement metrics are not the success criterion. The success criterion is *"12 essays I'd put in a book."* Decide that upfront, in writing, before essay 1 ships — otherwise the LinkedIn dashboard will steer the work and you'll end up with bait posts instead of memoir.

## The arc

### Act I — Before
*Who you were when this story starts.*

| # | Mode | Working title | Angle |
|---|------|---------------|-------|
| 01 | scene-led | The last shift | Open inside a specific night you can still smell. Bar, kitchen, tip jar, the regular at the end of the rail. Let the reader stand there with you before the story moves. |
| 02 | reflective | Service was not a lesser art | Defend the work. Read the room, hold pressure, become useful invisibly. The transferable skills nobody credits as skills. |
| 03 | scene + reflection | The quality of things | The QC chapter. Attention as discipline. Learning to see what's broken before the customer does. The first time precision became a practice. |
| 04 | quiet / structural | Ceilings you don't see until you hit them | The conditions that made the old life stop being viable. No blame — structural. The moment the math stopped working. |

### Act II — The crossing
*Pivot, struggle, transformation.*

| # | Mode | Working title | Angle |
|---|------|---------------|-------|
| 05 | turning point — scene-led | The first useful sentence | First encounter with AI that wasn't trivial. Where you were sitting, what you typed, what came back, what shifted. |
| 06 | process / montage | Apprentice years, compressed | Teaching yourself a craft with no school. The night hours, the printed-out PDFs, the YouTube tabs at 2x. What it cost to learn this way. |
| 07 | scene + dialogue | Hired into a room you have no credentials for | The Intinc interview. What the room was actually testing for. The moment you realized fluency had become its own credential. |
| 08 | essay / material | The hands you build with | The workspace. The screen layout. The shortcuts, the rituals, the stack. Mechanics of the new life — the way a chef writes about their knives. |

### Act III — After
*Who you are now, what's still unresolved.*

| # | Mode | Working title | Angle |
|---|------|---------------|-------|
| 09 | essay / comparative | What bartending taught me about software | The skills that actually transferred — and the ones that surprised you. Avoid the LinkedIn-influencer version of this essay. Specific, not aspirational. |
| 10 | manifesto-adjacent | The lane without a lane | Building outside the day job. The accumulator philosophy. Why "hobby" undersells what it is. Earn this one — manifesto without scenes is sermon. |
| 11 | direct address (letter) | To the person still on the other side | Direct address. Someone in your old seat. What you'd say. Resist advice — describe instead. |
| 12 | reflective — leave open | Unsettled | Refuse the tidy ending. What you don't yet know. What's true today and might not be tomorrow. The reader should close it wanting to know what you do next. |

## Voice rules

- First person. Past tense braided with present-tense reflection.
- **Specific over abstract.** Brand names, model numbers, the actual smell of the kitchen, the actual song on the speaker. Specificity is what separates memoir from LinkedIn essay.
- **No "lessons learned" close.** Let the reader extract. The moment you write "and what I learned was…" you've turned memoir into content.
- **One concrete image per essay** that stays with the reader after they close the tab. Pick it before you draft.
- **Pacing rhythm**: scene → reflection → scene. Avoid two reflective passages in a row.
- **Length**: 1,200–1,800 words. Long enough to land, short enough to read on a phone.
- **Read it aloud once before posting.** Anywhere your tongue trips, the reader's eye trips.

---

## Posting cadence

**Recommended: bi-weekly, Sunday 7am Central, 26-week run.**

Why bi-weekly and not weekly: each essay needs ~4–6 hours of writing + revision + 48-hour cool-off. Weekly with a day job will burn you out by essay 6 and the work will degrade. Bi-weekly gives you the buffer to ship at quality.

**Buffer rule:** never post essay N if you don't have a complete draft of essay N+2 already written. If the buffer drops below 2 essays ahead, post a "pause" note rather than ship something thin.

**Fallback cadence**: monthly, first Sunday — if life surges, drop to this rather than skip.

## Per-essay ship gate

Before any essay ships, all six must be true:

- [ ] Contains one specific scene with sensory detail (sight + at least one of: sound, smell, taste, touch).
- [ ] Contains one uncomfortable truth — something you'd rather not have to write. If everything in the essay flatters the narrator, it's not memoir yet.
- [ ] No listicle close. No "what I learned." No "here are 3 takeaways."
- [ ] Read aloud once, marked up, revised.
- [ ] 48-hour cool-off after the final revision. Read once more before posting.
- [ ] Hero image selected (B&W, monochrome palette consistent with kylerosebrook.com).

## Series-level milestones

| Milestone | Trigger | Action |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| Act I complete (essay 4 published) | ~Week 8 | Read all 4 in sequence. Are they actually one story? Adjust Act II angles if not. |
| Halfway (essay 6 published) | ~Week 12 | Honest check: would *you* keep reading this if it weren't yours? If no, what's missing — voice, stakes, or scene? |
| Act II complete (essay 8 published) | ~Week 16 | Send the 8-essay run to 3 trusted readers (memoir-literate, not just supportive). Ask: where do you stop trusting the narrator? Where do you skim? |
| Series complete (essay 12 published) | ~Week 26 | Compile all 12 into a single document. This is your manuscript zero-draft. Decide: agent route, hybrid, or self-pub from here. |

## Open questions (answer before essay 1)

These are decisions that are cheaper to make on paper than discover in the middle of essay 7:

1. **What is genuinely off-limits?** Names, places, relationships, specifics of the transformation that you will not write about publicly. Map this *before* you start, not in the middle of an essay you've already half-written.
2. **Whose permission do you need?** People still in your life who will appear in scenes. The conversation goes better before publication than after.
3. **What is the one image from your life that, if it ended up on the cover of the book, you would be okay with?** That image is probably hiding inside one of these essays. Find which one.
4. **Are you writing toward an agent, a publisher, or self-publication?** This doesn't change the essays, but it changes how you frame the eventual pitch — and the framing affects which beats you'll want to surface in essay 12.

---

## What this spec is not

It's not your story. The titles are placeholders that hold structural shape — *the last shift*, *the first useful sentence*, *unsettled* — but the truth in each of those slots is yours to put there. Expect to rewrite at least 70% of the titles once you start drafting. The structure is what's load-bearing; the language on top of it is decoration until you replace it.
