## Discovery Framework (Kelan's Reference — Given to Kelan Before E8)

Kelan should have this memorized or on a notecard (not on the shared screen). This is the call structure.

### Pre-Call (5 min before)
- Know Marcus's company (Auditly — compliance SaaS, Series B, 120 employees)
- Know the scenario: they're doing AI exploration, compliance team overhead
- Have the demo environment open and tested
- Have the next step ask ready

### Call Opening (2 min)
"Marcus, thanks for making time. I want to respect your calendar — here's what I'm thinking for today: I'll ask you a few questions about where your team is with AI and compliance tooling, then I'll show you about 6 minutes of the product, and we'll use whatever time is left to talk about whether this makes sense for Auditly. Does that work?"

Why: Sets agenda. Signals you're not going to pitch for 30 minutes. Invites consent.

### Discovery (8–10 min)

**Question sequence (adjust based on responses, but hit all 5):**

1. "Tell me about your current compliance workflow — specifically the audit prep side. Where does your team spend the most time?"

2. "You mentioned [whatever they said] — how long has that been a problem, and what have you tried to solve it?"

3. "Have you explored AI tooling for compliance work? What have you looked at, and what was that experience like?"

4. "If you were to solve this problem, what's the business outcome — is there a specific audit cycle or deadline driving urgency?"

5. "When you make a decision like this, who else is involved? Is it primarily you, or do you bring in security and legal?"

**What to listen for:**
- Pain that connects to ComplianceReport or SecurityScan (audit overhead, security review blocking AI)
- Timeline pressure (upcoming audit, Q2 deadline)
- Budget authority and process
- Prior AI experience (bad experience = SecurityScan anchor is your opening)

**Bridge to demo:**
"Based on what you've told me, I think I can show you something directly relevant. Do you have 6 minutes for a live demo?"

Always ask for permission before launching the demo.

### Demo (6 min max)

Five-beat structure. See E5 reference card.

When Marcus interrupts with the acquisition objection:
"Good question — let me finish this step so you can see the data handling in context, then I'll address that directly."
[Continue Beat 4 or 5.]
"On the acquisition question: your data lives in your tenant. Full stop. It's not stored in Claude, it's not in Anthropic's infrastructure. If FlashFusion ceased to exist tomorrow, every ComplianceReport you've generated lives in your tenant, in your control. Does that address the concern?"

### Commercial (5–7 min)

After demo close and pause:

"Based on what you saw — does this connect to the problem you described?"

Wait for their response. If yes:

"The way we typically work with teams like yours is a 6-week POC — one agent, live in your environment, with a full audit trail and a ComplianceReport ready before your next audit cycle. The POC is $20,000. After sign-off, the platform starts at $60,000 annually. Given the Q2 timeline you mentioned, we'd want to kick off no later than [date 6 weeks before audit date]."

If they raise budget concern:

See E7, Objection 6 response.

### Close (2 min)

"I'd like to schedule a follow-up call with you and your security team so Kyle can walk through the architecture directly. Would [day] or [day] work for a 30-minute call?"

Wait. Do not fill the silence. Do not offer alternatives before they respond.

Acceptable outcomes:
- Scheduled meeting (win)
- "Let me check my calendar and get back to you" + commitment to specific timeline (acceptable)
- Clear objection you can address (useful)
- "Not interested" (honest, useful)

Not acceptable:
- "Let me know if you want to learn more" (not a close)
- Vague "I'll think about it" without a follow-up plan (not a close)
