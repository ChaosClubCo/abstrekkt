Theme 1 — Context Engineering
LinkedIn
The prompt was never the problem. The problem was what surrounded it. The files you did or didn't give it. The constraints you did or didn't write down. Whether the model had any idea what "your project" meant before you started typing. Prompt engineering is what you call it when you're trying to fix an environment problem with vocabulary. Context engineering is what you do when you realize the prompt lives inside a larger system. CLAUDE.md. Persistent instructions. Tool access. Session hygiene. These determine 80% of your output quality. The other 20% is the prompt you're obsessing over.
~100 words · no hashtags · no CTA required
X / Twitter
Prompt engineering is debugging symptoms. Context engineering fixes the cause. Your CLAUDE.md. Your tool access. Your session hygiene. Your persistent instructions. That's where 80% of the variance lives.
Short post · strong standalone
X / Threads — "Socratic Clarifier"
If you're getting 2,000 words of well-formatted wrong output, the model didn't fail. You let it guess. Before any complex task: "Do not start working yet." Force it to ask 4-5 questions about constraints and edge cases first. Then let it run. Cost: 30 seconds. Benefit: not untangling a bad assumption buried in 500 tokens.
Observation · practical · no hype language
Theme 2 — Instructional Orchestration
LinkedIn
If changing one word in your prompt breaks your AI workflow, the workflow isn't working. It's passing. Reliability comes from decoupling the logic from the phrasing. CLAUDE.md does this at the session level. Structured output schemas do this at the response level. Frameworks like DSPy do this at the build level — the prompt becomes something you compile, not something you write and pray. You're not writing spells. You're writing contracts. Contracts that specify what good output looks like, not just what you're asking for.
~90 words · B2B angle · works for FlashFusion or personal
X / Twitter
One word changed. Output collapsed. If that's your system, you don't have a system. XML boundaries. Structured schemas. Compiled prompts. Stop writing spells. Write contracts.
Hot take · punchy close
X / Twitter — XML boundaries
Reasoning models hold instructions better when you give them structured boundaries. <scratchpad> tags separate the model's internal reasoning from the output it delivers. Not magic. Just scope management. The model doesn't drift if you give it walls.
Technical observation · no hype
Theme 3 — Reasoning Patterns
LinkedIn
The reason "think step by step" works is the same reason rubber duck debugging works. The model's response to each step becomes context for the next one. You're not unlocking hidden reasoning capacity. You're making it show its work before it commits — which catches wrong turns earlier. Chain-of-thought. Least-to-most. Tree of thoughts. Different versions of the same instruction: don't skip steps. The failure mode isn't a dumb model. It's a model that jumped to an answer before it knew the full shape of the problem. Ask it to show its work. Then act on the work, not the conclusion.
~90 words · educator register · good for CLC or newsletter
X / Twitter
LLMs don't reason. They predict the next token. "Think step by step" works because the model uses its own generated logic as context for the next token. You're not making it smarter. You're making it slower in the right direction.
Contrarian framing · accurate · no hype
X / Twitter — Tree of Thoughts
When quality matters more than speed, let the model branch. Tree of Thoughts: multiple reasoning paths, self-evaluation, backtracking from dead ends. Takes longer. Costs more tokens. Returns better answers on high-stakes decisions. Know when to use it.
Short · practical · no buzzwords
Theme 4 — The Re-Sourcing / Outsourcing Disruption
LinkedIn
Klarna's announcement that one AI assistant absorbed the workload of 700 customer service agents didn't surprise me. I work in Tier-1 IT support. Every task I do is documented, standardized, and measured by SLA. The same documentation that exists so a new hire can be trained in a week also exists so an agent can be configured in an afternoon. The reason IT support was easy to outsource is the same reason it's easy to automate. Standardization was always the point. In February 2026, India's IT sector lost roughly $10 billion in market value in a week. TCS announced 12,000 layoffs. That's not a correction. That's a repricing.

Theme 5 — Outcome-Aware AI
LinkedIn
The difference between AI as a tool and AI as infrastructure isn't the model. It's whether the model knows what your company is actually trying to do. If your AI doesn't know your pricing model, your customer segments, your standing constraints, your preferred stack — it's giving advice for a generic company that doesn't exist. A Business Brain isn't a concept. It's a CLAUDE.md file, a structured skills library, a document the model reads every session before it does anything. The companies scaling without headcount aren't running smarter prompts. They're running AI that knows the business before the conversation starts.

Theme 6 — AppSec / Ghost in the Code
LinkedIn
Static security scanners flag variables entering query strings as SQL injection vulnerabilities. They don't check whether the variable was already validated. They don't check whether an attacker can actually reach the vulnerable function. They produce 47 critical CVEs where 3 are real and 44 are noise. Then security teams build processes around reviewing 47 findings. AI-native security walks the call graph. It knows whether the execution path ever reaches the vulnerable code. Three findings, not 47. The release ships. This isn't AI hype. It's graph traversal applied to security review. The math is straightforward. The tooling is new. (World-class security audits are now catching real bugs in curl, FFmpeg, and django-allauth that pattern matchers missed.)
~120 words · technical but accessible · sourced claim at end
X / Twitter
47 critical CVEs flagged. 44 of them can't be reached by an attacker. Traditional scanners don't check reachability. They flag the string. AI walks the call graph. Checks if the path exists. Cuts 90% of the noise. That's not intelligence. That's graph traversal. The tooling finally exists.
Short · specific · no "AI is amazing" framing
X / Twitter — PDF reports
300-page security scan reports with 95% false positives don't make software secure. They make engineers ignore real threats because they've been desensitized to noise. If your security tool can't write the pull request to fix the bug, it's just generating paperwork.
