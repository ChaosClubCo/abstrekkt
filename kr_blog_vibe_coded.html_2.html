The Vibe-Coded SaaS Tell List

Kyle Rosebrook · May 2026

You learn to recognize it fast. The same signals, same order, different URL.

Emojis as navigation and decoration. Not icons from an actual library — Unicode characters standing in for a design decision no one made. Professional products pick a system. This one skipped the choice.

Em-dashes everywhere. One or two is a style. Nine in a hero section is a tell that the copy ran off a model without anyone reading it before push.

Same font everyone else is using. Not because someone made a typography call — because the default wasn't changed. Type is tone. If your site looks identical to the last four tools I evaluated, you outsourced that decision without knowing you did.

AI-generated logo. There's a specific geometry to these — gradient, symbol, weight-matched wordmark. It reads immediately. It also signals what the rest of the product's quality bar looks like.

No evidence the product exists. No screenshots. No demo video. No GIF of the actual UI doing something real. Just a description of features distributed across six sections, none of which show the thing working.

Too much text, wrong structure. Every section has an eyebrow label, a headline, a sub-headline, and two paragraphs. Professional sites are quieter. One idea per section. Space doing work. When everything is emphasized, the emphasis is gone.

No company behind the company. No address. No team page. No LinkedIn that resolves to a real person. If I'm evaluating whether to put your software in my stack, I need to know you'll exist in six months. "Built by a passionate team" isn't that.

Anything broken on the production site. Nav overlapping on mobile. A 404 on the primary CTA. An animation locking the tab. If the marketing site ships with visible defects, I assume the same QC runs on the product. You get one impression.

Generic metrics with no methodology. "10x your productivity." "Reduce churn by 40%." A number without a source, a cohort, or a timeframe isn't data. It's a formatting choice applied to wishful thinking. Anyone who's been burned by a vendor pitch knows exactly what that looks like.

The list isn't a checklist for indie builders. It's a pattern. The same pattern, repeated across enough sites that you stop being surprised and start just noting which items are present.
