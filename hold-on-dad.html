Hold On, Dad

A letter from the future. A child, grown, writes back to a parent drowning in shame—telling him what he couldn't hear in the darkness: your presence was never a burden. Your survival was the promise I needed.

This song cuts through the isolation of depression by asking the hardest question: "What if the person you think you're sparing is actually waiting for you?"

It challenges the lie that perfection, sobriety, or conquest is the price of love. Instead, it offers something quieter and fiercer—that showing up broken, making mistakes, breathing through the chaos is exactly what mattered.

For anyone who's:

  • • Felt like a burden to the people they love
  • • Believed that leaving would somehow ease the pain for others
  • • Struggled with shame that whispers you're not enough
  • • Wondered if survival was worth it

Why This Matters to Me

— From me to you

I wrote this because I know what it feels like when the voice in your head gets so loud that leaving seems like the kindest thing you could do—and I need you to know that that voice is lying.

I'm a father, an engineer, someone who builds things for other people to trust. For the longest time, I couldn't apply that same care to myself. I thought I had to be perfect to deserve the people I love—until my daughter showed me that my broken, trying, real self was exactly what she needed.

If you're reading this and you believe everyone would be better off without you, I'm telling you directly: you're wrong. And there's someone—maybe someone you don't even know about yet—whose entire future depends on you staying.

I'm putting this out because shame dies in silence. Your presence isn't a burden; it's a promise. And showing up at all is everything.
