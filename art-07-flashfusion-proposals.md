## TEMPLATE 1 — AI Setup Audit ($4,500)

**Purpose:** Diagnostic engagement. Customer leaves with a written readout, a clear yes/no/maybe on follow-on work, and a deliverable they can take to their CFO.

---

### Subject line
Proposal: AI Setup Audit for [Company Name]

### Opening
Hi [Name],

Following our discovery call on [date], here's the proposal for the AI Setup Audit.

### What you get

**90-minute live audit session.** I'll walk through your current AI setup with whoever needs to be in the room. We cover: tooling currently deployed, intended workflows, actual workflows, gaps, risks, and the spots where the gap between intent and operation is largest. Recorded with your permission for reference.

**8-12 page written readout, delivered within 7 days of the audit session.** Specific findings, not generic advice. Each finding has: what's happening, why it matters, what to do about it, and roughly what it would take. Includes a clear recommendation: do nothing right now / Sprint engagement / Retainer engagement / something else.

**30-minute follow-up call** scheduled 7-14 days after readout delivery. Walk through the findings with your team, answer questions, decide on next steps if any.

### What it costs

**$4,500, fixed fee.** Payment terms: $1,500 on signed agreement, $3,000 on delivery of the written readout. No hourly billing, no scope creep, no surprises.

### What it doesn't include

- Implementation work (that's a Sprint or Retainer)
- Vendor negotiation (separate engagement if needed)
- Ongoing support beyond the 30-minute follow-up call
- Custom skill or workflow builds (that's a Sprint)

### Timeline

| Week | Activity |
|------|----------|
| Week 1 | Signed agreement, deposit, audit session scheduled |
| Week 1-2 | 90-minute live audit |
| Week 2-3 | Written readout delivered (within 7 days of audit) |
| Week 3-4 | 30-minute follow-up call |

Total elapsed: 3-4 weeks. Total customer time commitment: ~2.5 hours.

### What I need from you

1. Signed agreement (I'll send a 1-page agreement on yes)
2. Deposit ($1,500) via [Stripe/wire — confirm preference]
3. Audit session scheduled — calendar link will follow
4. Access to the systems we'll review (read-only is fine for the audit)
5. The right people in the room: whoever owns the AI deployment + whoever owns the metrics that depend on it

The audit is the most common entry point. About a third of audits become Sprints; about a quarter become Retainers. The rest are one-and-done diagnostics, which is also fine — the audit standalone is the product.

## TEMPLATE 2 — Enablement Sprint ($5,000)

**Purpose:** Deliver a custom skill pack, workflow, or capability that addresses a specific operational problem identified in the audit (or surfaced through other discovery).

---

### Subject line
Proposal: [Sprint description] for [Company Name]

### Opening
Hi [Name],

Here's the proposal for the Enablement Sprint, scoped to [the specific problem from discovery / audit findings].

### What you get

**A working capability, scoped to a specific problem.** This Sprint delivers [3-5 skill pack items / one workflow integration / one custom prompt library — pick one]. The capability is installed in your environment, validated with you on a live working session, and handed off with a runbook your team can use independently.

**Four weeks of scoped work, delivered in three milestones:**

1. **Week 1 — scoping & first build.** Confirmed scope document, first deliverable component, validation criteria
2. **Weeks 2-3 — iterative build.** Remaining components, integration with your existing tooling, internal testing
3. **Week 4 — handoff.** Live working session with your team, runbook delivered, 14-day support window opens

**14-day post-handoff support window.** I'll respond to questions, fix anything broken on delivery, adjust if the live use surfaces gaps. After 14 days, support transitions to either a Retainer engagement or end-of-engagement, your choice.

### What it costs

**$5,000, fixed fee.** Payment: $1,500 on signed agreement, $1,500 on Week 2 milestone, $2,000 on handoff. Same discipline as the audit: no hourly billing, no scope creep.

### What it doesn't include

- Discovery (covered separately by the AI Setup Audit, or in a paid scoping conversation if no audit was done)
- Ongoing support beyond the 14-day window (that's a Retainer)
- Multi-tenant or enterprise compliance work (separate engagement)
- Custom integration with internal tooling beyond what's stated in scope

### Scope discipline

Scope is locked at agreement signing. Mid-sprint scope changes get one of three responses:
- "That fits within scope, here's the plan to include it" — most common, no extra cost
- "That's adjacent to scope but we can include it in exchange for [specific tradeoff]"
- "That's out of scope. We can finish this Sprint and start a separate engagement to address it."

The third option is rarer than people expect. The discipline protects both sides.

### Reference: Chris Butler 5-skill suite

Most directly comparable: 3-week Sprint, 5 skills covering an interview-to-deliverable workflow. Delivered Q1 2026. Suite is in daily production use, paid back the engagement cost within ~6 weeks based on client time-tracking. Case study available on request.

## TEMPLATE 3 — Managed Retainer ($2,000/month)

**Purpose:** Ongoing operational support for AI capabilities already deployed. Either follows a Sprint or starts after an Audit recommends it.

---

### Subject line
Proposal: Managed Retainer for [Company Name]

### Opening
Hi [Name],

Following [the Sprint / the audit recommendation / our recent conversation], here's the proposal for an ongoing Managed Retainer.

### What you get

**Up to 8 hours of focused work per month**, drawn down as needed against an agreed scope. Examples:

- Skill pack updates and additions
- Workflow optimization based on use data
- New prompts or integrations as your needs evolve
- Light troubleshooting and diagnosis when things break
- Quarterly review of what's working and what isn't

**Monthly written report**, delivered the first week of each month, covering: what was delivered the prior month, what's queued for the current month, any patterns I'm noticing in your use, recommended adjustments.

**Same-week response time** for non-urgent requests. Same-business-day for urgent. Defined in the agreement.

**Quarterly strategic review (60 minutes).** A standing call to look at the broader picture: what's the AI capability stack actually delivering, what's the ROI shape, what should we be doing differently.

### What it costs

**$2,000/month**, paid in advance. Three-month minimum commitment, then month-to-month. Cancellation requires 30 days notice.

### What it doesn't include

- Net-new Sprint-scale builds (those are separate engagements; the retainer can include design and scoping for a future Sprint)
- Compliance audits (separate)
- Vendor management beyond the AI tooling specifically
- Implementation outside the AI capability stack

### Hours that aren't used in a month

Up to 4 hours roll forward to the next month. Any unused beyond that don't carry — the retainer is bought capacity, not a stockpile. This keeps the engagement cost-predictable on both sides.

### Why this exists

The most common failure mode for AI deployments isn't the build — it's the drift. Models change. Workflows shift. Internal teams turn over. Without a maintained capability, the value of the original investment decays inside 6-12 months. The retainer is the compound-interest engagement: small monthly inputs, durable capability over time.

### What I need from you

1. Signed agreement (3-month minimum, then month-to-month)
2. First month's payment ($2,000)
3. Continued access to the systems being maintained
4. A primary point of contact who can scope monthly draws
5. 60 minutes per quarter for the strategic review

### Termination

Either side can terminate with 30 days notice for any reason. No questions, no penalties. The retainer works because both sides want to be in it.

## Authoring rules (delete before sending any of the above)

1. **Always personalize.** Replace bracketed sections, drop in specific details from the discovery call. Generic = ignored.
2. **Always lead with the proposal subject line.** Don't bury the lede in pleasantries.
3. **Hold the price.** First customer pushback on price gets the same response: hold, refer to the free scorecard if not ready, lose the deal cleanly. Discounting breaks the floor for every subsequent conversation.
4. **Always include a deadline.** Without one, decisions don't happen.
5. **Always say what's not included.** This is a feature, not a flaw. Customers trust scoping discipline.
6. **Never name your day-job employer.** No "Intinc," no "MSP," no internal references. FlashFusion is the brand.
7. **One proposal per conversation.** Don't send the customer a menu of three options. Pick the right one based on the discovery, and send that one.
