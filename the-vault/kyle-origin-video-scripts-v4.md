# Kyle Rosebrook — Origin Story Video Scripts v4
## "Not Building in the Shadows Anymore"
*The real version.*

---

## VERSION 1: YouTube Anchor (4–5 min)

---

**[OPEN — quiet, direct, no performance]**

I want to tell you how I got this job.

Not because it's impressive. Because it's the most honest thing I can show you about how I think.

---

**[THE CONTEXT — fast, no dwelling]**

A few years before this, I overdosed.

Accidentally mixed two prescriptions. Wrong timing.

I got a brain scan after — processing speed, 99th percentile. Recall memory, 12th percentile. The engine was intact. The storage wasn't.

Here's what that actually looks like. I spent a year learning a Ludovico Einaudi piece on piano — Nuvole Bianche. I still can't read sheet music. I can't name a single key I was hitting. I just memorized the shape: the order, the arpeggios, where the syncopation falls.

Not the notes. The pattern.

I didn't recover from that. I built something different. External systems for a brain I couldn't fully trust. Tools as memory. Documentation as survival.

That's the foundation. Keep it in mind.

---

**[THE INTERVIEW — this is the story]**

About two years ago I applied for a CSR position at an MSP.

Customer service rep. Entry level. I had no idea going in that support was even done by email — I thought it was phone.

Didn't matter. I got to work.

Before the first interview I used agent mode to crawl the entire company website. Every page. Their affiliates. Their client pages. Partner pages. Case studies. I pulled employee names. Then I went to LinkedIn and pulled their partners' employee names too.

Then I used Gemini to build logical personas — realistic people, at realistic companies, at realistic levels, facing realistic problems — the kinds of calls that would actually come into a support desk from that specific client base.

Then I used Claude to reverse-engineer what I could find of their tech stack and started learning it.

I did all of this for a customer service job.

---

**[THE ROOM]**

The interview process ended up being four rounds over a month and a half — all video calls.

At some point I was on a video call with the president of the company.

And I walked them through it. All of it. What I'd gathered, how I'd gathered it, what tools I used, who I'd built personas for, what the tech stack looked like from the outside.

I even made a comment about the college he went to.

Because I knew. Because I'd looked.

---

**[THE SPIRAL — the honest part]**

Around week four of that month and a half, something happened.

I started spiraling.

I'd been doing all this work, building all these systems, going deeper than anyone had asked me to — and I started to wonder if I'd just convinced myself I was good at something I wasn't actually good at.

It got dark.

That's what happens when you build alone. No peers. No one to course-correct. No institutional backing that says the work is real. Just you and the work and the question of whether any of it holds up.

I didn't have an answer. I just kept going.

---

**[THE CONFIRMATION]**

They didn't hire me for the CSR role.

They hired me for a different position — one I hadn't applied for — because of how I thought about the problem.

And somewhere along the way, my boss put it in writing in my performance review:

I was the company's AI subject matter expert.

I was technically still an L1.

No CS degree. Rebuilt memory. Hired outside the normal process. SME designation in writing.

---

**[WHAT IT MEANS]**

I've been reading rooms my whole life.

At O'Toole's, bartending — knowing which customer was about to be a problem before he moved. In quality control — finding what was broken in a process before the system caught it. In that interview room — walking in knowing more about the company than some of the people who worked there.

Same instinct. Every time. Different substrate.

Piano. Bar. QC floor. Company crawl.

The medium changes. The processor doesn't.

The tools I built after the overdose weren't productivity hacks. They were how I kept the instinct running when the internal storage couldn't be trusted. External scaffolding for something that was always there.

---

**[WHY THIS VIDEO EXISTS]**

I run FlashFusion now. B2B AI enablement. Helping businesses figure out where AI actually changes things versus where it's just noise.

I've kept most of this quiet until now.

Partly because when you build alone you don't always know if what you're doing is real.

Partly because week four of that interview process taught me something about what doubt feels like when you're actually onto something.

I'm building in public now because the work is real. Because there are people doing serious work with no credential to point at and no one telling them it counts.

It counts.

---

**[CLOSE]**

My name is Kyle Rosebrook.

Self-taught. No CS degree. Overdosed and rebuilt. Walked into a CSR interview and gave the president an intelligence briefing on their own company.

Hired for a different job. AI SME in writing.

I've been building in the dark for a while.

This is me turning on the lights.
