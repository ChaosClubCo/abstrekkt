The Construction Manifesto.

Recovery assumes returning to a previous state. There was nothing to return to. So I built.

I.

The diagnostic instinct survived everything else.

At the bar I knew which guy was about to start trouble before he moved. In quality control, I read processes the way I used to read drunks. In code, I diagnose system failures before the error log catches up. Same skill. Different substrate. The architecture broke. The instinct didn't. This is the one thing the cognitive disruption left intact, and the entire reconstruction was built on top of it.

II.

I am not in recovery. I am under construction.

There is a difference. Recovery assumes the old self is still in there, waiting to be excavated. The old self is gone. The hardware was changed by what happened to it. Whatever I am now is not a restoration. It is a different person, built out of non-standard materials, using a process I am still inventing while I live inside it.

III.

My systems are prosthetic memory, not productivity.

About thirty tools I use deeply. The documentation isn't organization. It's the place I keep things when my head won't.

IV.

Reading the room is also a way of not being in it.

The instinct that survived is not free. Diagnosing a moment is not the same as being inside it. The systems saved me and the systems keep me at arm's length from the thing that broke me. Both are true.

V.

I show up where I am.

The credentialed path didn't open, so I built a different one in the margins. I'm not what my title says. I'm not what my title undersells either. I'm a person who keeps showing up at the work I'm paid for and uses the rest of the available hours to make something the door wouldn't open for.

VI.

I don't break rules. I find frameworks that allow me.

The frameworks I trust now are the ones I built. Including this one.

VII.

Abstrekkt is the same project as the book.

The mental health platform I am building is not separate from this story. It is the version of the story I am building for the people still inside their own. No masks. No clinical distance. I'm not selling recovery. I'm offering construction tools to people who, like me, are not going back anywhere.

VIII.

This whole construction project is, finally, for her.

My daughter will not remember the Fridays I was tired. She will remember what her dad built. The book I am writing is a letter. She is the only reader who has to understand it. The rest is overflow.

Start where you are.
