## Workshop — Session-by-session content library

### Session 1: What AI Is and Isn't (60 min)

**Learning objective:** Participants run a real, useful prompt by the end of the session and understand the difference between what AI can do reliably and where it fails.

**Timing:**
- 0:00–0:10 — Introductions and ground rules (no wrong answers, everything is editable)
- 0:10–0:20 — What AI is not (not a search engine, not always right, not reading your mind)
- 0:20–0:30 — What AI actually does (pattern-based language generation; why it's useful despite limitations)
- 0:30–0:50 — Hands-on: everyone runs their first useful prompt (facilitator provides the context, participants supply the task)
- 0:50–0:60 — Reflection: what surprised you? What would you use this for?

**Participant workbook — Session 1 pages:**
1. "Before this session, I thought AI was..." (fill in before)
2. "One thing AI cannot reliably do: ___" (fill in during)
3. My first useful prompt: [paste or write it here]
4. Three things I could use this for at work: ___, ___, ___

**Facilitator notes:**
- The "no wrong answers" framing matters. Skeptics in the room are gold — their resistance surfaces the real objections, which is the content.
- Live demo the failure mode first: ask Claude something it gets wrong or oversimplifies. This is more persuasive than showing only what works.
- Have three "starter prompts" ready for participants who freeze: (a) summarize this job posting in 3 bullet points, (b) write a follow-up email after a job interview, (c) explain [their field's jargon term] to someone who doesn't know the industry.

**Common objections in Session 1:**
- "AI will take my job" → "The people at risk are the ones who don't adapt. The value isn't in being an AI expert — it's in being the domain expert who also knows how to use AI. That combination is rare right now."
- "I'm not technical" → Run a prompt live. Show them it's typing a request, not writing code. "If you can write an email, you can do this."
- "It makes stuff up" → "Yes. That's why you're here — so you know when to trust it and when to check it. That's a skill, not a dealbreaker."

### Session 2: AI in Your Field (60 min)

**Learning objective:** Participants identify 2-3 specific tasks in their actual career track where AI can save time or improve output.

**Timing:**
- 0:00–0:10 — Review from Session 1: what did you try this week? (accountability + momentum)
- 0:10–0:25 — Field-specific examples: 3 career tracks, AI use cases for each (see below)
- 0:25–0:50 — Hands-on: participants apply AI to one task from their own field
- 0:50–0:60 — Share out: what worked, what needed editing, what was faster

**Career track examples (customize for actual enrollment):**

| Career track | AI use case | Prompt to demo |
|---|---|---|
| Business/Admin | Draft professional emails, summarize meeting notes, write job application cover letters | "Write a follow-up email to a hiring manager after a phone screen for an entry-level accounting position" |
| Healthcare/CNA | Summarize patient documentation language, study for certifications, draft care notes for review | "Explain the difference between Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes in plain language for a patient" |
| IT/Technical | Draft ticket responses, explain error messages, write documentation | "Explain what this error message means: [paste error]" |
| Trades/Construction | Write subcontractor scopes of work, draft RFQ emails, document procedures | "Write a scope of work for a residential bathroom tile installation" |
| Culinary/Hospitality | Write menu descriptions, draft training materials, respond to reviews | "Write a response to a 3-star Google review that mentions slow service but good food" |

**Participant workbook — Session 2 pages:**
1. My career track: ___
2. Three tasks I do regularly that involve writing or research: ___, ___, ___
3. The task I tried with AI today: ___
4. What it got right: ___
5. What I had to fix: ___
6. Time saved (estimate): ___

### Session 3: Making It a Habit (60 min)

**Learning objective:** Participants leave with a personal AI routine they will actually use, and the ability to evaluate AI tools critically.

**Timing:**
- 0:00–0:10 — Review: what stuck from Sessions 1 and 2?
- 0:10–0:25 — Building a personal AI practice (the one-habit rule)
- 0:25–0:40 — Evaluating AI tools and claims critically (what vendors say vs. what's real)
- 0:40–0:55 — Hands-on: participants draft their personal AI use plan
- 0:55–0:60 — Commitments and next steps

**The one-habit rule (facilitator talking point):**
"Don't try to use AI for everything. Pick one task you do at least three times a week and run it through AI every time, for 30 days. That's it. After 30 days, you'll know whether it's worth expanding. One habit beats a dozen intentions."

**Evaluating AI claims — the 3 questions:**
1. Can they show you the specific output? (Not a demo video — a real, live result)
2. What does it get wrong? (Any tool that claims 100% accuracy is lying)
3. What human is still in the loop? (The best AI workflows have a human reviewing before anything goes out)

**Participant workbook — Session 3 pages:**
1. The one task I'll run through AI every time for the next 30 days: ___
2. How I'll know it's working: ___
3. Three questions to ask when someone pitches me an AI tool: ___, ___, ___
4. One thing I'll explain to someone else about AI: ___
