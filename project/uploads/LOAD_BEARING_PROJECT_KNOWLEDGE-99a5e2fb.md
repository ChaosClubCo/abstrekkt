## Platform decision criteria (to write before choosing)

These are the questions that must have answers before a platform is selected:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Audience portability | Can I export the full subscriber list (email + name) in a standard format at any time? |
| Weekly friction | How many clicks from "blank document" to "sent to subscribers"? |
| Cost at 1K subscribers | What does this cost per month at 1,000 subscribers? |
| Custom domain | Can the newsletter live at loadbearing.co or a subdomain without restrictions? |
| Archive ownership | If I leave the platform, do I own the archive and can I redirect it? |
| Monetization shape | What's the path to a paid tier if that's ever the decision? |
| Discovery | Does the platform have a native discovery mechanic that helps readers find new newsletters? |

**Minimum bar for any platform:**
- Full subscriber export, always, no exit tax
- Custom domain
- Standard formatting (serif body text, no forced platform aesthetic)

**Beehiiv vs Substack decision surface:**
- Beehiiv: cleaner pricing structure, better export options, less discovery network, newer platform
- Substack: stronger discovery mechanic and reader network, more platform dependency, revenue split on paid posts
- Ghost: maximum control, requires hosting, highest ongoing technical maintenance
- Custom (flashfusion.co): maximum control + integrated with existing site, requires email delivery setup (Resend, Postmark, or SendGrid), highest setup investment

## Content pillars

### Pillar 1: Load-Bearing (core weekly)
Tools, systems, and workflows tested in real conditions. Honest reporting on what held and what broke. Not a product review, not a how-to — a field report.

Example topics:
- "The MCP servers that earned their keep (and the ones I turned off)" — Post 05 when tool retirements list is ready
- "Claude Code at [N] weeks: what's different from the launch hype"
- "The agentic workflow that seemed to work until it didn't"
- "What I use vs. what I tell clients to use — and why those lists are different"
- "Shadow AI is shadow IT: the quantified version"

### Pillar 2: Counter-patterns
The received wisdom about AI adoption that real use contradicts. Written for practitioners, not evangelists.

Example topics:
- "The AI tool budget trap: why more tools produces less output"
- "Why the demo always works: the gap between showcase conditions and production conditions"
- "Model-agnostic means something: why training on one model is a platform risk"
- "The 'just use AI' advice and what it's missing"

### Pillar 3: The workbench (honest operational posts)
What it actually looks like to build and deliver this work — the decisions, the dead ends, the things that took longer than they should have.

Example topics:
- "What a $4,500 AI audit actually consists of"
- "The champion model at six months: what transferred and what didn't"
- "Building on top of someone else's platform dependency: the risk I keep choosing"

### Pillar 4: Reading Room essays (series only, not weekly)
Longer-form. Quarterly series. Each series has 3-5 essays with a unified theme. These are the posts that take more writing time and go deeper than the weekly format allows.

Current series concepts:
- "If You've Earned the Judgment" — 5-post series (Posts 01-04 complete, Post 05 pending)
- "Construction Notes" — personal brand series (brief initiated, not yet complete)
- Substrate-Independent essays — complete
- Champion Method series — complete (Post 05 gated on client permissions)

## Voice reference — passing and failing examples

### Passing
- "The context window handling broke on a 40-file repo. Not gradually — it dropped detail from early files without flagging that it had done so."
- "I turned off three MCP servers I'd spent a week configuring. The overhead wasn't worth what they added."
- "The shadow AI research is more useful than it sounds: 90% of companies have employees using personal AI tools for work. 40% have a company-licensed tool. The gap is the actual story."
- "Specific observation → working-out → landing. No epiphany. No summary. The reader decides what it means."

### Failing
- "This is what I learned from six months of testing AI tools..." [forbidden lesson structure]
- "And that's when I realized the real value of..." [forbidden epiphany]
- "If you're a business owner looking to improve your AI strategy..." [forbidden consulting voice]
- "Here are five things every AI user should know..." [forbidden listicle framing]
- "I'm proud to have helped clients achieve..." [forbidden case study leak]

## Post production checklist (pre-publish)

**Content:**
- [ ] Opening doesn't start with a question or "Have you ever..."
- [ ] No epiphany language anywhere
- [ ] No implicit or explicit call to action for FlashFusion
- [ ] Every specific number or claim is either sourced or tagged [ESTIMATE]
- [ ] Strongest counterargument to the main point appears in the post
- [ ] Closing sentence lands — doesn't summarize, doesn't moralize

**Voice:**
- [ ] Could a stranger mistake this for FlashFusion consulting content? (if yes → rewrite)
- [ ] Could this appear in the Memoir without changing a word? (if yes → rewrite)
- [ ] Does it read like the room — a lamp, a ledger, no audience?

**Format:**
- [ ] Prose only — no bullets, no numbered lists, no headers within the body
- [ ] Under 900 words (weekly), under 2,500 (essay)
- [ ] Subject line is specific and functional, not clever for cleverness's sake
- [ ] Preview text extends, doesn't repeat, the subject line

**Operations:**
- [ ] Scheduled in the platform
- [ ] Added to publication log
- [ ] Social teaser drafted (separate surface, not the same copy)

## Cross-lane rules
- **Load-Bearing → FlashFusion:** A post can produce a case study idea, but the case study lives on FF, not in the newsletter.
- **FlashFusion → Load-Bearing:** Consulting wins cannot appear as newsletter content without being stripped of all client identification and rewritten for the newsletter voice.
- **Memoir → Load-Bearing:** Scene fragments from Tax the Damage are permitted ONLY in the Reading Room essay series, explicitly framed, never in weekly posts.
- **INT/day job → Load-Bearing:** Zero bleed. Nothing from the day job appears in the newsletter in any form.
- **Load-Bearing → Memoir:** The newsletter is the public version of operational thinking. The memoir is the private excavation. Different registers, different material.

## Distribution and growth

**Seed list:** Build from existing FF audience first. Direct outreach to people who'd actually read it — not a mass blast.

**Cross-post surface:** LinkedIn (adapted excerpts, not reposts), Twitter/X (observation-sized fragments), Threads (same).

**Guest essays:** After cadence is proven for 3 months. Outreach should be specific — "I want you to write about X because you have direct experience with it" — not an open call.

**Sponsorship:** Not before 1,000 subscribers. Decision requires written criteria for sponsor fit (must be tools or services the newsletter would write about anyway, not arbitrary).
