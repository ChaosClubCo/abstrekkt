§ About

The path wasn't linear.

I bartended for years. Read rooms, managed chaos, ran on instinct. That instinct survived everything that came after — quality control work, the rupture, the rebuild. No CS degree. I learned to code because the brain needed to work differently.

Not because I'm organized. Because I can't afford not to be.

On the tool stack — it's prosthetic, not a productivity flex

I built external systems to replace what internal ones couldn't do anymore. Dozens of tools. Obsessive documentation. Systematic approaches to everything — cognitive scaffolding for a brain that can't be trusted to remember.

Based in Grayslake, Illinois. Father. Service desk tech by day, building three things in the margins. This site exists to make the work legible.

Literary Memoir · Active Excavation
Construction, Not Recovery

A traumatic brain injury and addiction rewired the cognitive architecture. Memory became unreliable. Old strategies stopped working. The standard recovery arc — return to baseline, reclaim what was lost — doesn't apply when the hardware itself has changed. There's nothing to go back to.

So what happens when someone with a broken mind builds external infrastructure to replace internal function? Not the damage. The construction.

If someone reads this and thinks "what a brave recovery story," the book failed. If they think "he rebuilt the whole thing from scratch and it's still not done and he knows that" — the book worked.
