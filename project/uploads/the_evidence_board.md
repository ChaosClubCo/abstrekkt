# The Evidence Board

**Primary Source Document — Kyle Rosebrook's Literary Memoir**

*How a SPECT scan taken at Amen Clinics Chicago on July 7–11, 2022 connects to every system, every tool, every construction, and every person in Kyle Rosebrook's life.*

---

## I. The Grand Explanation

On July 7, 2022, a 36-year-old bartender named Kyle Rosebrook sat in a dimly lit room at Amen Clinics Chicago while a radioactive tracer mapped the blood flow in his brain. Four days later, he came back for a second scan at rest. He was there because his mother, Phyllis, had found the clinic. His chief complaint was two words: **"Addiction, depression."**

What the scan revealed was a map of damage — decreased perfusion across the temporal lobes, prefrontal cortex, parietal cortex, occipital lobes, and cerebellum. Scalloping on the surface consistent with toxic exposure and oxygen deprivation. A thalamus running severely hot. The clinical summary: **evidence of past brain injury and encephalopathic process.**

The cause was not a car accident. Not a fall. Not a blow to the head. Kyle's brain was injured by his own medications — a Xanax and Ambien overdose in the summer of 2020 that deprived his brain of oxygen. The addiction didn't just coexist with the brain injury. **The addiction caused the brain injury. And then the brain injury made escape harder**, because the prefrontal cortex that handles impulse control was now running at reduced capacity. A recursive trap.

> **The memoir's thesis in one sentence:** Recovery assumes returning to a previous state. Kyle can't return — the hardware is changed. So he built a new architecture from non-standard materials, using a brain that processes at the 99th percentile but recalls at the 12th.

This document maps how every construction in Kyle's life — the 240+ tools, the engineering career, Chaos Club Co., the memoir itself — traces back to what those four images showed. Not as metaphor. As engineering response to a diagnosed system failure.

> *"Among the living dead, I was king."*
> — Kyle, on being the model resident at Roger's Behavioral Health, summer 2020

## II. The Clinical Evidence

**SPECT Scan Findings — Amen Clinics Chicago, July 2022**

Two SPECT scans using 99m Technetium HMPAO tracer. The **concentration study** (July 7) captured brain activity during a task. The **baseline study** (July 11) captured the brain at rest. Both revealed widespread decreased perfusion — areas where blood isn't flowing the way it should.

The **surface renders** show the brain's exterior, where dents and holes represent areas of low blood flow. The **active renders** (blue wireframe with red clusters) show which regions activated during scanning. The gaps between clusters are the spaces Kyle's external systems were built to bridge.

### Affected Brain Regions

**Temporal Lobes (Left + Right)**
Status: DECREASED — both studies, worse at rest
Governs: Memory, mood stability, emotional reactions, language processing, reading social cues
Memoir implication: Memory problems. Mood instability. The age oscillation in the memoir ("I was 19 — or 21"). The bartender's diagnostic instinct — reading rooms — operating from a damaged region.

**Prefrontal Cortex (Inferior Orbital + Dorsal)**
Status: DECREASED — worse at rest
Governs: Impulse control, judgment, planning, follow-through, attention, empathy
Memoir implication: The recursive trap: addiction damages the region that controls impulse, making addiction harder to escape. Also: procrastination, task abandonment, FlashFusion at 60-70%.

**Parietal Cortex (Left + Right)**
Status: DECREASED — worse at rest
Governs: Spatial processing, direction sense, complex task performance, orientation
Memoir implication: Associated with toxic exposure and anoxia. The scalloping signature. Difficulty with complex multi-step tasks without external scaffolding.

**Cerebellum (Internal)**
Status: DECREASED at rest
Governs: Motor control, executive function, speed of cognitive integration, conditioned learning
Memoir implication: Usually the most active part of the brain. When low: impulsivity, slowed thinking. The cerebellum connects to PFC — it's the clock speed of the processor.

**Thalamus**
Status: SEVERELY INCREASED — both studies
Governs: Sensory relay, mood control, motivation, bonding, sleep, appetite
Memoir implication: The emotional switchboard running hot. Depression, isolation, sleep disruption. The thalamus processes every sensory input before routing it — when it's overactive, everything feels louder.

**Basal Ganglia + Insular Cortex**
Status: MILDLY INCREASED with concentration
Governs: Anxiety regulation, sense of calm, motor control, pleasure/addiction circuits
Memoir implication: Can indicate anxiety or high motivation. The nucleus accumbens mediates pleasure — the addiction circuit. Also: the insular cortex processes internal body awareness.

**Occipital Lobe**
Status: DECREASED
Governs: Visual processing
Memoir implication: Part of the overall decreased perfusion pattern. Contributes to the brain injury finding.

**Surface Scalloping**
Status: MILD — worse at rest
Governs: Overall brain surface integrity
Memoir implication: The bumpy, irregular appearance = toxic exposure signature. Oxygen deprivation. Substance history. This is the visual fingerprint of what happened.

### The Document's Own Contradiction

The clinical record itself demonstrates the same memory confusion the book is about. Even the doctors couldn't keep the timeline straight — or Kyle told it differently each time, because that's what damaged memory does.

### What This Actually Means

Kyle thought the 99th/12th split meant his brain was broken across the board. It means the opposite. **The processing engine is elite. The storage system is devastated.** His brain catches everything — patterns, systems, room dynamics, code architecture — and then can't hold it. The information arrives at full speed and evaporates.

This is why the 240+ tools work. He's not compensating for being slow. **He's building an external hard drive for a processor that runs too fast for its own memory.** The obsessive documentation, the systematic approaches, the Claude projects, the Notion databases, the n8n workflows — all of it is prosthetic recall for a brain that can process at the 99th percentile but store at the 12th.

**The Conners CPT3 finding:** The attention test found NO pervasive ADHD pattern. Despite being diagnosed with ADHD in college and taking Adderall/Focalin for years, the computerized test couldn't confirm it. What it found instead: "Some Indication" of sustained attention difficulty. The attention problem isn't classical ADHD — it's the downstream effect of the brain injury itself.

**Readiness to Change: 10 out of 10.** The maximum score. Hope: 27 out of 64 — "slight." He was maximally ready to change and barely believed it was possible. That's the gap the construction project had to bridge.

> *"I don't even know what that means, I assumed that it meant it was very slow, and that my memory recall was very bad, and that my brain was in very bad shape, causes me to get angry, sobbing sad, so much that I doubt I will ever have another partner again in my life...and that hurts ALMOST as much as knowing I'm missing out on something like 75+% of my daughter's life."*
> — Kyle, April 2026, reading his own cognitive scores for the first time in four years

## V. The Connections

*How every construction traces back to the scan*

### Connection 1: Scan → 240+ Tools as Prosthetic Memory

`Processing Speed: 99th` → `Recall Memory: 12th` → `Information arrives fast, evaporates` → `Build external storage` → `240+ tools catalogued`

The tools aren't a productivity flex. They're a medical necessity. Notion, Claude, n8n, GitHub, Vercel, 50+ skills built — each one is a memory prosthetic for a brain that catches fire and then forgets it was burning. The obsessive documentation isn't organization. It's survival.

### Connection 2: Scan → The Diagnostic Instinct Surviving Injury

`Focus: 59th percentile` → `Pattern recognition intact` → `Reading rooms at O'Tool's` → `Reading processes in QC` → `Reading systems as engineer`

The diagnostic instinct is the memoir's spine — and the cognitive data explains why it survived. Focus and controlled attention are medium-range. The ability to detect patterns in real-time still works. What broke is the ability to store and retrieve what was detected. The instinct reads the room; the tools remember what it found.

### Connection 3: Scan → The Recursive Trap of Addiction

`Xanax addiction` → `Hypoxia (summer 2020)` → `PFC damaged` → `Impulse control reduced` → `Heroin (2021)` → `More brain damage`

The addiction caused the brain injury. The brain injury reduced the prefrontal cortex function that controls impulse. The reduced impulse control made escalation to heroin more likely. Four overdoses in 2021 — each one adding to the damage. The trap isn't metaphorical. It's visible on the scan. The PFC that should have said "stop" was already running at reduced capacity.

### Connection 4: Scan → Bartender to Staff Engineer

`Processing Speed: 99th` + `External memory systems` = `Self-taught engineering` → `Enterprise-scale competence`

The processing speed is the engine. The tools are the storage. Together, they create a synthetic cognitive architecture that can operate at Staff Engineer level — AppSec, platform engineering, teaching 50-200 engineer organizations — using hardware that a clinical eval said needed memory-enhancing medication. The career isn't despite the injury. It's a specific engineering response to it.

### Connection 5: Scan → Chaos Club Co.

`ACE Score: 0/10` + `Resilience: 2nd percentile` + `4 overdoses survived` → `"No masks required"`

Zero adverse childhood experiences. No abuse, no childhood trauma. The damage was self-inflicted in adulthood. Chaos Club's rejection of performative recovery — "no one has to pretend" — comes from someone who didn't fit the typical narrative. No childhood wound to point to. Just choices that cascaded into neurological damage. The crisis protocols that "connect rather than save" — informed by the trip to Sedona, reinforced by the four times someone had to save Kyle's life and he kept going back.

### Connection 6: Scan → The Memoir Itself

`Recall Memory: 12th` → `Can't locate own beginning` → `"I was 19 — or 21"` → `AI writes wrong book` → `The inability IS the opening`

The subject of the book is also the obstacle to writing it. Temporal lobe damage affects memory formation and retrieval. The eval's own internal date contradiction — "summer 2020" on one page, "October 2021" on another — proves the point. Even the clinical record can't keep Kyle's timeline straight, because Kyle told it differently each time, because that's what damaged memory does. The memoir has to sit in that uncertainty. It's not a flaw in the storytelling. It's the story.

### Connection 7: Scan → FlashFusion at 60-70%

`PFC decreased` → `Follow-through impaired` + `Flexibility: 17th` → `Difficulty shifting when stuck` → `Projects archive before completion`

The PFC governs planning and follow-through. The flexibility score measures the ability to shift approaches when one isn't working. Both are impaired. The character document says "the constructed self builds better than it maintains." That's not a personality trait. It's a neurological finding. The processing speed launches projects at elite velocity. The PFC and flexibility scores explain why they stall.

## VI. The Constructions

*What the damaged brain built*

**July 2022:** Occupation listed as "Bartender." Living with parents. Single. One child, age 3. Hope score: 27 out of 64 — "slight." Readiness to change: 10 out of 10.

**April 2026:** Staff Engineer. Self-taught. No CS degree. 240+ tools catalogued. 50 Claude skills built. Chaos Club Co. founded. Literary memoir in progress. 630+ GitHub repos. FlashFusion enterprise platform with 67-entity schema. Base44 production apps. n8n automation workflows. Figma design systems. Enterprise-scale security architecture.

> The distance between those two paragraphs is the book. Not a recovery story — he didn't return to a prior state. A construction story — he built something that never existed before, using hardware that a clinical evaluation said was damaged across nearly every region.

The construction wasn't rational. He didn't see the scan results and make a plan. He got the results weeks later, had already forgotten what they said, and it wasn't a subject of interest to him. The building started instinctively — the same way a damaged body compensates by shifting weight to the uninjured leg. The 99th-percentile processor began generating external systems to compensate for the 12th-percentile memory, without anyone explaining that's what was happening.

The tools arrived with Lovable.dev and Claude Code. But the pattern — systematize everything, document obsessively, build external rails — was already running. The technology just gave it scale.

> *"I have never felt like a man."*
> — Kyle, April 2026

That sentence sits next to 630+ repositories, an enterprise engineering role, a mental health platform, and a literary memoir. The construction is visible to everyone except the builder.

## VII. Kinsley

*The reader. The reason. Age 3 at scan. Age 8 now.*

The eval lists one child. Significant life events: **"Daughter born."**

Marijuana stopped in 2018 — "when my daughter was born." That's the first construction. Before the tools, before the engineering, before Chaos Club — there was the decision that Kinsley's existence required him to stop something he'd been doing all day, every day, for eleven years.

She knows he was sick. He told her it was his fault. He's cried to her, sobbing, apologizing for breaking up the family. She hugged him and put her head on his shoulder.

She was three when he walked into Amen Clinics. She won't remember that summer. But the book will.

> The memoir is a letter to Kinsley. Not "here's what happened to daddy" — she'll learn that through life, in age-appropriate pieces, the way he's already started. The book is "here's what daddy built after it happened, and here's the brain that built it, and here's what I want you to know about construction when the world tells you recovery is the only story."

**The custody math:** Friday pickup to Sunday return. That's roughly 25% of her childhood. He said it: "knowing I'm missing out on something like 75+% of my daughter's life." The book is one way to be present in the other 75%. A document that exists when he can't.

Processing Speed 99th. Recall Memory 12th. He processes their Fridays at elite speed. He can't always hold them. So he builds — StoryWeaver, KidCreator, the memoir itself — systems that capture what his brain won't retain. External memory for the most important memories he has.
