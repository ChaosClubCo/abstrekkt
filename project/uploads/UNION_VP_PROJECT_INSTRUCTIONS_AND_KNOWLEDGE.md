## The argument structure (what moves boards)

**The one framing that works:**
"You're not approving an AI initiative. You're approving a member professional development program. The board has voted yes on workforce training before. This is that — with current-moment urgency you can't ignore."

**The five arguments (in priority order):**
1. Members who don't learn AI are the ones at risk — not the ones who get trained. Avoidance is not protection.
2. First-mover unions define how AI enters the workplace instead of reacting to what employers impose.
3. One AI-fluent champion per department is a permanent member asset — not a replacement risk, a protection.
4. The window is 18–24 months. After that, the productivity gap is hard to close through negotiation.
5. This is member professional development — the same category as safety training and skills upgrading. The board has approved this before.

**The closing move:**
"What would you need to see in a board presentation to get this approved in the next meeting cycle?" — then build exactly that together.

## The champion model in a union context
Standard FlashFusion champion model: train one domain expert, make them the AI champion for their area, exit the dependency.

In a union context:
- The champion is a union member, not a manager
- The champion's value to colleagues comes from speaking the same professional language and understanding the same constraints
- The knowledge stays inside the union — not with an external vendor
- If the champion moves, the documented workflows remain; the union retains the knowledge infrastructure

**One AI-fluent champion per department:**
- Labor relations: draft grievance language, research contract precedents, summarize arbitration rulings
- Communications: member newsletters, meeting summaries, press statements
- Education/training: professional development content, certification prep materials
- Field operations: documentation, incident reports, scheduling

## Objection handling — board-specific

**"We need to evaluate this more before committing."**
→ "What specific information would change the evaluation? Let's build the board presentation around exactly that."

**"Our members aren't ready for this."**
→ "The program is designed for people who've never used AI — that's the point. The members who are 'ready' don't need training. The ones who aren't are exactly who this serves."

**"How do we know the members will actually use this?"**
→ "That's what the champion model solves. You don't train all 5,000 members. You train one person per department who speaks their language and sits in their meetings. Adoption follows credibility, not mandates."

**"What if AI hurts our members?"**
→ "That's the risk of not training them. Employers who deploy AI unilaterally are ahead of where they are now. A union with AI-fluent members has a countervailing voice in that conversation."
