CONSTRUCTION, NOT RECOVERY

The Card Table & The Wall

The card table was a temporary measure that became permanent. Bought for a dinner party that never happened, it ended up in the corner of the small apartment, its faux-wood surface quickly obscured by stacks of paper, half-filled notebooks, and pens that had long since run dry. It was meant to be a place to sort mail; it became the command center for a project that had no name.

Opposite the table was the wall. Not just any wall, but the specific stretch of drywall between the closet door and the window. It began innocently enough—a single Post-it note reminding me to buy milk. Then, a clipping from a magazine, taped up with masking tape. Soon, the wall was a mosaic of ideas, fragmented thoughts, and diagrams drawn on whatever scrap material was at hand.

→ ——◆—— ←

The space between the table and the wall became an active zone of construction. This was not about recovery; it was not about returning to a previous state of clarity. It was about building something entirely new from the wreckage of discarded drafts. The physical act of moving a note from the table to the wall felt like laying a brick.

"The wall does not care about narrative arc. It only cares about juxtaposition. What happens when the note about the river is placed next to the sketch of the collapsing bridge? The friction creates meaning." — Note 42, dated November.

Sometimes I would sit at the card table for hours, just staring at the wall, waiting for the connections to reveal themselves. The ambient light from the streetlamp outside would cast long, wavering shadows across the paper terrain, making the notes seem alive, breathing, shifting in their relationships to one another.
