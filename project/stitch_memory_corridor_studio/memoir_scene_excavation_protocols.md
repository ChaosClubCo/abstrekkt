# Memoir Scene Excavation Protocols

Working reference for Tier 1–3 character excavation. Copy prompts directly. Do not rewrite them into something kinder — the constraints are the point.

---

## 1. Scene Extraction Protocol

Apply to every Tier 1–3 character. Paste this verbatim into session:

> **For [CHARACTER NAME]:**
>
> 1. Give me ONE real moment with:
>    - physical environment
>    - exact positioning (where everyone was)
>    - at least one line of dialogue
>
> 2. Add:
>    - what I felt in my body
>    - what I misunderstood in the moment
>    - what I understand now
>
> 3. Constraint:
>    - No summarizing
>    - No explaining
>    - Only scene

If a response drifts into summary or reflection, stop and re-run. The constraint is load-bearing.

---

## 2. Missing Witness Recovery Protocol

List every person who:
- saw me high
- saw me withdraw
- saw me fail
- saw me try again

Then: **pick the one you least want to write about. Write that scene first.**

Resistance is the compass. The scene you're avoiding is the scene that has something.

---

## 3. Memory Distortion Layer

Unique advantage: documented memory damage + narrative awareness. Most memoirists have to fake this. You don't.

Exploit it:

> Write the same scene twice.
>
> **Version A:** how I remember it.
> **Version B:** what likely actually happened.
>
> Do not reconcile them.

The gap between A and B is not a flaw in the book. The gap *is* the book. Reconciling them kills the thesis.

---

## 4. Narrative Power Principles

**Primary weapon: contrast.**
- Bear vs Michael
- Kinsley vs OD
- Memory vs reality

**Secondary weapon: specificity.**
- "empanadas in a blue kitchen" > entire paragraphs of explanation
- One concrete detail outperforms a page of interpretation

**Biggest risk: over-explaining.** Trust the scene. If you've earned the image, the reader will do the work. If you haven't, no amount of explanation will save it.

---

## 5. Emotional / Ethical Guardrails

**Kinsley content.** Already flagged correctly. Keep:
- minimal
- intentional
- never exploitative

When in doubt, cut. She'll read this someday. Write like she's already reading it.

**Real people (Michael, Mackenzie, others).** Before publishing, decide:
- pseudonyms
- composite blending
- legal review

Draft with real names for accuracy. Pseudonymize at revision. Don't let anticipated consequences distort first-draft truth.

---

## 6. Execution Sequence

Do these in order. Don't optimize the order.

1. **Pick 3 characters only:** Phyllis, Mackenzie, Pete.
2. **Run full Scene Extraction Protocol on each.** Three scenes minimum per character before moving on.
3. **Identify two more witnesses:**
   - one missing OD witness
   - one O'Toole's regular
4. **Write those scenes next.**
5. **THEN build** chronological threading and thematic linking.

Structure comes *after* raw material. Do not build the skeleton before you have the bones.

---

## 7. Three Critical Thinking Moves

**1. Invert the narrative lens.**
Write a chapter where you are not the main character — someone else is observing you. Forces you out of interior monologue. Makes the reader see what witnesses saw.

**2. Exploit contradiction deliberately.**
If two memories conflict, keep both. Don't pick. Don't reconcile. The contradiction is not a flaw — it's the theme rendered in form.

**3. Introduce a neutral observer.**
A coworker, counselor, stranger — someone with no emotional stake. Their presence grounds the story. Without a neutral witness, everything reads as either confession or defense.
