## Positioning

**Tax the Damage** is a literary memoir about addiction, brain injury, fatherhood, unreliable memory, and the external systems Kyle Rosebrook built when returning to an earlier version of himself was no longer possible.

The memoir uses a systems-thinking framework, but it is not self-help, a productivity manifesto, an addiction confessional, or a conventional recovery memoir.

### Central thesis

Recovery assumes there is a previous state available to recover. This book asks what happens when there is not—when the original architecture cannot be restored and a functional self has to be constructed from different materials.

### Primary reader

The memoir is written as a letter to Kinsley. She is the reader and grounding constraint, not a character whose interior life is available for public development.

### Voice

Direct without being cold. Technically precise without becoming clinical. Metaphor should clarify lived experience rather than decorate it. Uncertainty remains visible. Meaning should not arrive faster than the record supports it.

Avoid motivational language, easy redemption, LinkedIn-style uplift, and any sentence that turns damage into a lesson merely because a lesson reads cleanly.

External systems—documentation, calendars, databases, code, automations, prompts, checkpoints, and state-preservation workflows—became practical infrastructure.

Approximately thirty tools are used hands-on. More than 240 refers to catalogued configurations, not tools mastered.

> Not because I am organized.  
> Because I cannot afford not to be.

The 2022 clinical record documented evidence of past brain injury and an encephalopathic process. It dates the hypoxia event as both summer 2020 and October 2021.

Public copy must preserve both dates unless a stronger primary source resolves the conflict. No editor, developer, or AI system should silently choose one.

This book is a letter to Kinsley. It is not an explanation she owes, a request for forgiveness, or a polished account of how everything worked out.

It is a record of what happened, what cannot be remembered, what the documents support, and what was built afterward.

## Source Classifications

- **Established** — Supported by a primary record or multiple consistent sources.
- **Recollected** — Remembered directly but not independently corroborated.
- **Reported** — Supplied by another person or record.
- **Interpreted** — A conclusion drawn from available material.
- **Disputed** — Contradicted by another source.
- **Unknown** — Not presently established.
- **Protected** — Documented but not approved for public disclosure.
