Tax the Damage.

The processor survived. The storage didn’t. A literary memoir about addiction, brain injury, fatherhood, unreliable memory, and the external systems I built when memory stopped doing its job.

The tool cleaned up the contradiction and found a recovery arc. Wrong book.

Tax the Damage starts with memory that cannot keep its own timeline straight and records that do not always agree with themselves. The rule is simple: preserve the source before deciding what it means.

The construction

Not recovery. Construction.

My brain did not fail evenly. The processing engine still catches patterns. Recall does not reliably retain them.

I built external memory: documentation, calendars, databases, code, automations, prompts, checkpoints, and systems designed to preserve state when I could not trust myself to hold it.

Not because I am organized.
Because I cannot afford not to be.

The earliest primary-source-backed scaffolding currently identified is dated July 18, 2022. The record names three tools:

TOOL-001

Four-quadrant matrix

Paper-based prioritization. Move relative importance outside the head.

TOOL-002

FocusMate

External accountability and an outside start signal for task initiation.

TOOL-003

GTD

External capture and commitment tracking. A system that remembers so the brain does not have to.

What the record does not prove: whether I used each prescription, for how long, what worked, or which digital tool came first. Those gaps stay gaps until I can answer them.

The letter

For my daughter.

This book is a letter to my daughter.

Not an explanation she owes me. Not a request for forgiveness. Not a polished account of how everything worked out.

It is a record of what happened, what I cannot remember, what the documents can support, and what I built afterward.

She is the reader and the grounding constraint. Her private life is not source material for public display.

Current status

In development.

The memoir entered active development in March 2026. There is no announced publication date.

Current work is source reconciliation, author-led scene excavation, manuscript development, and legal/privacy review of high-risk material. The public record does not claim completion where the evidence does not support it.

Damage has a cost after everyone changes the name.

It gets called experience. Growth. A lesson. The past. Something survived. The language becomes easier. The invoice remains.
