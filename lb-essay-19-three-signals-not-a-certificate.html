Three Signals, Not a Certificate

A certificate tells you someone sat through the material. It doesn't tell you what happens the first time the material doesn't match what's in front of them.

A certificate tells you someone sat through the material. It doesn't tell you what happens the first time the material doesn't match the situation in front of them — and that gap is exactly where the actual judgment either shows up or doesn't.

Feeling the Thin Spot

The first signal is being able to feel where a model's confidence is thin before it says so — because it never says so. A model doesn't lower its voice when it moves from something it actually knows into something it's filling in. The sentence keeps the same shape either way. Catching that shift isn't a skill you download. It's built from having been wrong enough times, on something that mattered, that you've learned what the seam feels like from the inside.

Present but Useless

The second is spotting the difference between a clause that's technically there and one that's actually doing anything. A contract can carry an indemnification section and still leave you exposed, because the section exists to satisfy a checklist, not to hold up under the specific situation it needs to cover. AI is good at producing the checklist item. It isn't the thing that tells you the checklist item is hollow.

None of the three signals shows up on a résumé. All three show up the first time the situation doesn't match the training data.

Arguing With the Argument

The third is the one that surprises people: catching yourself disagreeing with the model's own pushback. Ask it to pressure-test a plan and it will find something. Whether that something is a real flaw or a plausible-sounding non-issue is a judgment call layered on top of the first judgment call — and you can only make that second call if you already had the first one before you asked.

A certificate is a claim about the past. These aren't that. They're closer to a reflex — and reflexes only get built one way, which is slower than anyone wants to hear right now.
