CHARACTER: KYLE ROSEBROOK

=== TOP FIELDS ===

Name: Kyle Rosebrook

Roles: Narrator, protagonist, author. First-person throughout.

Pronouns: He/him

Groups: Self-taught engineers, TBI survivors, people in recovery, single fathers, Midwest tech outsiders

Other Names: None used in the book.

=== DETAILED FIELDS ===

Personality:
Pattern-recognizer first — reads rooms, systems, and people with the same diagnostic instinct. Direct without being cold. Uses engineering metaphors to explain emotional states because that's actually how his brain processes them. Self-aware about the irony of systematizing everything, including his own self-examination. Dry humor deployed to maintain proximity to hard subjects without defusing them. Not performatively tough or performatively vulnerable. Allergic to toxic positivity — acknowledges the mess before offering anything constructive. Obsessive documenter, not because he's organized but because his memory can't be trusted. Profanity is punctuation, not decoration.

Motivations:
Primary: Leave Kinsley a record of who he became and why — the letter-to-daughter frame is accountability, not sentimentality. Secondary: Prove that a functional self can be constructed from non-standard materials after cognitive disruption. Tertiary: Build tools for others navigating similar chaos (Chaos Club Co. as applied philosophy). Underneath all of it: quiet terror that the systems will fail and there's nothing underneath them.

Internal Conflict:
The systems-builder identity both enables and protects him. The tools and documentation are genuine survival infrastructure — but they also create distance from feeling. Every system he builds is simultaneously an act of reconstruction and an act of avoidance. He can describe the Sunday goodbye with technical precision ("every routine decompiles in 15 minutes") but that precision is also a shield. The book has to hold both truths: the systems saved him AND the systems keep him at arm's length from the thing that broke him. Additional tension: memory is genuinely unreliable. He can't pin down whether dependency started at 19 or 21. Some periods are inaccessible. The narrator doesn't fully trust his own account.

Strengths:
The diagnostic instinct — reads the real problem before anyone names it. At the bar it was knowing which customer was about to start trouble. In engineering it's spotting system failures before the error log catches up. Same skill, different substrate. Survived every version of him. Self-teaching capacity — bartender to engineer with no CS degree, no formal training, no mentorship. Built the path by building on it. Construction under pressure — doesn't freeze when the architecture fails. Builds replacement systems. Obsessive documentation as a form of honesty — the most accurate record of his own mind exists in external systems, not in his head. Humor that keeps hard subjects approachable without trivializing them.

Weaknesses:
Memory. The subject of the book is also the obstacle to writing it. Some periods are genuinely gone. He's told the same story with different facts and doesn't know which version is the lie. Emotional distance through systems — the precision that makes his voice distinctive also keeps him from sitting in feeling without framing it. Overbuilding as avoidance — 240 catalogued tools when 30 are deeply used suggests the building itself became a coping mechanism, not just the output. Difficulty with sustained projects — FlashFusion archived at 60-70%, multiple experiments that never graduated past POC. The constructed self builds better than it maintains. Custody grief that operates as background noise rather than confronted loss.

Character Arc:
Not recovery — construction. The arc isn't "broken man heals." It's "broken man discovers the old architecture can't be restored, builds a new one from non-standard materials, and has to figure out whether what he built is a self or a very sophisticated workaround." The book opens with him unable to locate his own beginning (age oscillation, failed AI draft). It moves through the diagnostic instinct's survival across every phase — bar, QC, code, engineering. It arrives not at resolution but at the honest question: what's permanently lost, and what exists now that couldn't have existed before? The arc is the shift from "something was taken from me" to "I built something that wasn't there before" — but it holds the cost of that shift rather than celebrating it.

Physical Description:
Approaching 40. Based in Mundelein, Illinois — Chicago suburbs, not coastal tech culture. Service industry body that transitioned to desk work. Specific physical details TBD by Kyle — the memoir isn't heavily physical-description-oriented. The most important "physical" detail may be the neuroimaging scan (brain scan image identified as potential cover concept).

Dialogue Style:
First-person narrator, so most "dialogue" is internal monologue rendered as direct prose. When he quotes himself or others, it's sparse and precise — one line that carries weight, not extended exchanges. Declarative sentences. Technical vocabulary applied to emotional subjects without irony. Strategic profanity for emphasis. Never explains a metaphor after deploying it. Admits uncertainty in the middle of assertions ("I was 19 — or 21 — I've told this story both ways"). Humor lands dry, never performs the punchline.
