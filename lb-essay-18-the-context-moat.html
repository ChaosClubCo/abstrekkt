The Context Moat

I built the documentation habit for a reason that had nothing to do with AI. Turns out it was the same thing all along.

I built the documentation habit for a reason that had nothing to do with AI. My memory isn't reliable enough to trust with anything that matters, so I write everything down — every decision, every process, every why-we-did-it-that-way — as a substitute for a working recall system. I thought I was patching a deficit. Turns out I was building the thing that matters most right now.

The Moat Nobody's Selling You

General models know the internet. They don't know your organization — your clients, the actual history behind a decision, the reason a policy that looks arbitrary exists at all. That unwritten layer, the stuff that only lives in someone's head, is the one thing AI can't replicate without that someone handing it over.

Whoever curates that context holds the advantage. Not because they're smarter than anyone else in the room. Because better context produces better output — plain input-output logic, no more complicated than that.

The thing I've learned

The people who will struggle most in this shift aren't the ones who never learned to use AI. They're the ones who never wrote down why they made the decisions they made — who kept all the institutional logic in their own head. When the execution gets handed to a machine, they have nothing to hand it, because the thing the machine needed was never on paper to begin with.

The moat was never the model. The moat is whatever you bothered to write down before you needed it.

What "Reclassified" Looks Like From Here

The job title stays the same. The thing that earns it doesn't — it moves from producing the output to auditing it, directing it, and catching where it's wrong in ways the model has no way to detect on its own. For someone who already has a decade of documented reasoning behind them, that's an expansion. For someone who's never written any of it down, the shift arrives with nothing to stand on.

I didn't build a documentation habit to get ahead. I built it because I couldn't afford to trust myself. It turned out to be the same thing.
