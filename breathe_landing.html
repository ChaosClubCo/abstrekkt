01 — The honest part
No one tells you that relief is the most addictive feeling of all.

— From the Introduction

Your brain isn't broken. It's reacting exactly the way a brain reacts when life has been genuinely hard. That doesn't make it comfortable. It doesn't make the 3am spirals easier to sit in. But it means the path forward isn't about fixing yourself.

It's about learning to navigate what's actually there.

This book doesn't promise recovery. It doesn't promise transformation. It promises a toolkit — built by someone who needed one badly enough to build it from scratch, piece by piece, on a card table in a one-bedroom apartment.

What this book is — and isn't

This is not

  • A clinical treatment plan or substitute for therapy
  • Toxic positivity with a rougher font
  • A ten-step program designed for people whose lives are mostly fine
  • Advice from someone who watched this from a comfortable distance
  • A place where you have to pretend you're okay

This is

  • A starter kit — practical, honest, no prerequisites
  • Tools you can use in the middle of the mess, not after it clears
  • Evidence that chaos can be navigated without being eliminated
  • Permission to be a work in progress
  • The Chaos Club: where no one has to pretend

Kyle Rosebrook
Builder · Founder
Chaos Club Co.
Mundelein, IL

This book didn't start as a book. It started as a survival mechanism — a collection of things that actually worked when nothing else did, assembled on a card table in a one-bedroom apartment over several years of putting myself back together.

I went from a homeless shelter to staff-level engineering without a CS degree. I spent years in active addiction and came out the other side not recovered, exactly — but reconstructed. Built differently than I was before, from different materials, with a better understanding of what I'm actually working with.

Chaos Club Co. is the platform that grew out of that construction project. This book is its foundation — the part that doesn't require an app, a subscription, or a good wifi connection. Just you, and whatever 20 minutes you can carve out of the wreckage of your day.

Built on the same card table where I first learned what a "parallax background" was — by tearing apart a life coach's website one screenshot at a time, copying every word into a document, looking up what each piece was called so I could ask for what I wanted to build. That's what this whole thing has been. Taking things apart. Understanding them. Building something better.
