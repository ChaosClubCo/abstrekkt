Dozens, Not Hundreds

The number in the folder is 244. It's a real number. It would also be a lie to tell you I use 244 of anything.

The number in the folder is 244. It's a real number — I can point to every one of them, name them, show you the files. It would also be a lie to tell you I use 244 of anything.

They're plugins. Configurations. Catalogued attempts — each one a moment I thought a particular capability was worth wiring up and saving. Two hundred and forty-four of those moments, filed. What that number measures is not skill. It measures accumulation. The stack contains 244 things. That is a different fact from the one people assume it is.

What the Stack Contains vs. What It Runs

The count that would actually describe my practice is smaller and less impressive. Somewhere between thirty and forty tools run with any regularity — the ones I reach for without thinking, the ones whose failure modes I know because I've hit them, the ones I could teach because I've used them past the point where the tutorial ends. Dozens. Not hundreds.

The gap between those two numbers is the whole story of AI adoption, and almost nobody tells it honestly, because the big number is the one that sells. "244 tools" is a flex. "Thirty tools I actually understand" is a confession. The flex is easier to say and worse to rely on.

A catalogued tool and a practiced tool are not the same object. One is a file. The other is a habit with scar tissue. You can accumulate the first kind endlessly — installing is free, cataloguing is free, the folder grows without cost. The second kind you earn one failure at a time, and there's a hard ceiling on how many you can hold, because attention doesn't accumulate the way files do.

Why the Honest Number Is the Useful One

When someone asks how many tools I use, the true answer is the smaller one, and giving it costs me the impressive version. I give it anyway, for a reason that isn't only integrity.

The inflated number is a liability the moment it's tested. Say 244 and you've made a claim someone can check, and the check will find thirty. Say thirty and the claim survives contact — every one of those thirty holds up, because every one is real. The honest inventory is the one you can stand behind in the room where standing behind it matters.

The stack is always bigger than the practice. Mine is. Everyone's is. The interesting question was never how many tools are in the folder. It's how many you can actually run when something's on the line — and that number is always dozens, never hundreds, no matter what the folder says.

The folder holds two hundred and forty-four. The practice holds dozens. Only one of those numbers survives being checked.
