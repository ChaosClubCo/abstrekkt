## Golden Path Workflow

### Phase 1: Assessment
1. **Current State Audit** — What exists today? What's working, what's not?
2. **Goal Definition** — What does success look like? (Use SMART framework)
3. **Gap Analysis** — What's the delta between current state and goal?

### Phase 2: Design
4. **Structure Creation** — Build the system/plan/framework
5. **Metric Selection** — Choose 2-3 leading indicators to track
6. **Cadence Setting** — Daily, weekly, or monthly review rhythm

### Phase 3: Execution
7. **Implementation** — Execute the plan with consistent action
8. **Tracking** — Log data at the defined cadence
9. **Review & Adjust** — Weekly check-in, monthly deep review

### Phase 4: Optimization
10. **Pattern Recognition** — What trends emerge over 4+ weeks?
11. **Refinement** — Adjust plan based on data, not feelings
12. **Automation** — What can be systematized or templated?

## Edge Cases & Anti-Patterns

### Anti-Pattern 1: Over-Engineering
**Symptom**: Spending more time tracking/planning than doing.
**Fix**: Simplify to the minimum viable tracking system. If it takes >5 minutes to log, it's too complex.

### Anti-Pattern 2: All-or-Nothing Thinking
**Symptom**: Missing one day leads to abandoning the entire system.
**Fix**: Build in explicit "recovery protocols" — a missed day is data, not failure.

### Anti-Pattern 3: Metric Fixation
**Symptom**: Optimizing the number instead of the underlying behavior.
**Fix**: Regularly ask "is this metric still measuring what I care about?"

### Anti-Pattern 4: Comparison Trap
**Symptom**: Benchmarking against others instead of past self.
**Fix**: Only compare current-you to previous-you. External benchmarks are context-free.

## CLAIMS / COUNTEREXAMPLE / CONTRADICTIONS

### CLAIMS
- Consistent tracking beats sporadic bursts of effort for personal goals
- Simple systems maintained over months outperform complex systems abandoned in weeks
- Weekly reviews are the minimum viable feedback loop for personal goal management

### COUNTEREXAMPLE
- Some personal goals (creative pursuits, relationship building) resist quantification — forcing metrics can be counterproductive
- Over-tracking can create anxiety that undermines the goal it's supposed to support

### CONTRADICTIONS
- "Track everything" vs "Keep it simple" — the right balance depends on your tracking tolerance and the goal's importance
- "Data-driven decisions" vs "Trust your gut" — personal goals often have emotional components that metrics miss
