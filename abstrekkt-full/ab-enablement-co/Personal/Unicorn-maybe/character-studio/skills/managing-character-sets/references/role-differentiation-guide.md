# Role Differentiation Guide — managing-character-sets

How to make each character in a set visually and narratively distinct
while keeping them obviously part of the same world.

---

## The Differentiation Principle

A character set fails when any two characters are "the same character in different colors."
Each character must have at least **3 differentiation axes** from every other character in the set.

### Differentiation Axes

| Axis | Options |
|------|---------|
| **Species** | Different animal, different humanoid type |
| **Age bracket** | Toddler / child / teen / adult / elderly |
| **Body type** | Round / angular / tall / stocky |
| **Primary color** | Must be distinct per character |
| **Role/archetype** | Hero / mentor / sidekick / comic relief / antagonist |
| **Expression energy** | Calm / excited / determined / playful |
| **Prop type** | Learning / action / comfort / status |

### Minimum Rule
Each character must differ from every other character on at least 3 axes. If two characters share species AND age bracket AND body type, they are too similar — change at least one.

---

## Pair Configurations (2 Characters)

### Hero + Sidekick
- Hero: taller, more balanced body, determined or confident expression
- Sidekick: shorter or rounder, brighter or contrasting color, playful expression
- Differentiation axes to prioritize: species, body type, expression energy, primary color

### Protagonist + Antagonist
- Protagonist: warmer palette, open expression, bright eyes
- Antagonist: cooler palette, sharper features, narrowed eyes
- Note: Antagonist should not look frightening for children's content — "mischievous" or "smug" is appropriate
- Differentiation axes to prioritize: palette temperature, expression, props (protagonist = active tool, antagonist = strategic prop)

### Lead + Mentor
- Lead: younger, mid-sized, energetic expression
- Mentor: older, larger or taller, calm expression, distinguished detail (silver hair, glasses, staff)
- Differentiation axes to prioritize: age bracket, body type, expression energy, props

## Cast Configurations (3–5 Characters)

### The Team of 4 (recommended ensemble structure)

| Slot | Role | Differentiation priority |
|------|------|--------------------------|
| 1 | Lead | Most recognizable species/color; balanced archetype |
| 2 | Action specialist | Different species; same world aesthetic; more athletic body |
| 3 | Intelligence specialist | Older or same-aged; glasses or tech prop; calmer expression |
| 4 | Comic relief | Smallest; roundest; brightest color; biggest smile |

### The Family (parent + children)

| Slot | Role | Differentiation strategy |
|------|------|--------------------------|
| 1 | Parent | Same species as children; adult age bracket; mentor expression |
| 2 | Older child | Teen bracket; slightly taller; more confident expression |
| 3 | Younger child | Child bracket; rounder; most playful expression |

**Rule:** Family sets can share species because age bracket differentiation is the primary axis.

### The Brand Team (3–5 professionals)

All same world aesthetic (professional), differentiate by:
- Role-specific garment (doctor coat vs. engineer vest vs. teacher cardigan)
- Species (mandatory: different per character)
- Expression energy (leader = confident, specialist = focused, newcomer = curious)

---

## Antagonist Design Rules

Antagonists in family/children's content follow different rules than hero characters:

1. **Not frightening** — no sharp teeth displayed, no angry scowl, no red glowing eyes
2. **Mischievous, not evil** — smirk or smug expression is the maximum intensity
3. **Cool palette, not dark** — slate blue, forest green, or deep purple rather than black
4. **Props = strategy, not weapons** — chess piece, sealed vial, folded map vs. swords or guns
5. **Same body style** — antagonist uses the same body type family as heroes (rounded for kids' content)

For adult-oriented brands, these rules can be relaxed — confirm with user.

## Common Set Failures and Fixes

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Same-species sameness | Two fox characters look identical except color | Change one to a different species |
| Color clash | Two characters' primary colors fight each other | Separate warm/cool; check palette token |
| Expression sameness | All characters have identical smiles | Vary: smile / smirk / curious / determined |
| Role ambiguity | Cannot tell leader from sidekick | Leader gets most saturated primary color; sidekick gets secondary |
| Age bracket collision | Two "adult" characters blur together | Assign different age brackets or clearly different body types |
| Prop repetition | Two characters hold a book | One keeps book; give the other a different tool from the same world |

## Consistency Checklist (Pre-Generation)

Before generating any character image, verify:

- [ ] All characters share the same 5 Design Tokens
- [ ] No two characters share the same species AND age bracket
- [ ] No two characters share the same primary outfit color
- [ ] Each character differs from every other on at least 3 differentiation axes
- [ ] Antagonist (if present) follows the non-frightening design rules
- [ ] Expressions are varied across the set
- [ ] Props are role-differentiated (no duplication)
- [ ] All CHARACTER_DESCRIPTIONs score ≥70/100 on the prompt rubric
