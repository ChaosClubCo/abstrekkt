Chapter 1: Reading the Room

The air in O'Toole's always felt heavy before the first rush. It hung there, a dense composite of stale beer soaked into the floorboards, industrial bleach used with too much enthusiasm the night before, and the quiet hum of the refrigeration units fighting a losing battle against the impending summer heat.

I stood by the service well, wiping down a surface that was already clean. It was a nervous habit, a physical tic to ground me before the chaos broke. You learn quickly in this business that the calm is a lie. The door would open, the bell would ring, and the room would demand a performance.

My jaw was tight. Task-locked. The physical state of anticipation. Every clink of a glass being stacked behind the bar by Jimmy sounded unnaturally loud. The sensory volume was turned up to ten, waiting for the crowd to drown it out.
