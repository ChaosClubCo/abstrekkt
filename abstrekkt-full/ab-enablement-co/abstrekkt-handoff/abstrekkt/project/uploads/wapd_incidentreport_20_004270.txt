SUMMARY (from Officer Dobschuetz)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
On 02/03/2020 at 2134 hrs., officers responded to 89/Mitchell regarding a male
party passed out behind the wheel of a vehicle. Officers utilized non-contact
vehicle containment and observed the vehicle was still in drive and the brake
was on. Contact was ultimately made with Kyle A. Rosebrook M/W who admitted to
taking Xanax and Buspirone. He exhibited slow and slurred speech, was unsteady
on his feet, and had watery eyes. He performed poorly on SFSTs and was arrested.
He was also in possession of several Xanax bars without a valid prescription.
After a consented blood draw he was processed at the WAPD before being released
to a responsible party. This case was reviewed by the Milwaukee DA's Office.

VEHICLE IN MOTION
-----------------
On 02/03/2020 at approximately 2134 hrs., officers were dispatched to the area
of S 89 St / W. Mitchell St. regarding a male party who was slumped over in
his vehicle. The vehicle was described as a dark color with Wisconsin plate
#ABA4690.

Upon arrival, a purple colored Toyota was observed on S 89 St., just south of
W. Mitchell St., stopped in the middle of the road. Headlights were on. The
driver was later identified via IL identification card as Kyle A. Rosebrook M/W,
slumped over in the front driver seat.

As the officer approached, the vehicle was running. Looking in the driver-side
window, the vehicle was still in drive and the brake lights were on, indicating
his foot was on the brakes.

Sergeant Iskandarani and Officer Dobschuetz utilized non-contact vehicle
containment (NCVC) to ensure the vehicle didn't move or flee.

The driver door was locked. After the officer pounded on the window several
times, Kyle woke up. He was advised to put the vehicle in park and turn it off.
Car keys were placed on top of the hood.

PERSONAL CONTACT
----------------
Kyle was asked why he was slumped over and stated he didn't realize he was. He
was asked to exit the vehicle.

He was slow to respond to questions.

He stated he took Xanax, approximately two hours prior, and that he was
prescribed it. He stated he was coming from a friend's house in the area of
31/Juneau in Milwaukee, on his way home.

He also stated he is prescribed Buspirone and Lexapro, last taken around 1–2 PM.
He stated he didn't use illegal drugs and didn't feel impaired.

Based on the totality of circumstances — passed out behind the wheel of a
running vehicle in drive, slow/thick speech, unsteady on his feet, and
admission to earlier medication — the officer believed Kyle was likely under the
influence of an unknown drug and requested SFSTs.

DRE Review:
  Morphine = Narcotic Analgesic
  Alprazolam = CNS Depressant

  Both CNS Depressants and Narcotic Analgesics can impair the ability to safely
  operate a motor vehicle.

  Common signs of Narcotic Analgesic impairment:
    Constricted pupils, little/no reaction to light, slow pulse, lowered blood
    pressure/temp, flaccid muscle tone, droopy eyelids, drowsiness, nausea,
    depressed reflexes, slow raspy speech, dry mouth, facial itching, euphoria,
    sensory distortions, disorientation, slow breathing.
    Duration varies by substance (heroin 4–6 hrs, methadone up to 24 hrs).

  Common signs of CNS Depressant impairment:
    Normal pupils (some may dilate), slow reaction to light, lowered pulse/BP,
    normal body temp, flaccid muscle tone, horizontal gaze nystagmus, lack of
    convergence, uncoordinated/disoriented, sluggish, thick slurred speech,
    drunk-like behavior, gait ataxia, drowsiness, droopy eyes, fumbling.
    Duration: barbiturates 1–16 hrs, tranquilizers 4–8 hrs.
