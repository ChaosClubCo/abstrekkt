THE BREAKTHROUGH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Question That Started It
-----------------------------
"I have that drug problem, dopamine issues there, right? So then I fall in love
with AI, dopamine there. These new tools allow me new 'powers' dopamine there.
I spend more money when coming across new tools, dopamine there."
Kyle recognized the pattern moments before this chat began. He'd built guardrails
to catch exactly this—and this session is the evidence that they work.
The Recognition
---------------
Not a relapse. System failure detection working as designed. Kyle was feeding
context, we hit a guardrail, Kyle re-evaluated, we found the correct approach.
This is what recovered dysregulation looks like:
The impulse to sprawl, to acquire, to chase novelty—that hardware never goes
away. But now it's operating from a regulated baseline. And now it has guardrails.
THE THESIS CRYSTALLIZED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is not a book about addiction recovery. This is not a book about
productivity. This is a letter to Kinsley.
"I want my daughter to know I'm not the piece of shit that I was. I want you to
see that your dad loves you and did the work to stay functional for you, even
when it cost everything."
DYSREGULATION VS. ADDICTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Actual Problem (Not Addiction)
------------------------------------
- Childhood trauma (root cause)
- Untreated dysregulation: ADHD, crippling self-worth, depression, unmedicated
- Family rescue system that prevented competence building
- Self-medication cycle: use to forget inadequacy → black out → do monstrous
things → get rescued → repeat
Evidence of the Monstrous Things
----------------------------------
- Blacked out on Xanax, damaged someone's car in parking lot at work
- Police called when someone filmed him driving intoxicated
- Woke up in hospital (at least 2 times, maybe more—can't remember)
- 60mg Xanax in 3 days, sometimes more. Aggressive, confrontational behavior.
The Distinction
----------------
This is dysregulation management, not addiction recovery.
The dopamine-seeking, novelty-craving, compulsion toward stimulation—that
hardware doesn't disappear. But now it's operating from a regulated baseline
instead of chaos. And it's generating documented output instead of hidden spiral.
THE GIRLFRIEND & THE BOTTOM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
How It Started
--------------
She asked him to try meditation. He tried it. But then:
- He became unhealthily invested in her
- Relapse on Xanax (bad relapse, court involvement)
- She tried to get restraining order to prevent him seeing Kinsley ("to fuck
with him")
- She had extensive mental health issues + enabling patterns
The Bottom
----------
Kyle was journaling about all the abuse she'd put him through.
Panic attack so severe: hyperventilated, cried, sobbed. Called suicide
prevention hotline.
Crisis hotline put him on hold and never came back.
Father walked by, heard him, came in concerned (showed up like Kyle would for
Kinsley). Kyle snapped because dysregulation couldn't receive help being offered.
The Journal
-----------
"It hurt. So much. I loved this woman. Poured everything, anything into it."
Kyle shredded it. The cost of loving someone who reproduced the chaos.
What She Actually Was
----------------------
Kyle has "borderline hate" for her. But he also acknowledges: "She did change
my life."
Because before she destroyed him, she asked him to try meditation.
GOING WEST: THE FOUNDATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Decision
------------
Mother arranged a west trip to meet a life coach. Kyle's realization:
"I've tried everything else. Now I'm going to try my hardest with this."
Different from trying. Actual commitment while still in crisis.
Meditation as Engineering Problem
-----------------------------------
Kyle didn't approach meditation as wellness or spirituality. He approached it
as an engineer:
- Identified the system: anxiety at nervous system level
- Found the mechanism: box breathing, grounding, guided meditation
- Learned the physiology: parasympathetic activation, predictable outcomes
- Practiced deliberately: not "tried it"—practiced with intention and rigor
- Predicted outcomes: could tell when sleep would come, when stress would
escalate
Taught It Systematically
------------------------
- To Kinsley
- To life coach
- Built apps around it
The Reframe
-----------
Instead of: "I want this, this, this" (prayer language)
He learned: "I am open to this. I am open to forgiving myself. I am open to
believing I can do this."
Permission, not force. Surrender, not defeat.
NORTH STAR: KINSLEY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Load-Bearing Question
--------------------------
What does Kinsley need to know about her father?
Every chapter, every scene, every piece of excavation serves this question.
The memoir is a letter to her. Not for readers. For her.
The Weight
----------
- 60% of weekends: Kinsley cries because she misses him
- "Why can't she see him more?"
- Kyle still cries too—blaming himself for her having to feel those feelings
What the Tools Actually Do
---------------------------
The meditation. The tools. The guardrails. The systems. All 240 of them.
They exist because Kyle has to show up on Friday and his daughter is going to
cry and he can't fix that, and he has to stay regulated enough to be present
with her while she's hurting.
Not productivity optimization. Not self-help. Not recovery.
Presence. Without rescue. Without disappearing.
What She Needs to Read
-----------------------
Your dad wasn't good. He did monstrous things. He hurt your mom. He hurt people.
Your dad didn't get fixed. He can't recover what was lost.
Your dad built himself into someone who could stay present with your pain
without running from it, without making it your job to fix him, without ever
disappearing on you again.
Your dad documented it because you mattered that much.
