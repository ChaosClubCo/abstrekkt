Lawyer Type 2 Chaos Club Only

Digital Health / AI + Behavioral Health Attorney

Illinois passed a law in 2025 restricting AI in mental health therapy. Peer support platforms are exempt — but that exemption is narrow and hinges entirely on how your platform is structured and described. This is not optional for Chaos Club Co. before launch.

The specific risk: Illinois's 2025 AI Mental Health Therapy Act caps violations at $10,000/violation. Peer support, self-help materials, and educational resources are explicitly exempt — but only if you don't "purport to offer therapy or psychotherapy services." One wrong line in your ToS or UI copy could blow the exemption.

Employment IP Audit

One-time review of your employment agreement with your MSP employer before FlashFusion signs any client contracts. Do not skip.
The risk: Most employment agreements contain an IP assignment clause that says anything you create during employment that's related to the employer's business belongs to them. If that clause is broad, it could cloud FlashFusion's IP — especially any tools built at work or skills libraries created on company time. You need to know exactly what it says before a client pays you.

What to Pull Out of Your Employment Agreement

  • !IP Assignment Clause — Does it assign "all inventions" or only those "related to employer's business"? The narrower, the better for you.
  • !Work-for-Hire Language — Does it claim employer owns anything created using employer equipment or time?
  • !Non-Compete Clause — Does it restrict you from providing competing services? (Memory says no non-compete — verify the exact language.)
  • !Moonlighting / Outside Employment Clause — Does it require disclosure of outside business activity?
  • →Illinois IP Exception (765 ILCS 1060/2) — Illinois law specifically exempts employee IP if it was developed entirely on your own time, without employer equipment, and doesn't relate to the employer's business or anticipated R&D. Know if this applies to your situation.

What to Tell Your Startup Attorney

BRIEF FOR ATTORNEY — EMPLOYMENT IP REVIEW I am a full-time employee at a managed IT services firm (not named for privacy reasons). I am forming FlashFusion LLC, a B2B AI enablement consultancy. I have no non-compete clause to my knowledge, but I need the exact language verified. Key questions for review: 1. Does my employment agreement contain an IP assignment clause, and if so, what does it cover? 2. Does the clause apply to work done entirely on my own time, using my own equipment, unrelated to my employer's core business? 3. My employer is an MSP (managed IT / service desk). My business is B2B AI enablement consulting. Is there a colorable argument that these are related enough to create an IP claim issue? 4. Do I have any disclosure obligation to my employer about FlashFusion before I begin taking paying clients? 5. Are there any other provisions — non-solicitation, confidentiality, moonlighting — that I need to structure around? 6. Does the Illinois Employee Patent Act (765 ILCS 1060/2) protect any of my pre-formation work from employer claims? Please advise before I execute the IP Assignment Agreement or sign any client contracts under FlashFusion LLC.
