I am not in recovery. I am under construction.

There is a difference. Recovery assumes the old self is still in there, waiting to be excavated. The old self is gone. Whatever I am now is a different person, built out of non-standard materials, using a process I am still inventing while I live inside it.

A story that doesn't arc clean.

What broke

The architecture broke. The instinct didn't. The cognitive disruption left exactly one thing intact, the ability to read a room, diagnose what's happening in it, and call it before it lands. The entire reconstruction was built on top of that.

What the systems are

About thirty tools, used deeply. They are not productivity. They are prosthetic memory, the place I keep things when my head won't.

The Sedona line

I had been holding the wheel for so long that the moment I let go, I started building something I could have only built with both hands free.

Chapter 01, the frameworks that didn't fit

Recovery assumes there's something to return to.

The treatment paths I walked through were built on a baseline assumption: that the person on the other side of the program is, fundamentally, the person who walked in. The work, then, is to clear the wreckage and let that person come back.

That isn't what was sitting in front of any of the programs I went through. The hardware had been changed by what happened to it. There was no earlier self waiting to be excavated. Each program was designed for a different patient than the one in the chair.

So the credentialed path didn't open. The frameworks I trust now are the ones I built. Including this one.

Chapter 02, what the scan said out loud

A picture of the thing that didn't have a name yet.

The SPECT scan was the first piece of evidence that what I had been describing for years wasn't an attitude problem. It was a map of the actual hardware. The image confirmed what the systems already knew: this is a brain that processes the world through diagnosis, not through experience.

Reading the room is also a way of not being in it. The instinct that survived is not free. The systems saved me and the systems keep me at arm's length from the thing that broke me. Both are true.

"I had been holding the wheel for so long that the moment I let go, I started building something I could have only built with both hands free."
Chapter 03, what got built instead

Abstrekkt is the same project as the book.

The platform is not separate from this story. It is the version of the story I am building for the people still inside their own. No masks. No clinical distance. Not selling recovery. Offering construction tools to people who, like me, are not going back anywhere.

Underneath all of it, the project is for my daughter. She will not remember the Fridays I was tired. She will remember what her dad built. The book I am writing is a letter. She is the only reader who has to understand it. The rest is overflow.
