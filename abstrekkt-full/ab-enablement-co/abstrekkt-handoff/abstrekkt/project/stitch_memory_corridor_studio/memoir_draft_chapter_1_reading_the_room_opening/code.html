Chapter 1: Reading the Room

The air in O'Toole’s always tasted of old wood, spilled lager, and industrial bleach—a specific, astringent terroir that signaled the start of a shift. In the twenties, before the systems broke down, it wasn't about intuition; it was raw processing power. A functional competence so pure it felt like breathing.

I lived in a jaw-clenching dual-track state. Track One: the immediate. Three pints of Guinness settling, exact change calculated down to the penny, the subtle shift in weight of the keg downstairs. Track Two: the atmospheric background track, a continuous, looping internal dialogue entirely scored by John Williams. Star Wars motifs bleeding into the clatter of pint glasses. It was loud, chaotic, and perfectly calibrated.

The 'Diagnostic Instinct' wasn't something learned; it was native hardware. I didn't need to look at a patron to know what they needed; I felt the disruption in the room's frequency. A shift in posture at the far end of the rail registered as a physical pressure drop. No conscious thought, just immediate, autonomic response. A surveillance readout of human behavior executed without latency.
