Sedona · The Construction

I've tried everything else.
Now I'm going to try my hardest.

Phyllis offered the lifeline, three days in the red rock, a chance to meet Soaring Bear. I went carrying every reason it wouldn't work. I went anyway.

Field record · Sedona, AZ

The first morning, the noise didn't follow me up.

I kept waiting for it, the running count, the static, the part of me that narrates every failure in real time. It usually arrives before I'm fully awake. Up there it just… didn't. I didn't trust it. I stayed in it anyway.

That was the whole instruction, as far as I could tell. Don't fix the quiet. Don't explain it. Stay.

I am open to this helping.

I'd spent years reading rooms. Here there was no room to read.

Just rock that had been doing this for two hundred million years and didn't need anything from me. The diagnostic instinct had nowhere to point. For the first time the engine idled instead of redlined, and the quiet that came wasn't empty. It was load-bearing.

Soaring Bear came with me the whole way up.

Small enough to fit in a jacket pocket. A keepsake, a witness, a reminder that the thing that finally reached me wasn't a system or a protocol, it was a person, and a place, and a willingness I'd refused for years.

I set him on the rock and let the valley do the talking.

The old architecture broke here, quietly, and something else started getting built.
