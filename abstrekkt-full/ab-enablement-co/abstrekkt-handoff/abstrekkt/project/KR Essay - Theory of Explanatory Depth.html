The Theory of Explanatory Depth

Most explanations feel complete until a second "why" exposes the gaps.

May 12, 2025·12 min read·Mindset

The Illusion of Understanding

We mistake familiarity for understanding. I can name a thing, recognize it, use it in a sentence, and feel as if I grasp it. Then someone asks me to explain how it actually works, and the floor gives way. The feeling of knowing is not knowledge. It is a placeholder we forget to redeem.

The Second Question

The test is simple and merciless: ask "why" twice. The first answer is usually retrieved. The second has to be reasoned. Most explanations stop at the first plausible story, not because the story is true, but because it ends the discomfort of not knowing.

The value of an idea is proportional to the depth of understanding it generates.

Depth Creates Leverage

Surface-level understanding creates fragile results. Depth creates leverage: it lets you build models that generalize, teach clearly, and survive new domains, stress, and time. A shallow grasp works until conditions change. A deep one adapts because it knows what it depends on.

How to Earn It

Move iteratively from what is seen to what is true: observation, pattern, hypothesis, mechanism, principle. Each level must be explainable before you go deeper. Can you say it in simpler terms? What is the first principle underneath? Where does the explanation break? Earn the depth, and the idea becomes yours to use.

If you can't explain it further, you don't yet understand it.
