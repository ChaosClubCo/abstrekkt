MSP Strategy Reality Check // MAY 2025

The MSP AI Playbook
Is Fiction

Not provocatively wrong. Confidently, expensively, specifically wrong, and most MSP operators don't have the time to figure out where it breaks before they've already committed to it.

There's a version of the MSP AI strategy document in every consulting deck right now. Four moves, always the same: build a multi-tenant data lakehouse, fine-tune an open-source model on your cross-client telemetry, layer agentic scaffolding into your RMM, pivot to outcome-based pricing. Clean. Ambitious. Completely untethered from what a real shop can execute next quarter.

I've read enough of these to recognize the pattern. The people writing them have never had to actually do it.

The Data Lakehouse Fantasy

Snowflake and Databricks are real products. They're also expensive, operationally complex, and require data engineering capacity that most MSPs don't have, and can't justify hiring for before the revenue exists to pay for it.

The pitch is real: aggregate anonymized cross-tenant telemetry, use that signal as a moat nobody can replicate. The problem is the pitch skips everything between "idea" and "running pipeline." Data governance agreements with every client. An ingestion layer someone has to build and maintain. Enough normalized volume to make the downstream model work useful. A 51-to-200-person MSP doesn't have that.

Translation Layer
"Build a data lakehouse" = 12-to-18-month infrastructure project at a well-funded org with dedicated ML ops.

As an MSP playbook step, it's a Year 3 roadmap item dressed up as a Q1 execution task.

The Fine-Tuning Fallacy

"Fine-tune Llama 3 for your client industry." That's GPU infrastructure, labeled training data, ongoing model ops, and someone who knows what they're doing with all three. When a consulting doc lists this in one bullet, what they mean is: eventually, a company with real ML resources will do this, and you should be aware it's possible.

What you should do right now: use frontier models via API and put your energy into the prompting, orchestration, and integration layer. That's the work that ships this year.

The Citation Problem

Source Audit, Real Doc in Circulation
Cited as evidence for autonomous OODA loop architecture claims:

instagram.com  ·  mexc.com (crypto exchange)  ·  youtube.com  ·  linkedin.com

No specific pages. Just the domain. Filed as [SOURCED].

When the sourcing is that weak, the claims underneath are weaker. Be skeptical of any AI strategy doc that lists a crypto exchange as a reference for cybersecurity architecture. What you're reading is generated confidence, not evidence-based analysis.

One doc I reviewed cited "OpenAI's Daybreak framework" as a named validation system for AI-generated patches. I can't verify this exists. It reads like a hallucinated product name inserted to sound credible. In a doc making architectural recommendations, that's not a small detail to fabricate.

What It Actually Gets Right

Signal Through the Noise

Trust and system permissions are the hardest moat. An MSP three years deep in a client's network has something no AI startup can purchase. That's chronically undervalued in the strategy conversations.

Outcome-based pricing is the right direction. Hourly billing degrades as automation scales. The direction is correct even if most execution paths described are fantasy.

AI Governance-as-a-Service is a real near-term wedge. Shadow AI is already a breach vector. Clients don't know what their employees are feeding into public models. Auditing that has immediate, billable value.

The Honest Version

The companies winning at the MSP AI layer aren't building foundational models or data lakehouses. They're building execution scaffolding, the integrations, automations, and workflow orchestrations that let existing models act on real client environments.

They're winning on trust, on deep system access, on the ability to ship something that works next quarter instead of promising something transformational in 18 months.

That's a smaller, more specific opportunity. It's also a real one. The playbook that says otherwise isn't lying exactly, it's just describing someone else's operation and handing it to you as instructions.
