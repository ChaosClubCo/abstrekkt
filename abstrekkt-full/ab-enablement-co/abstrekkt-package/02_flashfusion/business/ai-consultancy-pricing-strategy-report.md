### 3. Effective Rate From Target Income (15 billable hrs/wk, 48 wks)

**Method [ESTIMATED model; tax/overhead assumptions flagged]:** Billable hours = 15 × 48 = **720 hrs/year**. Assume **$12,000/year overhead** (E&O/cyber insurance ~$2K, software/LLM APIs/tools ~$4K, accounting/legal ~$2K, marketing ~$2K, misc ~$2K). Self-employment tax = 15.3% of net earnings (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare), an effective ~14.1% after the 0.9235 net-earnings adjustment and half-deductibility [SOURCED: IRS SE-tax structure]. "Target income" = personal income kept *before* federal/state income tax.

Formula: **Net profit needed = Target ÷ 0.859; Revenue = Net profit + overhead; Hourly = Revenue ÷ 720.**

| Target income | Net profit needed | Revenue needed | **Effective hourly** | Day rate (8h) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $60,000 | ~$69,850 | ~$81,850 | **~$114/hr** | ~$910 |
| $80,000 | ~$93,150 | ~$105,150 | **~$146/hr** | ~$1,170 |
| $100,000 | ~$116,400 | ~$128,400 | **~$178/hr** | ~$1,425 |

**Interpretation:** Every floor rate sits *below* prevailing solo AI-consulting rates ($150–$300/hr). The conclusion is decisive: at your target incomes, **pricing is not your constraint — sales velocity and utilization are.** Filling 15 genuinely billable hours/week on nights/weekends (≈720/year) is the hard part. A 50% utilization shortfall (360 hrs) roughly doubles the required rate to ~$228–$356/hr.

### 5. Pricing Models Compared
**AI Setup Audits — fixed-fee vs value-based.** *Recommendation: fixed-fee for SMBs, value-based for mid-market/enterprise.* Flat packages remove decision paralysis and convert faster [SOURCED: AgentiveAIQ, 2024]. **Numbers: SMB fixed-fee $6,500–$9,500; mid-market $15,000–$35,000; enterprise value-based or $50,000+.**

**Custom builds — fixed-fee vs T&M vs milestone.** *Recommendation: milestone-based with a paid discovery phase first* (15–25% of anticipated project value, credited if they proceed) [SOURCED: Stack, 2026]. Fixed-fee only when scope is locked; T&M only when scope is genuinely uncertain (a yellow flag for a "standard" deliverable). **Numbers: discovery $5,000–$12,000; MVP $20,000–$50,000; production build $50,000–$100,000.**

**Training/enablement — per-day vs per-seat vs per-cohort vs retainer.** *Recommendation: per-cohort flat fee for organizations, plus a retainer for ongoing enablement.* Per-seat scales poorly for a solo founder; per-cohort captures full value and matches the champion model. **Numbers: 1-day cohort workshop $5,000–$12,000 flat; multi-week champion program $15,000–$50,000 per cohort; ongoing enablement retainer $3,000–$7,500/month.** Avoid per-day rates that anchor you to a commodity-trainer benchmark.

### 7. Legal Entity Recommendation (Illinois)
- **Recommendation: form a single-member LLC now; elect S-corp taxation once net profit reliably exceeds ~$40,000–$50,000.**
- **Sole proprietorship:** simplest, but no liability shield — unacceptable for AI consulting given the liability exposure below.
- **Single-member LLC:** liability protection + pass-through taxation (Schedule C). Illinois taxes pass-through income at the flat **4.95%** personal rate, plus a **1.5% Personal Property Replacement Tax** on net income for LLCs taxed as partnerships/S-corps [SOURCED: Jupid, 2026; UpCounsel, 2025].
- **S-corp election:** splits income into reasonable salary (payroll-taxed) + distributions (exempt from the 15.3% SE tax). Generally worthwhile above ~$40,000–$50,000 profit [SOURCED: Jupid, 2026]; one Illinois CPA example shows ~$4,620 saved on $40,000 of distributions [SOURCED: Lewis CPA, 2025]. At your $60K–$120K target, S-corp election is likely advantageous in Years 1–2 — confirm with a CPA, factoring payroll-admin cost.
- **Illinois specifics:** LLC **annual report = $75** (late penalty $100, reduced from $300 under HB 4578) [SOURCED: LexiState/Illinois SOS, 2026; FileForms, 2026]; LLCs are **not** subject to franchise tax, and Illinois **phased out the corporate franchise tax** for amounts due on/after Jan 1, 2025 [SOURCED: LexiState, 2026]. Articles of Organization filing ~$150; you may serve as **your own registered agent** at no cost with a physical Illinois address (Grayslake qualifies), or pay $100–$300/yr for a service [SOURCED: LexiState, 2026]. Illinois recognizes the federal S election [SOURCED: Wolters Kluwer/BizFilings].
- **Verify against:** IRS (Form 2553), Illinois Secretary of State (ilsos.gov), Illinois Department of Revenue.

US generative-AI lawsuits exceeded 700 by early 2025, up ~10x since 2021 [SOURCED: Gallagher Re, 2025]; **Gallagher's 2026 AI Adoption and Risk Survey found one in five insurance professionals said a client experienced AI-related losses in the past year, with most policies not explicitly addressing AI risk** [SOURCED: Gallagher, 2026].

Berkley** filed an "absolute" AI exclusion and **Hamilton** a generative-AI exclusion [SOURCED: Zelle LLP, Oct 2025; Harvard Law Forum, Sep 2025]. A Gallagher Re/MIT study found only about half of AI-related losses in 2025 were fully covered [SOURCED: Gallagher Re/MIT, 2025]. Affirmative AI products are emerging: **Armilla** (first affirmative AI liability policy, Lloyd's-backed, Apr 2025), **Munich Re aiSure**, **AIUC** (up to $50M), **Testudo** (up to $10M) [SOURCED: AI Standard of Care, Dec 2025]. **Action: explicitly ask your broker whether your Tech E&O policy contains an AI exclusion before binding — if your policy renewed after Oct 2025 the language may have been added silently.** An Illinois-relevant suit (*Nippon Life v. ### 9. Risks
- **Key-person risk (solo founder):** the "bus factor" — if you're sick/burned out the company stops. *Mitigation:* contractor bench, documented SOPs, certified champion-delivery partners.

## DELIVERABLE 3 — FUNDING LANDSCAPE

### 1. Do Consulting Businesses Raise VC? (No — and that's fine)
VCs structurally avoid pure services businesses: they scale linearly with billable headcount, exit at low revenue multiples, and "the critical assets have legs that can walk out the door" [SOURCED: John Gannon, VC blog; Zendog Labs, 2023]. VCs need businesses that go from a few customers to millions fast — services can't. **What investors fund instead:** productized/repeatable offerings, or a consultancy that uses services profit to finance a product, then "morphs from a consultancy to a product company" [SOURCED: Zendog Labs, 2023]. **Threshold before a services-adjacent business raises:** productized, recurring (ARR-like) revenue with non-linear scaling. **Bootstrapping is the norm** for professional services and is an advantage (cash-flow positive Day 1).

### 2. Grants for AI Upskilling / Workforce Development
- **Federal — WIOA (DOL):** DOL issued **TEGL 03-25 (Aug 26, 2025)** explicitly encouraging states/local boards to use WIOA Title I funds for AI literacy and training [SOURCED: US DOL ETA, 2025]. *Mechanism matters:* WIOA training dollars flow to participants via **Individual Training Accounts**, and a provider must be on the state **Eligible Training Provider List (ETPL)** — which requires programs leading to a recognized credential [SOURCED: CareerOneStop; Arizona DES ETPL example, 2025]. The **Industry-Driven Skills Training Fund** awarded $86M+ to 14 states in Sept 2025, reimbursing employers for training in AI-infrastructure occupations [SOURCED: US DOL, 2025].
- **Federal — NSF SBIR (America's Seed Fund):** Phase I up to **$305,000** (2026 solicitation; some AI topics historically ~$275K), Phase II up to ~$1.25M; AI is an eligible topic; ~10–20% funding rate; for-profit, <500 employees, ≥50% US-owned; requires genuine high-risk R&D (not "straightforward engineering or incremental product development") [SOURCED: NSF SBIR, 2026; BW&CO, 2026]. **Caveat: SBIR statutory authority lapsed Sept 30, 2025 and NSF paused new Project Pitches as of Dec 2025** [SOURCED: NSF, Dec 2025; PreVeil, 2025]. Prep is 80–120 hours.
- **Illinois — DCEO:** the **Employer Training Investment Program (ETIP)**, administered via the Illinois Manufacturers' Association, reimburses **up to 50%** of eligible training costs to Illinois employers [SOURCED: IMA, 2025]; the **Job Training and Economic Development (JTED)** grant funds career-pathway training for barrier-facing populations [SOURCED: Illinois DCEO NOFO]. Illinois ranks #1 for workforce development in the Midwest [SOURCED: Illinois DCEO, 2026]. *These flow to employers/non-profits, not to a solo training vendor directly.*
- **Platforms (Granted.com / Kindora):** index the above categories — e.g., the NSF SBIR AI topic at $275K–$305K is listed on Granted [SOURCED: Granted AI, 2026].
- **Eligibility, sole proprietor vs LLC:** As a **sole proprietor** you can technically pursue SBIR (for-profit small business), but most workforce dollars (WIOA/ETPL, DCEO ETIP/JTED) require an **organized entity, GATA pre-qualification (Illinois), recognized credentials, and often non-profit or established-provider status** — an **LLC materially improves eligibility and credibility**. Realistically, the training arm's fastest path is to (a) get on the Illinois ETPL with a credentialed program, and (b) partner with employers/community colleges that *already hold* the grants, delivering as their vendor — rather than winning grants directly as a solo shop.

### 3. Y Combinator Reality Check

### 4. Friends-and-Family Round Mechanics
- **Instruments:**
  - **SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity):** no interest, no maturity date; converts on the next priced round via valuation cap and/or discount. Simplest and most founder-friendly; preferred for F&F by most US startup counsel [SOURCED: Arc, 2025].
  - **Convertible note:** debt that converts; carries interest (typically 2–8%) and a maturity date — adds default risk if conversion is delayed [SOURCED: Dreamit Ventures, 2021].
  - **Straight equity:** grants ownership immediately but forces an early (often wrong) valuation [SOURCED: UpCounsel, 2025].
  - **Revenue-share loan:** repaid from revenue; non-dilutive but a cash-flow drag — viable for a cash-flow-positive consultancy but unusual in startup F&F.
- **Typical 2024–2026 terms:** discount **15–25% (20% most common)**; convertible-note interest **2–8%** [SOURCED: Allied Venture Partners, 2024; Dreamit, 2021]. **For F&F specifically, leading startup counsel recommends an *uncapped*, discounted SAFE with an MFN ("most favored nation") provision** to avoid mis-anchoring your valuation too early/too low [SOURCED: Silicon Hills Lawyer, 2015/2021]. 2025 annual **gift-tax exclusion is $19,000** per recipient if a family member structures it as a gift instead [SOURCED: Rho, 2025].
- **"Iron-clad proof" a family investor should expect:** a written agreement/term sheet (never a handshake); a one-page business plan + financial model; documented traction (signed audit/build contracts, pipeline, the 244-plugin catalog / 32 courses / 50 skills as IP evidence); the executed SAFE; and regular (quarterly) investor updates [SOURCED: Rho, 2025; UpCounsel, 2025].
- **Securities-law compliance (do not skip):** Even an F&F round sells "securities" and needs an exemption. The standard is **Rule 506(b) of Regulation D**, a safe harbor under **Section 4(a)(2)** [SOURCED: SEC / Investor.gov]. Under 506(b) you may sell to **unlimited accredited investors plus up to 35 non-accredited but "sophisticated" investors**, with **no general solicitation/advertising** [SOURCED: SEC / Investor.gov]. **Accredited investor = $1M net worth excluding primary residence, OR $200K individual / $300K joint income in each of the prior two years** (thresholds unchanged since 1982; only ~19% of US households qualify) [SOURCED: SEC.gov, updated Jun 12, 2024; SEC via NAPA-Net]. **A Form D must be filed with the SEC within 15 days of the first sale** (the date the first investor is irrevocably committed) [SOURCED: SEC.gov, Rule 503(a)]; 506(b) also gets a blanket exemption from state "blue-sky" registration, whereas Rule 504 (up to $10M) and bare Section 4(a)(2) do not [SOURCED: JAH Law]. *Critical:* if even one non-accredited family member invests under 506(b), you must provide formal disclosure documents to all non-accredited investors, and non-compliance exposes you to rescission claims and penalties [SOURCED: SEC / Investor.gov; Moschetti Law, 2024].

## FINAL SECTION — 5 Questions You Should Have Asked But Didn't

1. **"What happens to my pricing when my clients can do 80% of my audit with Claude themselves in 18 months?"** Commoditization is already compressing execution rates, and the pivot to value-based pricing is the market's response [SOURCED: Leanware, 2026]. Your defensibility must live in the *methodology and outcomes* (the champion model, documented ROI) — not in deliverables an LLM will soon generate. Build the moat now, while you still have pricing power.

2. **"Does my Tech E&O policy actually cover an AI hallucination that costs my client money — or did an exclusion get added at renewal?"** This is the single most dangerous blind spot in the plan. With ISO AI-exclusion forms live as of Jan 1, 2026 and carriers like W.R. Berkley filing "absolute" AI exclusions [SOURCED: Zelle LLP, 2025], you could be uninsured for your core delivery risk without knowing it. Confirm coverage in writing before your next engagement.

3. **"Am I building a consultancy or a product company — and have I accidentally priced/named myself into the wrong one?"** You hold genuinely productizable IP (244 plugins, 50 skills, 32 courses). If the long-term goal is an asset sale or outside capital, the services-vs-product framing determines everything — including whether YC/VC is ever relevant. Decide deliberately; don't drift.

4. **"What is my realistic billable-hour ceiling after sales, admin, and delivery overhead — and what's my plan when the pipeline is empty?"** The rate math proves income is achievable, but only at ~720 truly billable hours. On 15–20 total hours/week, sales and admin eat into billable time hard. Model utilization honestly: a 50% utilization miss nearly doubles your required rate. The champion/referral motion is your hedge — every trained champion is a future lead source.

5. **"Will my training arm ever touch grant money directly, or am I better off as the subcontracted vendor to organizations that already hold the grants?"** WIOA/DCEO dollars rarely flow to a solo vendor; they require ETPL listing, credentials, GATA pre-qualification, and usually an organized/non-profit-adjacent entity. The faster, higher-probability path is partnering with community colleges, unions, and employers who already control the funding — which also happens to be your ideal champion-model customer. Align the funding strategy with the go-to-market, not against it.
