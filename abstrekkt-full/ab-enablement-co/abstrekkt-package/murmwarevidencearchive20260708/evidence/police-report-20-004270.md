DRUG RECOGNITION EXPERT REVIEW
Morphine is a Narcotic Analgesic and Alprazolam is a CNS Depressant. I know from training and
experience as a West Allis Police Officer and a State of Wisconsin certified DRE, that both CNS
Depressants and Narcotic Analgesics can have an impairing effect on a person's ability to safely
operate a motor vehicle.
COMMON SIGNS OF A NARCOTIC ANALGESIC
With a Narcotic Analgesic a person's pupils are constricted. The reaction to light is little or none
visible, slow pulse rate, lowered blood pressure, lowered body temperature, and flaccid muscle
tone. Horizontal gaze nystagmus, vertical nystagmus, and lack of convergence will not be present.
A person taking a Narcotic Analgesic may have droopy eyelids (ptosis),drowsiness, nausea, be "on
the nod," depressed reflexes, low raspy slow speech, dry mouth, facial itching, euphoria, fresh
puncture marks, track marks, sensory distortions, disoriented, slow breathing, and constricted
pupils.
The duration of effects are as follows:
Heroin: 4-6 hours
Methadone: Up to 24 hours
Others: Varies
The usual methods of administrations are injected, oral, smoked, and insufflated. The overdose
signs are slow shallow breathing, clammy skin, coma, and/or convulsions.
