TALKING TO YOUR DOCTOR
ABOUT SPECT IMAGING AND THE
AMEN CLINICS METHOD
At Amen Clinics, we use brain SPECT imaging in the following way: When we read scans,
we systematically evaluate 55 areas of the brain on a 9-point scale, looking for areas of
under-activity and over-activity, compared to our normal database and the research that has
been published in the scientific literature. Our findings are based on the physiology of the
brain published in the scientific literature, combined with our own clinical experience of 25+
years, working with approximately fifty thousand (50,000) patients to date.
We always consider the imaging data alongside detailed clinical histories and
neuropsychological testing. Sometimes the imaging data for different psychiatric disorders,
such as ADD, anxiety, depression, and addictions are similar to what is published in the
scientific literature. However, sometimes it is very different, which is why taking other clinical
information into consideration along with the imaging information is so important.
SPECT has taught us that there are multiple subtypes of all the major psychiatric illnesses
like depression, anxiety, ADD, and autism. The scientific literature is now confirming that which
we learned through our years of experience with SPECT imaging—that there are different
causes and biological ‘faces’ of similarly presenting symptoms.
SPECT also gives us a window into the overall health of the brain. Is it vibrant and healthy?
OR Are there toxic, degenerative or traumatic processes to consider?
This information is critical to understanding if a deeper structural or metabolic workup
is indicated, and to investigate the cause of the unhealthy changes and their resultant
symptoms, rather than just putting a band-aid on the problem by treating the symptoms.
Our treatment plans combine the findings of scientific research with our own clinical
experiences. We use the best treatment methods to specifically target each person’s unique
neurobiological needs in order to create a new foundation for long-term success and wellbeing. This is something that could not be possible without looking at the brain we are treating.
Our interventions include:
- medications and dietary supplements
- natural, non-pharmacological treatments and therapies
- lifestyle changes (e.g., sleep, nutrition, exercise, stress management)
We realize that our system is not perfect and that we do not know everything that we want
to know. The nature of science is a never-ending process of questioning, learning, testing
and refining. This is why we follow every patient for satisfaction and clinical outcomes for six
months—something almost no one else does. This is also why we have published research with
scientists at such prestigious universities as UCLA, Thomas Jefferson University, UC Irvine,
University of British Columbia, NOVA Southwest University, and New York University— and we
will continue to do so.
Here is a link to our online database of almost 3000 peer-reviewed studies documenting the
utility of functional imaging in neuropsychiatry:
http://danielamenmd.amenclinics.com/spect-research-introduction/spect-research-tables/
At Amen Clinics, our published data shows that having a SPECT scan changes the
diagnosis or treatment 79% of the time.
Additionally, in a study of 500 consecutive Amen Clinics patients, who on average had 4.2
diagnoses, had failed 3.3 providers and 6 medications; after 6 months, 77% were better across
all measures and 85% had improved quality of life scores.
Canadian research, published in the Journal of Neuropsychiatry in 2014, reported that having a
SPECT scan improved patient outcomes.
Of course, we do not know it all. We learn new things every day, and are continuously excited
about our opportunity to bring greater and greater benefit to our patients. We encourage
you to look at the above information without the preconceptions that are common in the
psychiatric establishment.
We think you will find it compelling, and would invite you to partner with us in the development
and application of this powerful approach to mental health assessment and treatment.

Dr. Creado’s treatment contract:
- My recommendations are based on what I think is going to be
beneficial to you, and the decision to incorporate those
recommendations, is yours.
- When I make a medication recommendation, I provide you resources
to look up the medication, supplements and talk to you briefly about
potential side effects. If you accept a prescription, you have consented
to treatment.
- I want to make it clear, that you need to reach out for help (calling the
emergency number, 911 or reaching out to family or friends) PRIOR
to attempting any self-harm or harm to others. That is a cornerstone of
a therapeutic relationship, without which, it puts us in a situation
where we feel the patient may not be completely candid about how
close they are to the edge, that they are not invested in the therapeutic
process, and limits our ability to help them.
- Just as my patients are free to do what they want with the
recommendations I put forth, I too, have the right to refuse to
prescribe certain medications or interventions that I may believe may
not be in my patients best interests. For example, I am very cautious
about prescribing controlled substances, and usually require my
patients to submit to random drug screenings, to make sure they are
not using illicit substances in conjunction with the treatment, as well
as not change the doses, misuse or abuse, or share medications they
have been prescribed (by me or other providers).
- I have a right to terminate the therapeutic relationship in
circumstances where I may feel the patient is disrespectful to me or
other staff, engages in disruptive behavior, is not invested in the
therapeutic relationship, is not compliant with my recommendations,
wants to pressure me into prescribing medications or other
interventions that may not be in their best interests, or where they
continue to use substances which will limit my ability to help them or
where they refuse random drug screening or other tests to determine
their compliance with the treatment plan.
- If the patient or I decide, for any reason to terminate the therapeutic
relationship, I will provide 30 days of support in terms of prescription
refills, and urgent calls in the interim. If you are becoming manic or
depressed or having thoughts of hurting yourself or others, please call
911 or have somebody take you to the nearest ER. You can then look
for another provider within the Amen network or outside it.
- It is important for my patients to schedule follow-ups, even when
stable, at least every 6 months, for continuity of care, prescription
refills, and monitoring for symptoms and side effects. The onus is on
the patient, to schedule these with one of my assistants. If you do not
do this, then it will be hard to get a follow-up appointment at a time of
your choice, due to a long wait list. Additionally, I will assume that
you have decided to work with another provider. Far too often in the
past, we had situations where patients would not follow-up in time,
slowly worsen, taper off medications or stop implementing the other
recommendations, deteriorate further, end up in crisis or in a
psychiatric hospital, and then reach out to my clinic. This clinic is not
a psychiatric ER/ urgent care facility.
- I thank you for your understanding.
Yours faithfully,
Dr. Shane A. Creado, MD
General psychiatry, Sports Psychiatry and Sleep Medicine
