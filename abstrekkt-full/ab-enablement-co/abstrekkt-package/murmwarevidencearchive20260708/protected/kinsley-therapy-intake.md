Observations of Non-Typical Behaviors
Self-harm
Nail-digging (twice, ~a year apart): I personally witnessed Kinsley dig her nails into her skin during intense sobbing on two separate occasions, leaving visible marks. On one occasion she said she “likes it.”
Episode A (spare bedroom/Leni): After I told Kinsley I wasn’t going to exclude Leni (my then-girlfriend’s daughter) from playing with us, Kinsley ran to the spare bedroom. When I went in, she was uncontrollably sobbing and dug her nails into her skin hard, leaving marks.
Episode B (outdoor cushions/heavy chairs): After a small task conflict (I placed outdoor seat cushions when she had said she would), she became very upset; mucus was streaming, and I observed claw-like scratch marks she’d made on her leg while upset.
Biting at school (reported & documented): Last school year, her teacher and her mom reported she bit herself at school hard enough to leave marks (possibly blood). My mom told me that Dreanne sent her photos of the bite marks, so there is documentation.
Possible school follow-up: My mom recalls that Dreanne said the school counselor would intervene; I don’t have records of that.
When asked: When I try to talk about the scratching/biting, Kinsley often groans/whines or tells me to stop and looks very uncomfortable. When asked later by her mom, she denied doing it.
Transition distress (predictable pattern)
Every weekend when I drive her back to her mom’s, Kinsley sobs for ~5–7 minutes (typically from the expressway exit until arrival).
While crying she often repeats: “I don’t want to go home,” “I want to live with you,” “Why can’t I live with you?” “I don’t see you enough.”
She usually wipes tears and tries to stop before arriving. Once she asked me not to warn her when we were close because it made her sad; even without a warning (and with distraction) she still cried.
At the door: When her mom asked if she’d been crying, Kinsley said “no” despite sobbing minutes earlier.
Asymmetry: This happens every weekend returning to her mom’s. She has only cried once or twice when leaving her mom’s to come to me.
Comfort ask: When I asked how I could help, she once asked for a “daddy-daughter date,” which helped.
Privacy near the house: Walking up to her mom’s once, she said “I’m glad Will isn’t here,” then gave me a look to stop—as if she didn’t want anyone nearby to overhear.
Expressions & “protector” pattern
She has said “I hate my life.” (She may have said stronger words; I don’t want to misquote.)
Sometimes denies or changes things I’ve just witnessed (crying/scratching) when another adult asks later.
She often protects adults instead of herself:
At a movie outing, I raised my voice in a disagreement with her grandma. Kinsley began bawling, begged me to stop, and told me to be nice to her grandma.
She has told me not to “talk to her mom” in tense moments.
Comfort pattern: In some upset moments she resists comfort or tells me to stop if I try to soothe her; other times she seeks closeness. Because of this, I now ask before hugs/kisses so she has choice.
