*Grounded in Wisconsin statute and case law (CourtListener). This is how the courtroom plays out.*

### Issue 1: Paternity & Standing

- **You:** Name on the birth certificate = VPA filed or court order. Certified by the state. Paternity established. Move to § 767.41. *(Randy A.J. v. Norma I.J., 2004 WI 41)*
- **Her:** Cannot confirm VPA was filed; if not, no standing until paternity established first.
- **You counter:** The state's own certified document is the evidence. Wisconsin doesn't place an unmarried father on a BC without a VPA or order. Burden is on them to challenge it.
- **Assessment:** You win IF the envelope confirms your name. Open it.

### Issue 2: Custody & Placement — The Core Fight

- **You:** § 767.41 presumes joint legal custody. Court weighs each parent's willingness to support the child's relationship with the other. You drove 85 miles each way, every weekend, 5+ years, all cost absorbed. She made that drive twice. She denied access on a third party's word. She offered extra time only for childcare. That's convenience-based parenting.
- **Her:** Illinois residency means 170-mile round trips weekly for an 8-year-old. Her whole life is in Milwaukee. "Willingness to support" cuts both ways — she allowed this for years without forcing court.
- **You counter:** Five years proves it works. Don't disrupt an established pattern absent harm. *(In re Custody of H.S.H.-K, 193 Wis. 2d 649)* We're not moving the child — we're formalizing what exists.
- **Assessment:** Your strongest ground. Five years is a long track record the statute requires the court to weigh.

### Issue 3: The Name Change

- **You:** § 786.36 is dispositive. She already changed the surname at dance without consent — documented. Paternity + non-abandonment = court SHALL require consent. The child's unprompted "that's not my last name," witnessed by the dance director, shows she objects.
- **Her:** A dance enrollment form is administrative, not legal. No petition is before the court today.
- **You counter:** It's evidence of intent and pattern. We ask the order to require both parents' consent before any name change petition or institutional name substitution.
- **Assessment:** Strong. Concrete evidence. The dance director is a potential witness.

### Issue 4: Substance Use History

- **Her (leading):** Not a relic — a pattern. Two+ relapses. TBI with 12th percentile recall means he can't reliably track the child's life. Smoking marijuana until this proceeding. 85 miles away, living with parents at 38.
- **You:** Sober since October 2021 — nearly 5 years, weekly custody maintained throughout, zero incidents. The same Amen eval showing 12th percentile recall shows 99th percentile processing speed. Memory addressed through external systems — life coach (18 months), structured routines, the weekly custody quality log. ACE score 0/10. And the marijuana — stopped. Will submit to testing at any time, no notice required.
- **Her counter:** "He stopped" is what he said before each relapse. Order random testing.
- **You final:** No objection to court-ordered random testing. None.
- **Assessment:** Your most dangerous terrain and your cleanest counter. Proactively agreeing to testing reframes substance history from liability to resolved.

### Issue 5: Best Interests — The Dispositive Question

- **You:** The child told her father: "I hate my life." "I don't want to go home." "I want to live with you." Before returning to her mother's: "shhhh don't tell mom because I get in trouble." She was self-harming — nail-digging until marks, said she "likes it." Father initiated therapy. Mother attended zero sessions, then confronted the child about the self-harm outside therapy, causing her to deny it. Documented events, therapist records to be produced.
- **Her:** Statements from an 8-year-old filtered through a parent in litigation are hearsay, limited weight at her age. Self-harm was addressed; therapist said "better." The confrontation was concerned parenting. The child is thriving.
- **You counter:** The therapist's records — not the father's characterization — are what we introduce, to support a child-centered request: a court-ordered provision that the child's ongoing therapeutic support continue and that both parents cooperate with it under § 767.41(5)(am). Not to punish the mother. To protect the child.
- **Assessment:** Lead with the child, not the mother's failures. The therapy framing is the right move.

### Issue 6: Child Support

- **Her:** $49,500/year, nascent business with no revenue. Sole placement formula (§ 767.511) = 17% = ~$700/month.
- **You:** ~104 overnights = above the 92-night shared threshold (DCF 150). Shared formula uses both incomes and the overnight split. Produce her income. No order ever entered, no arrears. Calculate prospectively.
- **Assessment:** Math, not argument — and it favors you.

### Bottom Line From Both Chairs

**Your weaknesses (what your attorney must manage):** Substance history hit first and hard. Illinois residency framed as a burden. TBI memory result is in your own evidence. The informal arrangement recharacterized as her accommodation.

**Her weaknesses (what your attorney exploits):** Five years of presence can't be rewritten. Therapy refusal documented. Name change documented. The obstruction pattern directly implicates § 767.41. The child's statements, surfaced through a GAL rather than your testimony, are harder to dismiss.

**The truth neither side says out loud:** The GAL investigation is where this case gets decided. Your attorney's job is to make sure the GAL sees the full picture before the other side frames it as a stability argument.
