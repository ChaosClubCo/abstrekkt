## The pitch in one paragraph

Content teams invest deeply in source research and distribute thinly. One topic becomes one post for one audience, drives one engagement spike, then goes dead. The Render Engine takes a single piece of source material — an incident report, a customer interview, a post-mortem, a KB article, a podcast transcript — and renders it into four audience-tiered outputs, each calibrated for a different cognitive level and a different distribution surface. Four publications. Four audiences. One research investment.

## What the client gets

Four distinct, audience-calibrated artifacts from one source. Each rendered for its own cognitive level, its own vocabulary, and its own distribution surface.

| Tier | Audience | Cognitive level | Length | Voice | Surfaces |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Newcomer** | New to the topic | Analogies, fundamentals | 800–1,200 words | Friendly, curious | Blog, social, knowledge base |
| **Case Study** | Topic-aware, learning | Concrete narrative | 1,000–1,500 words | Storyteller | Blog, newsletter, sales deck |
| **Strategic** | Decision-makers | Operational implications | 1,200–1,800 words | Authoritative | LinkedIn long-form, executive brief |
| **Technical Protocol** | Practitioners & architects | Specs, edge cases | 1,800–2,500 words | Reference manual | Technical docs, internal wiki, partner enablement |

Each tier covers the same source material but never duplicates phrasing. A reader who sees all four learns four different things — that is the design, not an accident.

## The pipeline (8 stages)

This pipeline is built on the FlashFusion Outcome-Aware Systems framework. It is the same 8-stage architecture used in our Pipeline Specification engagements, applied to content rendering instead of data routing.

### Stage 1 — Source ingestion
The client delivers a single source artifact. Acceptable formats: incident write-up, customer interview transcript, post-mortem, KB article, recorded demo, technical decision memo, product release note. Minimum length: 600 words of substantive material.

### Stage 2 — Source validation (GIGO check)
Before any rendering begins, the source is validated for fact density, narrative arc, and audience-relevance markers. Sources that fail validation are returned to the client with specific gaps identified. Garbage in is not a fixable input.

### Stage 3 — Tier decomposition
We extract the dimensions that must change per tier: vocabulary level, example density, framing metaphor, opening hook, technical depth. This step produces a per-tier rendering brief, not the renders themselves.

### Stage 4 — Parallel rendering
The four tiers render in parallel, each from the same tier decomposition. Outputs are first-pass drafts. No tier is treated as the "primary" — all four are first-class deliverables.

### Stage 5 — Cross-tier consistency check
Every fact in every tier is checked against every other tier. Numbers, names, dates, and causal claims must agree across all four outputs. A factual discrepancy at this stage blocks delivery until reconciled.

### Stage 6 — Brand voice alignment
Each tier is tuned to the client's brand voice for that tier's surface. The newcomer voice for a financial-services client is different from a developer-tools client. We capture this upfront in onboarding; it persists across engagements.

### Stage 7 — Distribution routing
Each tier ships to its designated surface with formatting appropriate to that surface: markdown for blog, native LinkedIn formatting for executive long-form, KB-template for internal docs, etc. Client owns the publish action.

### Stage 8 — Performance tracking
After publication, engagement is tracked per tier per surface. Within 60 days, we surface which tier drove which engagement on which surface for this client. Over time, this becomes a per-client distribution intelligence asset.

## Service tiers

Pricing structure follows the FlashFusion Outcome-Aware AI Systems Maturity Tier model. Final pricing is agreed in the engagement letter; the structure below describes what is included at each level.

### Mini — Single Render
**Format:** one-shot project.
**Cadence:** delivered within five business days from validated source.
**Scope:** one source artifact rendered into all four tiers.
**Brand voice:** generic professional. No client-specific voice tuning.
**Performance tracking:** none.
**Use this when:** the client wants a proof-of-concept render or has one high-value source they want to maximize.

### Mid — Subscription
**Format:** monthly subscription.
**Cadence:** four source artifacts per month, each rendered into all four tiers (sixteen outputs/month).
**Brand voice:** client-tuned during onboarding, persists across renders.
**Performance tracking:** monthly per-tier engagement report.
**Use this when:** the client has a steady content cadence and wants to multiply distribution leverage on every topic.

### Enterprise — Embedded
**Format:** custom engagement with platform integration.
**Cadence:** continuous. Source artifacts pulled directly from the client's systems (Notion, Confluence, FreshService, internal wiki, etc.).
**Brand voice:** client-tuned, with quarterly recalibration.
**Performance tracking:** real-time dashboard, A/B testing per surface.
**SLA:** response-time and uptime commitments documented in the engagement letter.
**Use this when:** the client treats content as a strategic distribution surface and needs render velocity to match their source velocity.

## Proof point — the INT KB equivalence

Before this service was packaged, the FlashFusion team operated this exact pipeline internally for a managed-services-provider context. A single service-desk incident — a multi-system integration failure involving a softphone, a CRM, and an SSO provider — was rendered into four tiered artifacts:

| Tier | Internal artifact | Purpose | Audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| Newcomer | "The Troubleshooting Ascent: A Guide to Tiered Technical Support" | Onboarding for new techs | L1 service desk hires |
| Case Study | "Mastering the Support Stack: A Case Study in Tiered Troubleshooting" | Pattern recognition for working techs | L1–L2 service desk staff |
| Strategic | "Operational Protocol: Managing SaaS Integration and Connectivity in Distributed Environments" | Operational decision-making | Service desk leads, ops managers |
| Technical Protocol | "Technical Operations Standard: Hardware Provisioning, Software Integration, and Risk Mitigation in Distributed Environments" | Architectural reference | Senior IT, integration architects |

All four were produced from the same source incident, in the same batch, within the same hour. Each is a first-class artifact — none is a summary or watered-down version of another. Each shipped to its own distribution surface within the MSP organization.

The Render Engine productizes that internal capability for client engagements. The pipeline is the same. Only the source material and the distribution surfaces change.

## Acceptance & quality standards

A render set is "delivered" only when all of the following are true:

1. All four tiers are present and meet their respective length bands.
2. Cross-tier consistency check has passed — no factual or numeric drift across tiers.
3. Each tier matches its target voice (sampled against the brand voice profile, scored ≥ 8/10 on the voice rubric).
4. Each tier is formatted for its designated distribution surface.
5. No tier exceeds the agreed length band by more than 15%.
6. Source attribution is preserved in every tier where appropriate.

Failures trigger a revision cycle. Two revision cycles are included in Mini and Mid; additional cycles are billed at the engagement's documented hourly rate.

## Out of scope

The Render Engine does **not** include:

- Source research or original interviews. We render the source; we do not produce it.
- Design or visual assets (graphics, charts, illustrations). Outputs are text-first.
- Publishing or distribution execution. Client owns the publish action and the surface ownership.
- Translation or localization. Outputs are produced in the client's working language.
- SEO optimization beyond standard formatting. Search optimization is a separate engagement.
- Performance guarantees on individual tier engagement. We track and report; we do not guarantee outcomes that depend on the client's audience and surface health.

## Why this works (the FlashFusion thesis)

Most content services optimize for the producing team, not the consuming audience. They ask "what should we write?" and the answer is one thing per topic. The Render Engine asks "who needs to learn this, and what do each of them need?" — and the answer is four things per topic, each calibrated for its reader.

The leverage compounds. A topic that took twelve hours of source research now distributes across four audiences and four surfaces instead of one. If even two of the four tiers outperform the equivalent single post, the engagement pays for itself on the next round.

The technical underpinning is the FlashFusion Outcome-Aware AI Systems framework: deterministic orchestration (the eight-stage pipeline), probabilistic rendering (the LLM-driven tier outputs), with explicit evaluation (the cross-tier consistency check and the per-tier voice rubric). It is a pipeline architecture applied to content. The architecture is the product. The content is what flows through it.
