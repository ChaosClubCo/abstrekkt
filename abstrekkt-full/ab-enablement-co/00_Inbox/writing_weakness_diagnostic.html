01

The AI fingerprint problem

Your published voice reads machine-assembled — and it contradicts your whole positioning.
High
›

"Beyond the Hype" carries nearly every tell of unedited AI drafting: "seismic shift," "yawning chasm," "sobering reality check," bolded nouns every other sentence, a rhetorical-question closer labeled "Final Thought." Readers in your exact target audience are the most fluent at spotting this.

"We are currently witnessing a seismic shift from the era of 'chatbots' to the rise of autonomous 'Company Builders'…"

The brutal irony: your brand thesis is authenticity — you just rewrote all three case studies to draw from real experience. A post like this undoes that work.

FIX → Treat AI drafts as research notes, never as publishable prose. Rule: every published piece must contain ≥1 specific moment only you could have written (a ticket, a failure, a timestamp). If it could appear on anyone's blog, it doesn't ship.
02

Borrowed authority

"As a strategist, I've seen…" — claiming a generic vantage point when your real one is rarer.
High
›

The post speaks as a veteran enterprise strategist surveying "field data" and "the frontlines." But your actual story — L1 service desk tech who self-built an AI practice, got informally designated SME, and runs a real automation stack at night — is more specific, more credible, and impossible to fake.

"As a strategist, I view Tight Coupling as the primary architectural failure."

Generic authority invites generic skepticism. Specific, slightly unusual authority invites trust.

FIX → Replace persona claims with provenance: "I run 11 n8n workflows for my own platform; here's the one that broke" beats "industry data indicates" every time.
03

Abstraction over scene

Big conceptual claims, zero concrete first-person moments.
Medium
›

The post is wall-to-wall category statements ("value has migrated from information retrieval to autonomous operational execution") with no grounding incident. Strong technical writing alternates altitude: claim → concrete example you lived → claim. Yours stays at 30,000 feet.

FIX → Per section, force one paragraph that starts with a time, a place, or an error message. If a section has no lived example, that's a signal you're repeating someone else's take.
04

Template-shaped structure

Listicle + bold hooks + "Final Thought" — the most common shape on the internet right now.
Watch
›

"5 Surprising Lessons" with a CTA-question closer is structurally indistinguishable from thousands of AI-content-farm posts this month. Structure is a voice decision too. Your "Construction over Recovery" frame is a structure no one else has — use it.

FIX → Pick one piece per month with a non-listicle architecture: a single narrative thread, a before/after teardown, or a failure autopsy.

05

"Author performing depth"

Your own flag: the memoir voice drifts toward someone narrating profundity instead of you talking.
High
›

This isn't my diagnosis — it's yours. You built a voice-check protocol specifically because drafts slide into a register that sounds like "author performing" rather than Kyle. That self-awareness is an asset; the weakness is that the drift keeps happening, which usually means the performing voice is the easier one to produce.

FIX → Draft hard sections out loud (voice memo, then transcribe). Your spoken syntax is the calibration target. If a written sentence couldn't be said across a bar at O'Toole's, rewrite it.
06

Structure paralysis at the entry point

"Where does the book start — I keep going further back in the timeline."
Medium
›

You've described circling the opening, reaching further and further back for the "true" beginning. That's a classic chronology trap: treating the earliest cause as the right first page. Memoirs almost never start at the beginning — they start at the moment of maximum pressure and earn the backstory.

FIX → Declare the opening provisional and ban relitigating it for 90 days. Write the middle. Openings are chosen in revision, not drafted into existence.
07

Scaffolding outpacing pages

More infrastructure around the book than book.
Medium
›

You've built craft skills, continuity protocols, material-search sweeps, thesis-integrity checks — a genuinely impressive support system. But the build/write ratio looks inverted. You've named over-engineering as your known compensation pattern, and the memoir may be where it's running hottest: tooling is progress-shaped, but it isn't prose.

FIX → Hard metric: words drafted per week, tracked, nothing else counts. New tooling is only allowed after a 1,000-word week.
