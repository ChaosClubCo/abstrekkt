Pre-Call Mental Framing

The Frame That Works

You are not selling. You are having a conversation with someone who works at the same altitude — someone who deals with AI in the real world with real constraints. You are peers exploring whether there's overlap. That's it.

You have something real to offer: you see AI implementation across multiple environments because of your MSP context. He sees AI inside one org. That's a useful asymmetry — it positions you as someone with perspective, not a pitch.

How to Stay Calm
  • He already said yes. The hard part is done.
  • He's driving. The energy is casual. Match it.
  • You don't need to impress in the first 60 seconds. You need to be curious.
  • Slow your opening by 15%. Rushing sounds nervous.
  • One question at a time. Never stack two.
What to Avoid
  • Do not over-explain your background upfront
  • Do not monologue about what AI can do
  • Do not use "leverage," "synergy," or "transformative"
  • Do not pivot to selling before you've listened for 10 minutes
  • Do not fill silence with more talking
Say These to Yourself Before You Dial

He agreed to this call. He is not a hostile room.

Listening Support Layer

The Core Problem You're Solving

When you focus on what to say next, you miss what he's actually saying. Your job in each phase is to listen for one thing and respond to that.

Phase Anchors — One Thing Per Phase

OPENING → Is he relaxed or distracted? Match his pace immediately.
RAPPORT → Does he say anything spontaneous? That's the thread.
DISCOVERY → What does he own vs. what was handed to him?
POSITIONING → Does he respond with recognition or skepticism?
ALIGNMENT → Is he nodding verbally or correcting you? Both are good.
CLOSE → Is he offering anything — a name, a time, a topic? That's your next step.

What His Words Often Actually Mean

"We're already using Copilot / ChatGPT / [tool]"

Translation: We have the tool. May not have the strategy. Ask: "How's adoption been — are people actually using it or has it gone quiet after the launch?"

"We have to be careful with our data"

Translation: Compliance is a real blocker on several things he'd otherwise try. Ask: "Is that mostly a policy problem or a technical problem at this point?"

"We're still figuring it out"

Translation: No clear owner or strategy. He may be that person by default. Opening — someone needs to help him figure it out.

"Leadership is really excited about AI"

Translation: He has pressure from above and may not have resources to match it. Often frustration disguised as positivity.

Signals to Read

INTERESTED Asks follow-up questions, shares a specific problem, mentions a person or team by name
CAUTIOUS Keeps answers general, says "we're still figuring it out," pauses before answering
CURIOUS "How does that work?" / "What would you do in a situation like X?"
BRUSHING OFF Short answers, "yeah for sure," redirects to logistics, doesn't ask back

Recovery Tools

IF YOU BLANK
"Give me one second — I want to respond to that properly, not off the top of my head."
"That's actually a more layered question than it sounds. Let me think about the most honest answer."
IF YOU TALKED TOO MUCH
"Sorry — I went long there. What's the version of that problem that's actually relevant to you?"
"I just realized I've been talking for a while. What's your take on this?"
IF HE DOMINATES THE CONVERSATION

Let him. That's good. When there's a pause, ask one specific follow-on about what he just said. Do not introduce a new topic.

"You mentioned [X] — is that something you inherited or built from scratch?"
IF THE ENERGY DROPS
"I want to make sure I'm not keeping you on something that's not useful — is there a direction in this conversation that's actually interesting to you?"
IF YOU MISSED SOMETHING IMPORTANT
"Actually — can I go back to something you said a few minutes ago? I glossed over it and I think it was more important than I gave it credit for."

Use when: he mentioned a specific problem you have something real to offer on.

"You mentioned the adoption friction around [X] — I've actually thought through that problem in a few different ways. Would it be useful if I put something concrete together? Not a pitch, just a framework for thinking about it."
VALUE-ADD CLOSE

DIRECT FOLLOW-UP CLOSE

Use when: there's obvious mutual interest.

LONG-GAME CLOSE

Use when: warm but premature — he's not ready for anything concrete yet.

"I'll stay on your radar. If something comes up where an outside perspective would be useful, or you're evaluating something and want a second opinion — I'm easy to reach. And vice versa: if I come across something relevant to what you're working on, I'll send it along."
