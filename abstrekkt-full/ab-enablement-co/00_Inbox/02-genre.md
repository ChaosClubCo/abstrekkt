Literary nonfiction. Specifically: literary memoir with a systems-thinking framework.

NOT recovery memoir. NOT self-help. NOT productivity manifesto. NOT addiction confessional.

Comparable works:
- "The Noonday Demon" by Andrew Solomon — depression examined with intellectual rigor, not sentiment
- "In the Realm of Hungry Ghosts" by Gabor Maté — addiction without moralism
- "Brain on Fire" by Susannah Cahalan — cognitive disruption narrated from inside
- "The Body Keeps the Score" by van der Kolk — but less clinical, more personal and systems-oriented

What makes this different from those: The engineering response. Most cognitive disruption memoirs center the medical or emotional journey. This one centers what happens when someone with a broken mind builds external infrastructure to replace internal function. The construction, not the damage.
