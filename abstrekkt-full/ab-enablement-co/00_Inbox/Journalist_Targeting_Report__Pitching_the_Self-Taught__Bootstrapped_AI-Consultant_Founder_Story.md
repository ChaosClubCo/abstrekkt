# Journalist Targeting Report: Matching the Self-Taught, Bootstrapped AI-Consultant Founder Narrative

## TL;DR
- The strongest matches are journalists who already cover AI-in-the-workplace and bootstrapped/solo founders as a beat — at the top sit **Jared Lindzon** (Fast Company), **Tim Paradis** (Business Insider), **Amanda Breen** (Entrepreneur), and **Ben Sherry** (Inc.), all of whom have repeatedly written stories aligning with two or more of the seven narrative angles in the last 12 months.
- The "shadow AI as shadow IT" and "champion model" angles are heavily covered by trade/enterprise press, but most authors there (e.g., VentureBeat's Louis Columbus) skew enterprise/Fortune 500 — making them weaker fits for a bootstrapped solo-founder story despite topical overlap.
- Avoid pitching the topic-only writers and vendor-blog authors that dominate search results; prioritize the named, currently-active bylines below, and lead with the angle each journalist has personally demonstrated affinity for.

## Key Findings
- Two distinct journalist clusters match the narrative: (1) **future-of-work / founder-profile reporters** (Lindzon, Paradis, Breen, Sherry, Aki Ito) who are receptive to underdog origin, solo-founder, nights-and-weekends, and small-business angles; and (2) **AI-governance / shadow-AI reporters** (largely enterprise-focused) who match the shadow-AI angle but rarely cover bootstrapped founders.
- The single best overall fit is **Jared Lindzon**, who has explicitly written about white-collar workers pivoting to blue-collar work, the future of work in the AI era, and small businesses — touching at least three of the seven angles.
- **Inc.** is the best-fit publication overall for this founder: its AI coverage (Ben Sherry) and small-business/tech columns (Jason Aten) consistently center entrepreneurs and SMB owners rather than funded startups.
- Several high-topical-relevance authors carry **red flags**: Louis Columbus (VentureBeat) writes almost exclusively enterprise cybersecurity/AI governance; Geoffrey Fowler has shifted to AI-and-kids/consumer-safety and left daily tech column work.

## Recommendations
1. **Stage 1 — lead with the two beat-perfect fits.** Approach **Jared Lindzon** and **Amanda Breen** first. Lindzon owns the future-of-work + trades/blue-collar frame (angles #1, #5, #6); Breen owns the bootstrapped, nights-and-weekends solo-founder franchise (angles #6, #1). For each, tailor to the exact angle they've personally published. Benchmark to change approach: if neither responds within ~2 weeks, widen to syndication-friendly framing.
2. **Stage 2 — the AI-at-work reporters.** Approach **Tim Paradis** and **Ben Sherry** with the "champion model" (#3) positioned as the practical answer to the shadow-AI / AI-anxiety problem they're already covering (#4). Provide a concrete client example (a real estate agent, restaurant owner, or furniture maker) to anchor the SMB angle and differentiate from their usual enterprise/funded-startup subjects. Sherry in particular responds to the MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide" 40%-vs-90% data point.
3. **Stage 3 — columnists and local.** Use **Jason Aten** and **Aki Ito** for thematic/columnist angles, and **John Pletz** only if there is a genuine Chicago hook. These need a sharper news or data peg.
4. **Do not pursue Tier 3 (Columbus) unless** the story is reframed as enterprise shadow-AI governance — which contradicts the bootstrapped-SMB narrative. Threshold to reconsider: only if the founder lands a notable enterprise client or publishes original shadow-AI data.
5. **Verify contact before outreach.** Only Lindzon, Paradis, Aten, and Ito have self-published emails. For Breen, Sherry, and Pletz, use LinkedIn or publication tip lines — do not guess addresses.
