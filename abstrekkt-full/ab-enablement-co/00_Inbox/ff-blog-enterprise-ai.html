The MIT number everyone quoted wrong

In July 2025, MIT published "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025." Based on 52 executive interviews, 153 leadership surveys, and analysis of 300 public AI deployments: 95% of enterprise AI pilots delivered no measurable P&L impact. $30–40 billion invested. Five percent returned anything measurable.

That number circulated everywhere. What circulated less was the actual diagnosis. The failures weren't model failures. They were infrastructure failures. Organizations tried to force probabilistic AI into deterministic, rule-bound systems. The integration layer wasn't built for it. Governance didn't exist. Workflows were never redesigned to match how AI actually operates.

The Stanford finding that reframes everything

One month before that report, Stanford published the Meta-Harness paper. The finding: the orchestration code wrapping an AI model — the harness — drives up to a sixfold performance difference using identical model weights. Same model. Different scaffolding. Six times the output gap.

Most teams are having a model selection conversation when they have an infrastructure problem.

Swapping Claude for GPT-4, or GPT-4 for o3, doesn't close a 6× gap that lives in how context is managed, how retrieval is structured, how the model is wired to your actual systems. That gap closes when the harness improves. The model is not the variable.

Shadow AI is a demand signal, not a security problem

The 2025 State of Shadow AI Report found the average enterprise hosts 1,200 unauthorized AI applications. Eighty-six percent of organizations have no visibility into their own AI data flows. Only 37% have policies to detect or manage unauthorized AI use.

This isn't rebellion. It's employees building workarounds because the official procurement path is six months behind their actual work. They've already integrated AI. Leadership just doesn't know how, where, or with what data. Meanwhile, 42% of companies scrapped most AI initiatives in 2025 — up from 17% in 2024.

The three patterns that show up every time

  • 01The model is getting blamed for an integration problem. Teams chase better models without fixing the context pipeline, retrieval architecture, or workflow fit. The model improves marginally. Nothing else does.
  • 02The governance doesn't match the actual footprint. Official inventory: eight tools. Real number: somewhere between fifty and four hundred. No policies, no visibility, no audit trail.
  • 03The pilots never had a production path. Built to demonstrate capability. They demonstrated capability. They never shipped. The MIT study found back-office automation produces the highest ROI — but more than half of enterprise AI budgets go to sales and marketing.

Fixing this starts with knowing what you actually have — what's deployed, what's connected to what data, what's running without oversight, and where the integration layer is broken. That's not a technology audit. It's an AI setup audit.
