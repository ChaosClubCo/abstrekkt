CHARACTER: PRE-INJURY KYLE

=== TOP FIELDS ===

Name: Pre-Injury Kyle

Roles: Ghost. The version of Kyle that existed before cognitive disruption. Present through absence and contrast.

Pronouns: He/him

Groups: The before

Other Names: Could appear as "the other Kyle," "who I was," or simply referenced in past tense without naming

=== DETAILED FIELDS ===

Personality:
This is the version of Kyle whose memory worked organically. Who didn't need 240 catalogued systems to function. Who could read a room at O'Toole's on instinct without documenting the pattern afterward. Competent in a way that felt natural rather than engineered. The hospitality years — managing chaos socially, bartending, reading drunk strangers — suggest someone extroverted, quick-processing, physically present. But also: the architecture was already under strain. Age 17, counting streetlights, thinking "If I don't find a way to quiet my head, it's going to tear me apart." Pre-Injury Kyle wasn't unbroken. He was unbroken in a different way.

Motivations:
Survival through social intelligence. Reading rooms to manage them. Finding frameworks that permitted what he needed (the permission pattern that would later become destructive). Pre-Injury Kyle was looking for silence — the same thing Post-Injury Kyle builds systems to achieve.

Internal Conflict:
The noise. Whatever was happening internally that drugs would later "solve" — the inner monologue, the self-monitoring, the thing that needed quieting. Pre-Injury Kyle was navigating this without external scaffolding and it was already failing. The late-night walk is evidence: the system was throwing warnings before the crash.

Strengths:
Organic memory. Social processing that didn't require documentation. The diagnostic instinct in its original, unassisted form — reading a bar, reading a room, reading people. Physical presence in work and social contexts. The kind of competence that comes from actually being in the room, not managing it from behind a screen.

Weaknesses:
No tools for the internal problem. The pattern-seeking brain that would later build 240 systems had no outlet and no framework. The noise had no engineering solution yet. Vulnerability to the permission framework — when someone offered a way to quiet the noise, the architecture that allowed it was already in place.

Character Arc:
Pre-Injury Kyle doesn't arc — he's frozen in time. He exists in the book as the person Kyle can't return to and shouldn't idealize. The question "What would pre-injury Kyle think of constructed Kyle?" is genuine, not rhetorical. The answer might be complicated: maybe pre-injury Kyle would be impressed by the systems. Maybe he'd be horrified that they were necessary. Maybe he wouldn't recognize himself at all.

Physical Description:
Younger. Bartender's physicality — on his feet, in motion, in the room. The contrast with the current desk-work body is implicit. Specific details TBD by Kyle's memory (which is unreliable on this subject).

Dialogue Style:
When quoted (if quoted), Pre-Injury Kyle speaks in the register of someone who hasn't yet learned to describe feelings with technical precision. Less measured. More reactive. The voice before it was rebuilt. Any quoted material from this era needs to feel unengineered — no metaphors about systems, no documentation language. Just the raw register of someone in the room.
