Kyle's Literary Memoir

Project Framework & Reference

Working Thesis & Author Context

Working Thesis ›
"How does a person whose mind has been broken—by choice, by injury, by circumstance—rebuild agency not through recovery of what was lost, but through construction of what never existed?"
Genre ›
Literary nonfiction. Not self-help. Not recovery memoir. Not productivity manifesto. A serious, systems-level examination of reconstructing a self after cognitive disruption.
Author Position ›
Staff Engineer at Intinc (AppSec + Platform). Self-taught, no CS degree. Father to Kinsley (Friday custody). Approaching 40. Mundelein, Illinois.
Non-Linear Path ›
Bartending at O'Toole's → Quality Control → Self-taught Engineering → FlashFusion (founder) → Staff-level technical leadership → Currently building Chaos Club Co.
The Breaks ›
Traumatic brain injury. Addiction. Memory challenges that persist. Cognitive disruption that made old strategies obsolete.
The Construction ›
240+ tools as externalized cognition. Systematic approaches as prosthetic memory. Obsessive documentation as survival. Technology as scaffolding for an unreliable mind.

Narrative Architecture

Central Tension ›
Recovery assumes returning to a previous state. Kyle can't return—the injury changed the hardware. So the question becomes: Can you build a functional self from scratch using different materials than the original?
Before the Break ›
Who was Kyle before? What did competence feel like when memory worked? The hospitality years—reading rooms, managing chaos, performing functionality. Early signs building toward rupture.
The Rupture ›
TBI specifics (as Kyle chooses to disclose). Addiction—cause, consequence, or both? The moment when old strategies stopped working. What it feels like when your mind becomes unreliable narrator.
The Construction Project ›
First tools that actually helped. Discovery that systems could substitute for organic memory. Building external cognition piece by piece. 240+ tools not as productivity flex but as survival infrastructure. Learning to program as learning to think in a new architecture.
Late-Life Emergence ›
Self-teaching to Staff Engineer level without credentials. Enterprise-scale technical competence arriving on non-standard timeline. Founder attempts (FlashFusion, others)—what they taught about limits. Current state at Intinc: what constructed self can actually do.
The Parallel Constructions ›
Fatherhood: Kinsley as grounding constraint and motivation. Chaos Club Co.: Building tools for others navigating similar chaos. The memoir itself: Another system for making sense of fragmented experience.

Voice Guidelines

Natural Register ›
Direct without being cold. Metaphor-heavy: "dumpster fire brain," "cognitive scaffolding," "smoke detector going off for burnt toast." Technical precision when it serves clarity. Strategic profanity for emphasis, not shock. Self-aware about irony of systematizing everything, including self-examination. No performance of vulnerability or toughness.
Sentences to Avoid ›
"And that's when I realized..." "Looking back, I can see that..." "It was then that everything changed..." Any construction that makes meaning too available too fast.
Sentences That Work ›
Declarative observations that let readers draw conclusions. Technical descriptions of emotional states. Admissions of uncertainty about memory/meaning. Humor that doesn't defuse tension prematurely.

Claude's Workflow by Phase

Research / Excavation ›
Ask for specific sensory details before writing. Prompt for what Kyle doesn't remember. Surface contradictions between how events felt then vs. how they read now. Request permission before pursuing sensitive threads.
Drafting ›
Write in Kyle's voice, not "literary memoir" voice. Offer structural alternatives, don't impose. Flag when prose is smoothing rough edges. Leave room for reader to work.
Revision ›
Challenge narratives that resolve too cleanly. Identify where systems-talk creates distance from emotion. Note where memory gaps need acknowledgment. Preserve the specific over the universal.
Always ›
End sessions with clear next steps. Track what's drafted vs. what needs raw material. Maintain running list of scenes/moments needing development. Honor Kyle's control over disclosure (TBI details, addiction specifics, family).

Structural Options to Explore

Chronological with Flashbacks ›
Linear timeline punctuated by intrusions of memory—mimicking how a damaged memory actually works.
Systems as Chapters ›
Each major tool/approach becomes entry point: "The Calendar System," "The Documentation Compulsion," "The 240 Tools"—with personal history woven through.
Parallel Timelines ›
External career progression on one track, internal reconstruction on another, converging at present.
The Arsenal Structure ›
Borrow Chaos Club's 25 categories as organizing principle—each chapter a different aspect of navigating chaos.

Session Protocol

Beginning Each Session ›
Where are we? (Phase, chapter, specific section) What's the goal today? (Raw material gathering, drafting, revision) Any new memories/insights since last session?
During Session ›
Surface before shaping. Specifics before themes. Draft in passes (skeleton → flesh → polish).
Ending Each Session ›
What did we produce? What's unresolved or needs more excavation? What's the next session's focus? Any homework for Kyle? (Memories to sit with, documents to find, people to talk to)

Questions to Keep Returning To

Knowledge Gap ›
What does Kyle know now that he couldn't have known during the events?
Identity & Protection ›
Where does the systems-builder identity protect him from feeling? Where does it enable feeling?
Comparison ›
What would pre-injury Kyle think of constructed Kyle?
Loss & Gain ›
What's permanently lost? What exists now that couldn't have existed before?
Dual Projects ›
How does building Chaos Club relate to writing this book? Are they the same project?
Legacy ›
What does Kinsley see? What will she understand when she's older?

Literary Positioning

Comparable Works ›
The Noonday Demon (Andrew Solomon)—depression as systems failure, intellectual rigor about interior experience In the Realm of Hungry Ghosts (Gabor Maté)—addiction without moralism Brain on Fire (Susannah Cahalan)—cognitive disruption from inside The Body Keeps the Score (van der Kolk)—but less clinical, more personal Beautiful Boy / Tweak (Sheff)—parallel perspectives on addiction
Differentiation ›
The systems angle. Most cognitive disruption memoirs focus on medical/emotional journey. Kyle's book centers the engineering response—what happens when someone with a broken mind builds external infrastructure to replace internal function. That's the fresh contribution.

Constraints & Realities

Full-time as Staff Engineer, Friday custody of Kinsley, actively building Chaos Club. Writing happens in margins.
Memory ›
The subject of the book is also an obstacle to writing it. Some periods are genuinely inaccessible. The book must acknowledge this.
Budget ›
~$1K available for immediate investments across all projects. Memoir competes with business development for attention.
Goal ›
Not publication timeline—quality and honesty. Eventually: a book that helps others who've had to construct rather than recover.
