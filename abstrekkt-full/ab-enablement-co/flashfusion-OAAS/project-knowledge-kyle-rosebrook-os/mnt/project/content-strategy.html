TL;DR

Two brands. Two jobs. FlashFusion sells. Chaos Collective builds the audience for Tax the Damage — anchored by Load-Bearing, the weekly AI newsletter for anyone trying to figure out what actually works in AI — no hype, no sales pitch, no jargon. The moat is the stress-test: your reviews and frameworks get stakes-tested in real work, not demo conditions. That's the differentiator nobody can copy by reading harder. Plan assumes 6–7 hrs/week. Below that, drop Load-Bearing to biweekly — don't dilute both brands by faking the cadence.

INT AI · Out of Scope

Why this isn't in the strategy

INT AI is internal product/adoption work, not external content marketing. Different audience (employees, not market), different channels (Teams/SharePoint, not LinkedIn), different metrics (token consumption, domain unlocks — not list growth). It runs on the int-ai-enablement-pm and int-data-analyst skills already.

If something built at INT becomes externally publishable, it surfaces under Load-Bearing, with no Intinc attribution. That's the only crossover path.

The Ops Layer

Two brands, one infrastructure. Without shared ops, even two brands fails.

Content Database

One source of truth. Notion or Airtable.

  • Tables: Ideas, Drafts, Scheduled, Published, Repurposed
  • Tags: brand, property, pillar, channel, status, source
  • Voice guardrails per brand pinned to each draft

Voice Guardrails

Two brands, two registers. Pinned at draft time.

  • FF: anti-hype, ROI-grounded, sales-coded but not sales-y
  • CC: memoir voice guide — no epiphanies, no recovery framing, declarative
  • Shared: Intinc never named, no inflated titles, stakes over hype

Repurposing Engine

One unit of input → multiple outputs. Voice-locked, never copy-pasted across brands.

  • 1 Load-Bearing issue → 2 social hooks + 1 essay seed
  • 1 Construction essay → 3 LinkedIn excerpts
  • 1 essay → 5 Threads/X fragments
  • CC → FF crossover only if systems-lead, never sales-lead

Measurement Cadence

Weekly review, monthly retro, quarterly KPI.

  • Friday 30-min: what shipped this week
  • Month-end 60-min: per-brand KPI scan
  • Quarterly: kill / keep / amplify decisions

PRESSURE TEST Where this could still break

  • Weekly is hard. A real Load-Bearing issue with your voice and stress-test depth is 2+ hours each. Miss two in a row and you've broken the contract with subscribers. Either commit fully or run biweekly with higher quality.
  • "Stress-tested" as a claim has to be real. The moat collapses fast if Load-Bearing starts running surface-level reviews. The standard is: every tool you write about has been used in real work, ideally past the point where its weaknesses surfaced. No demo-day theater.
  • Voice drift on convergence. Load-Bearing's job is AI through memoir voice — declarative, stakes-based, no hype. If issues start sounding like productivity newsletters, the moat is gone.
  • Memoir excerpts as content. Fragment-only rule is non-negotiable. The temptation to burn good scenes early is real.
  • FF still has to feed itself. Audience growth on CC doesn't pay rent. Don't sacrifice FF cadence for CC volume. The audit funnel is the revenue engine.
  • Chaos Collective ≠ ChaosClubCo ambiguity — until that's resolved, brand positioning has a load-bearing question mark. Resolve before Phase 1 launches.
