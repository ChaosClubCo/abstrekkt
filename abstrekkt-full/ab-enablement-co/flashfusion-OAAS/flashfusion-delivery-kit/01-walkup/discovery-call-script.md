# Discovery Call Script

**Duration:** 20–30 minutes
**Goal:** Understand the problem well enough to scope and price an engagement. Do not pitch until you've listened.
**Rule:** Talk less than 40% of the time.

---

## Before the call

- Know the business name, number of locations, and what they sell
- Look at their Facebook page if it exists — note: are they posting, are they boosting, do they have a pixel?
- Have the one-pager ready but do not send it before the call

---

## Opening (2 min)

> "Thanks for making time. I want to make sure I understand what's actually happening before I say anything about what I do — so I'm going to ask you a few questions. Sound good?"

Wait for yes. Then:

> "Tell me what's going on with your marketing right now. What's working, what isn't."

**Listen for:**
- What they've already tried (ads, agencies, boosted posts)
- How they measure success (or whether they don't)
- Frustration signals — "we tried X and it didn't work," "I don't have time," "I don't know what we're paying for"

---

## The 5 questions

Ask these in order. Let them talk. Don't interrupt. Write down exact words — those become your proposal copy.

**1. "Where do most of your new customers come from right now?"**

Listen for: referrals, walk-in, social, Google. If social is already working, it's easier to improve than to start from zero. If it's all referrals, the ask is harder.

**2. "What does a new customer look like for you — what do they actually buy first?"**

Listen for: the entry-point service or product. For a guitar shop, it might be a setup or a string change. This is what the ad should sell — not the business, the first transaction.

**3. "Have you run Facebook or Instagram ads before? What happened?"**

Listen for: "we tried it and it didn't work" = they have no idea how to measure it. "We ran it ourselves" = no strategy. "We used an agency" = they got burned and are skeptical. Each answer changes how you position.

**4. "Who on your team would be responsible for this day-to-day, if anyone?"**

Listen for: "just me" = they need the Build+train or Build+managed option, not Build+coach. "We have someone who does social" = that person is your training target, and you need them bought in. "Nobody" = Build+managed is the only option that works.

**5. "If this worked — if you had a system running ads and capturing leads automatically — what would that be worth to you?"**

Listen for: a number, or a reaction. If they can't answer, help them: "If you got 20 more repair customers a month, what does that look like in revenue?" Use their number in your proposal — it becomes the ROI anchor.

---

## Closing (3 min)

> "Okay — that's helpful. Based on what you've told me, I think I can help. Let me put something together for you. I'll send it over by [tomorrow/end of week]. One question before I do: is there anything that would make this a no-go before you even see a proposal?"

**Listen for:** budget constraints, timing issues, internal politics ("I have to check with my partner"). Surface blockers early.

> "Perfect. I'll send it over. If it looks good, we can get started within the week."

---

## After the call

Fill in these notes immediately:

- Problem in their words: ___
- Current marketing situation: ___
- Entry-point product/service: ___
- Who runs day-to-day: ___
- Estimated value of success: ___
- Blockers mentioned: ___
- Recommended tier: [ ] Build+train  [ ] Build+coach  [ ] Build+managed
- Benchmark metric to propose: ___
- Proposal send date: ___

---

## Red flags — do not proceed without resolving

- They want to own all the creative decisions but don't want to be involved in training
- Budget is below $1,500 total (below minimum viable engagement)
- They want guaranteed results without an agreed benchmark first
- They mentioned a bad agency experience but won't say what went wrong (hidden expectations)
- The person on the call isn't the decision-maker and can't get you access to one
