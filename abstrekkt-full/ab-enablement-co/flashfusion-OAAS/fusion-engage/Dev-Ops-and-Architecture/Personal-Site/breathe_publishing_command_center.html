These 5 questions determine which path gets the most effort. Answer them and the full plan locks in.

1

Traditional book deal, money now, or both?

This is the actual fork. Traditional deal = agent query + 18-24 month runway. Money now = Substack + course immediately. Both = parallel tracks with completely different daily task lists.

2

Self-help starter kit or literary memoir — which book are you writing?

Not a judgment. A routing question. The 187 pages that exist are self-help architecture. The story inside them is memoir material. These can be two separate products. The answer changes which agents, which comps, and which letter goes out today.

3

How much of the story goes public before a deal?

Serialization builds platform but puts material in public. Some agents want first publication rights. The TBI specifics, addiction details, Intinc situation — what goes public and when affects the book deal, the consulting business, the YC application, and Kinsley's ability to read it on her own terms.

4

3-5 hours per week to write, or margin-time only?

Full-time job. Co-founder conversations. Consulting launch. Kinsley on Fridays. The memoir competes for everything. Knowing the real weekly bandwidth determines whether the chosen path is achievable or just aspirational.

5

Is Chaos Club Co. in the book or separate from it?

This changes the comp titles, agent list, query framing, and publisher category. If Chaos Club is in the book — the business is an artifact of the story — then this is a founder memoir and the comp universe includes Shoe Dog and Lost and Founder. If Chaos Club is separate, it's a pure cognitive reconstruction memoir. Both publishable. Different books.
