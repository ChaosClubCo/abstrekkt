# Solo Founder AIaaS Strategy Report
**Version:** 1.0 | **Date:** May 2026
**Audience:** Founder, internal strategy reference

---

## Executive Summary

A solo founder building an AI consulting practice has one structural advantage over every boutique, MSP add-on, and SI trying to enter this market: zero committee latency. Decisions happen in minutes. Pivots happen in days. Client relationships are direct, not mediated through account managers.

The business model that fits a solo operator is not "replicate what a 10-person consultancy does at smaller scale." It is a productized service model: fixed-scope engagements, repeatable delivery playbooks, and a recurring retainer tier that generates predictable revenue without requiring proportional time investment. Custom everything destroys a solo practice. Productized delivery scales it.

The strategic thesis: **Sell fixed-scope discovery first. Convert the best-fit discoveries into deployments. Build a recurring retainer base. Never carry more active client load than the capacity ceiling allows.**

Year-one realistic target for a solo founder: **$180K–$280K** in AIaaS revenue. This assumes 50–60% of working time allocated to client delivery, with the remainder on business development and productization. A $400K+ run-rate is achievable by end of year two if retainer MRR compounds correctly — but it requires discipline on scope and client selection, not volume.

## 1. The Solo Founder Advantage

### What a Solo Founder Actually Has

The pitch that works for a solo AI consultant is not "I'm as good as a consulting firm." It is "you're talking directly to the person doing the work." That is not a consolation prize — for the midmarket buyer, it is often the preferred buying experience. The managing partner at a boutique firm runs the sales call; the junior analysts do the delivery. A solo founder does both. That is a feature when the buyer values accountability over headcount.

Structural advantages to lead with:

- **Speed**: No internal approvals, no project manager layer, no handoff friction. Discovery to proposal in 48 hours is normal.
- **Transparency**: The client knows exactly who is doing the work. No bait-and-switch after the sale.
- **Cost structure**: Near-zero overhead means competitive pricing without sacrificing margin. A boutique carrying 8 employees prices to cover salaries whether or not they are billable. A solo founder does not.
- **Bias toward action**: No consensus required. A solo founder can recommend the right platform even when it is not the firm's preferred vendor.
- **Platform agnosticism**: No reseller incentive. The recommendation is based on fit, not partnership tier.

### What a Solo Founder Does Not Have

Being honest about this matters because clients will probe it:

- **Bench depth**: If the founder is sick, traveling, or overloaded, delivery slips. There is no coverage model. Mitigation: productized delivery with documented playbooks so work can pause and resume cleanly, and honest scoping that prevents overcommitment.
- **Brand credibility at first contact**: A solo practice is a reputation business. The first 3–4 engagements are the proof of concept. Until there are reference clients, the brand is the founder's personal credibility.
- **Institutional continuity**: A solo practice cannot take on engagements where the client requires 24/7 coverage, multiple simultaneous workstreams, or an embedded team. These are not the right clients.

## 2. Market Position

### The Addressable Gap

Enterprise AI is owned by the hyperscalers and large SIs. SMB AI is largely self-served via Microsoft Copilot and off-the-shelf tools. The underserved segment is the **midmarket**: companies between 50–500 employees with real AI budgets ($30K–$150K/year range), real governance requirements, and no internal AI staff.

These companies are in a specific bind: they know they need to do something with AI, their procurement team will not approve a $300K SI engagement, and their IT department does not have the expertise. They need a delivery partner at a price point that fits a departmental budget, not an enterprise transformation budget.

A solo founder is price-competitive in this market. A boutique consultancy with 8 staff cannot profitably deliver a $12,000 discovery engagement. A solo founder can.

### Who the Right Client Is

Not every midmarket company is the right client for a solo AIaaS practice. The right client profile:

- **Has a named internal champion** — an operations lead, IT director, or department head who has already made the case internally that AI adoption needs to happen. The founder is not evangelizing; they are enabling someone who already wants to move.
- **Is not in a regulated vertical requiring deep compliance work on day one** — healthcare and financial services can be served, but they require more governance overhead than a solo practice should take on in year one without a defined compliance playbook.
- **Has a specific workflow problem, not just "AI curiosity"** — clients who can describe a concrete pain point convert. Clients who want to "explore AI" rarely do.
- **Has a realistic budget** — discovery engagements starting at $5,000–$9,500 should not require the client to go to board for approval. If the budget conversation requires three escalations, the company is the wrong size.

### What the Differentiation Is Not

Do not lead with technical depth. Most midmarket buyers cannot evaluate technical depth — they evaluate trustworthiness, clarity, and whether they believe the founder understands their business. Lead with workflow specificity: "I help operations teams eliminate the three specific manual processes that are wasting the most time." That is more compelling than "I have deep expertise in LLM architecture."

## 3. Service Model

The service model runs on three tiers. The fourth tier (AI Build) exists but is off-menu in year one — it is project-based custom work that is appropriate once the playbooks exist and the client relationship is proven.

### Tier 1 — AI Launchpad (Discovery & Prioritization)

**What it is:** A 2–3 week fixed-scope diagnostic. The output is a prioritized use-case backlog with ROI estimates, a platform recommendation, and a 90-day pilot plan.

**Why it works for solo delivery:** Launchpad is the highest-leverage engagement in the model. It does not require ongoing coordination or client team management. It is bounded in time, bounded in scope, and produces a concrete deliverable. It is the engagement a solo founder can run efficiently without risk of scope creep.

**Deliverables:**
- Workflow audit across 2–3 priority departments (stakeholder interviews + process mapping)
- Data readiness snapshot (what data exists, where it lives, what governance gaps block AI use)
- Prioritized use-case backlog (top 3–5 opportunities scored by ROI, risk, and implementation complexity)
- Platform recommendation with rationale
- 90-day pilot plan with defined acceptance criteria

**Capacity note:** A solo founder can run 2 concurrent Launchpad engagements. Three is the ceiling before quality degrades. Do not exceed it.

| Scope | Price |
|---|---|
| Single department | $5,500 |
| Two departments or cross-functional | $9,500 |
| Multi-department, regulated vertical | $14,000–$18,000 |

---

### Tier 2 — AI Deploy (Governed Deployment)

**What it is:** Implementation of 1–2 use cases identified in the Launchpad. Includes configuration, governance documentation, training, and adoption baseline setup.

**Why clients buy it:** They have the prioritized backlog from the Launchpad. The internal champion has sign-off. They need someone to deploy it inside their governance and technical constraints without relying on their IT team, which does not have the expertise.

**Deliverables:**
- Tenant and identity configuration (SSO, DLP where applicable, data classification)
- Deployment of selected AI tooling against defined use cases
- Governance baseline: acceptable use policy, data handling rules, escalation playbook
- Prompt library v1 specific to the deployed workflow
- Role-based training for impacted staff (async-first, not full-day workshops)
- Adoption metrics baseline
- Runbook for ongoing management

**Capacity note:** A solo founder should not carry more than one active AI Deploy engagement simultaneously. It requires client team coordination and staged delivery that cannot be parallelized cleanly.

| Scope | Price |
|---|---|
| Single use case, standard governance | $14,000–$22,000 |
| Single use case, regulated vertical | $24,000–$36,000 |
| Two use cases | $28,000–$45,000 |

---

### Tier 3 — AI Assure (Managed AI Retainer)

**What it is:** Monthly retainer covering governance monitoring, usage and cost review, prompt quality audit, policy updates, and new use-case intake.

**Why it matters for solo economics:** This is the tier that makes the business viable at scale. A solo founder cannot grow revenue indefinitely by adding more Launchpad and Deploy engagements — time is the constraint. AI Assure is the recurring revenue layer that compounds without proportional time investment. A client at month 6 of AI Assure takes significantly less active management than a client in their first Deploy engagement.

**Monthly deliverables:**
- Usage and cost report with anomaly flags
- Prompt quality review (sampled audit of deployed workflows)
- Policy compliance check against current regulatory posture
- New use-case intake and triage
- Async support for AI-related questions and incidents
- Quarterly business review with ROI measurement

**Capacity model:** A solo founder operating AI Assure efficiently (templated reporting, async-first communication, documented runbooks) can carry 8–12 AI Assure clients before the retainer workload requires structural support. At 6 clients averaging $3,500/month, that is $21,000 MRR from recurring work alone.

| Scope | Monthly Price |
|---|---|
| 1–2 deployed use cases, standard governance | $2,500–$3,500/month |
| 3–4 use cases or regulated vertical | $4,500–$6,500/month |
| Complex or multi-department deployment | $7,500–$10,000/month |

---

### Tier 4 — AI Build (Custom, Off-Menu in Year One)

Custom AI systems, agentic workflows, internal copilots, and integrations where off-the-shelf tooling is insufficient. Minimum engagement: $20,000. Only sold to clients who have completed AI Deploy and have a specific scoped need. This tier requires a defined SOW with acceptance criteria — it is not T&M.

Do not lead with AI Build. It is the highest-risk engagement for a solo founder (scope creep, extended delivery, technical dependency) and it requires the most trust to deliver well. It belongs in year two once the playbooks exist.

## 4. Capacity Model and Revenue Ceiling

The most important planning document for a solo AIaaS practice is not the revenue forecast — it is the capacity model. A solo founder has a fixed number of billable hours. Understanding where the ceiling is prevents overcommitment, which is the primary failure mode for solo consultants.

### Capacity Assumptions

- **Available delivery weeks per year:** 44 (excluding vacation, conference, admin, and business development time)
- **Delivery hours per week (50% of working time):** ~20 hours
- **Remaining 50%:** business development, productization, content, operations

### Time Per Engagement Type

| Engagement | Estimated Founder Hours |
|---|---|
| AI Launchpad (single dept) | 25–35 hours |
| AI Launchpad (multi-dept) | 40–60 hours |
| AI Deploy (single use case) | 60–90 hours |
| AI Assure (per client per month) | 3–6 hours |

### Year-One Realistic Mix

| Service | Volume | Avg. Hours | Total Hours | Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI Launchpad | 8 | 35 | 280 | $68,000 |
| AI Deploy | 3 | 75 | 225 | $54,000 |
| AI Assure (building to 6 clients) | 6 clients avg 4 mo | 5 hrs/mo | 120 | $72,000 |
| **Total** | | | **625 hrs** | **~$194,000** |

625 hours against 880 available delivery hours (44 weeks × 20 hrs) = 71% utilization. This is the right ceiling — it leaves 30% buffer for unexpected scope, business development during delivery weeks, and the reality that estimates are always low.

### Year-Two Realistic Mix (Retainer Base Compounds)

If 6 AI Assure clients from year one continue and 4 new ones are added, retainer MRR alone reaches $35,000–$50,000/month by end of year two. Add 6–8 Launchpads and 2–3 Deploys and total revenue reaches $350K–$450K with the same solo capacity model.

The $400K+ threshold is achievable solo **only if** AI Assure delivery is productized: templated reports, async-first communication cadence, defined scope per tier. If AI Assure becomes custom for every client, the capacity model breaks.

## 5. Go-To-Market Sequence

### Phase 1 (Now → 90 days): First Three Clients

The only goal of Phase 1 is three completed Launchpad engagements. Not ten. Not a full pipeline. Three, done well, with documented outcomes.

Where to find them:
- **Warm network first**: Former colleagues, current contacts, anyone who has mentioned AI frustration in the last 6 months. A Launchpad at $5,500 is a low-friction "yes" for someone who already trusts the founder.
- **LinkedIn content as signal**: Publishing specific content about workflow-level AI problems (not generic AI takes) attracts the internal champions who are already looking for a partner. This is a 60–90 day play, not a 10-day play.
- **Direct outreach to second-degree network**: Not cold outreach. Targeted warm intros through mutual contacts to operations leaders at midmarket companies.

Phase 1 success criteria: 3 Launchpads invoiced, at least 1 converting to AI Deploy conversation.

### Phase 2 (Month 4–9): Reference Case and Retainer Base

With 3 Launchpads complete and 1 Deploy in progress, the practice has enough material for a reference case. This is the unlock for credibility at a distance — people who do not know the founder can now evaluate the work.

- Build 1–2 case studies (anonymized or named with permission) documenting: the problem, the approach, the outcome, the measurable result.
- Publish them. Not buried on a portfolio page — LinkedIn posts, short articles, email list if one exists.
- Begin converting Deploy clients to AI Assure. The retention conversation happens naturally at Deploy close: "Here is what ongoing management looks like."

Phase 2 success criteria: $15,000+ in AI Assure MRR, 1 named or anonymized reference case live.

### Phase 3 (Month 10–18): Productization and Referral

At $15K–$20K MRR from AI Assure plus ongoing project revenue, the business is financially stable. Phase 3 is about making it less dependent on the founder's active business development:

- Build a referral structure: existing clients referring peer contacts is the highest-conversion channel for a solo practice
- Productize the Launchpad deliverable so it can be delivered with less custom configuration per engagement — standardized templates, reusable frameworks, defined interview protocols
- Evaluate whether AI Build capacity requires a subcontractor relationship rather than solo delivery

## 6. Pricing Philosophy

**Do not underprice to win.** A solo founder who underprices signals one of two things to the buyer: inexperience, or desperation. Neither helps close the right client.

The right clients for a solo AIaaS practice are buying expertise and accountability, not hours. Price to expertise, not to time. A Launchpad at $9,500 takes 35 hours — that is a $271/hour effective rate. That is appropriate for senior AI consulting. A Launchpad at $4,000 is $114/hour. That is entry-level IT support pricing, and it attracts clients who think of the engagement as IT support.

**The anchor is the outcome, not the effort.** A client who implements the Launchpad recommendations and saves 15 hours/week of manual work across a department of 10 people at $40/hour loaded cost is looking at $312,000/year in recoverable time. The Launchpad costs $9,500. The ROI conversation writes itself — but only if the founder prices to signal that the outcome is worth buying.

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Severity | What to Do About It |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity overcommitment | High | Enforce the capacity model. Define the ceiling before the pipeline fills. Saying no to a bad-fit client is a skill, not a failure. |
| Single point of failure (illness, personal emergency) | High | Documented runbooks for every active engagement. Clear contract language on delivery pause terms. No client should be dependent on daily availability. |
| Retainer scope creep | High | AI Assure scope must be documented in the SOW with explicit out-of-scope language. "Quick questions" that become half-day engagements break the capacity model. |
| First client delivers poor outcome | High | This is the existential risk in year one. Solve it by being ruthlessly selective about first clients — choose ones where success is likely, not ones that maximize revenue. |
| Microsoft Copilot self-service replaces the need | Medium | The clients who can self-serve Copilot are not the target. The target needs governance, workflow specificity, and implementation support that Copilot licensing alone does not provide. |
| Pricing compression from AI tools reducing delivery time | Medium | As AI tooling improves delivery efficiency, the temptation is to lower prices. Resist it. The value is the outcome, not the hours. |
| Personal brand dependency | Medium | The practice is the founder. If the founder stops publishing, speaking, and being visible, pipeline stalls. Build content habits before the pipeline runs dry. |

## 8. What Is Not in This Report

Required before the practice is client-ready:

1. **SOW templates for each tier** — before the first Launchpad closes, there needs to be a signed-agreement template with scope, deliverables, payment terms, and pause/cancellation clause
2. **AI Assure monthly report template** — the recurring deliverable needs to be designed once and reused; custom reporting per client is not scalable
3. **Launchpad interview protocol and output template** — the 2–3 week engagement needs a defined process, not a blank canvas each time
4. **Case study template** — at least one anonymized reference case ready before Phase 2 outreach
5. **Pricing and proposal system** — a fast way to generate scoped proposals without starting from scratch each time

## GAPS / BLINDSPOTS / UNKNOWN-UNKNOWNS

- Network size and warmth: the Phase 1 "warm network" strategy depends entirely on the founder having 20–30 relevant contacts who trust them enough to have a conversation — this is not guaranteed
- Close rate on Launchpad outreach: the revenue model assumes a close rate that is not validated; if it takes 10 conversations to close 1 Launchpad, the BD time allocation needs to double
- Whether the target buyer segment (operations leaders at 50–500 person midmarket companies) actually has budget discretion for a $9,500 engagement without procurement involvement — this varies significantly by company
- Subcontractor availability for AI Build overflow: not evaluated; needed before AI Build goes on-menu
- Founder's effective delivery rate once AI tooling is incorporated into Launchpad delivery — could materially reduce hours-per-engagement, which changes both the capacity ceiling and the pricing strategy
