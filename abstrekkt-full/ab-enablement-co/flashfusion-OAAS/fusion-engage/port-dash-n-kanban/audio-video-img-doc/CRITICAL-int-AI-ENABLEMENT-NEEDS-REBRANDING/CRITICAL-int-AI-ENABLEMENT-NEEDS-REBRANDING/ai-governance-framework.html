🛡️ AI Governance Framework

Enterprise policies, data classification, and risk management for AI deployments

📋 Version 2.0 📅 Effective: January 2025 👤 Owner: IT Security / AI Center of Excellence 🔄 Review: Quarterly

📋 Executive Summary

This AI Governance Framework establishes the policies, procedures, and controls for the responsible use of artificial intelligence technologies across the organization. It ensures AI deployments align with business objectives while managing risks related to security, privacy, ethics, and regulatory compliance.

🎯 Framework Objectives

  • Enable Innovation: Provide clear guidelines that empower teams to adopt AI responsibly
  • Manage Risk: Identify, assess, and mitigate risks associated with AI use
  • Ensure Compliance: Meet regulatory requirements (GDPR, HIPAA, SOC2, etc.)
  • Protect Data: Safeguard sensitive information in AI workflows
  • Build Trust: Establish accountability and transparency in AI operations

📜 Guiding Principles

1. Human-Centered AI ▼

AI systems augment human decision-making; they do not replace human judgment for consequential decisions. All AI outputs require human review before external communication or significant business impact.

2. Transparency & Explainability ▼

When AI is used to generate content or make recommendations, this must be disclosed to affected parties. Teams must be able to explain how AI-assisted decisions were made.

3. Privacy by Design ▼

Personal and sensitive data is minimized, anonymized, or excluded from AI processing wherever possible. Data retention is limited to the minimum necessary for the intended purpose.

4. Security First ▼

All AI tools must meet minimum security standards before deployment. Sensitive data is only processed by approved enterprise-grade platforms with appropriate certifications.

5. Continuous Improvement ▼

This framework evolves with technology and regulatory changes. Feedback mechanisms enable continuous improvement of policies and practices.

🏷️ Data Classification Levels

All data processed by AI systems must be classified according to sensitivity level. Classification determines which AI tools can be used.

PUBLIC
Information intended for public release. No restrictions on AI processing.
INTERNAL
Business information not for external sharing. Standard AI tools permitted.
CONFIDENTIAL
Sensitive business data requiring protection. Enterprise AI tools only.
RESTRICTED
Highly sensitive data (PII, PHI, financial). Approved private AI only.

⚠️ Prohibited Data for AI Processing

The following data types must NEVER be submitted to AI systems:

  • Passwords, API keys, secrets, or authentication tokens
  • Social Security Numbers, government IDs
  • Credit card numbers, bank account details
  • Raw PHI without explicit HIPAA-compliant platform approval
  • Classified government information
  • Child data (users under 13)

Risk Categories

🔒 Data Security Risks ▼
  • Data Leakage: Sensitive data exposed to AI vendors or third parties
  • Prompt Injection: Malicious inputs manipulating AI behavior
  • Model Extraction: Proprietary training data or logic exposed
  • Access Control: Unauthorized users accessing AI-generated insights
⚖️ Compliance Risks ▼
  • GDPR Violations: Processing EU personal data without proper basis
  • HIPAA Breaches: PHI processed by non-compliant systems
  • Copyright Infringement: AI generating content that infringes IP
  • Audit Failures: Inability to demonstrate AI governance controls
🎭 Ethical Risks ▼
  • Bias & Discrimination: AI outputs reflecting or amplifying biases
  • Misinformation: AI generating false or misleading content
  • Lack of Transparency: Stakeholders unaware of AI involvement
  • Job Displacement: AI replacing human roles without proper transition
💼 Operational Risks ▼
  • Vendor Dependency: Over-reliance on single AI provider
  • Quality Degradation: AI outputs declining over time
  • Cost Overruns: Unexpected API costs or licensing fees
  • Service Disruption: AI system outages impacting operations

📖 Acceptable Use Policy

✅ Permitted Uses

  • Drafting and editing documents, emails, and presentations
  • Summarizing and analyzing public or internal information
  • Generating code with appropriate review before production
  • Research and competitive intelligence (public sources)
  • Brainstorming and ideation
  • Translation and localization
  • Data analysis (with proper classification)

🚫 Prohibited Uses

  • Submitting passwords, API keys, or secrets
  • Processing customer PII without approved enterprise tools
  • Making autonomous decisions affecting customers or employees
  • Generating content for external use without human review
  • Using AI outputs as sole basis for legal, medical, or financial advice
  • Circumventing security controls or content policies
  • Personal use during work hours (beyond incidental)

⚠️ Human Review Required

All AI-generated content intended for external audiences (customers, partners, public) MUST be reviewed by a qualified human before distribution. This includes:

  • Customer communications
  • Marketing materials
  • Legal documents
  • Financial reports
  • Press releases

💡 Prompt Engineering Guidelines

Best Practices

  1. Be Specific: Clear, detailed prompts yield better results
  2. Provide Context: Include relevant background information
  3. Define Format: Specify desired output structure
  4. Iterate: Refine prompts based on outputs
  5. Verify: Always fact-check AI-generated claims

Security in Prompts

🔐 Prompt Security Rules

  • Never include real names of customers, employees, or partners
  • Use placeholder data: "[CUSTOMER NAME]" instead of actual names
  • Avoid including account numbers, IDs, or identifiers
  • Strip sensitive data before pasting documents
  • Use synthetic or anonymized datasets for testing

🔒 Technical Security Controls

5-Layer Defense Model

Layer Control Implementation Owner
1 Input Validation Sanitize inputs, check length, block injection patterns Development
2 Prompt Structure Separate system prompts from user data Development
3 Output Filtering PII detection, content safety checks Security
4 Monitoring Anomaly detection, audit logs, usage alerts Security
5 Human Oversight Approval workflows for high-risk operations Business

Authentication & Access

  • All AI tools require SSO authentication via corporate identity provider
  • API access requires approved service accounts with rotate secrets
  • Access logs retained for 90 days minimum
  • Quarterly access reviews for all AI tool permissions

Data Protection

  • TLS 1.3 required for all AI API communications
  • Data encrypted at rest using AES-256
  • No data retention by AI vendors (enterprise agreements)
  • Geographic restrictions for data processing (US/EU only)

⚖️ Regulatory Compliance

GDPR Requirements

  • Lawful basis documented for all AI processing of personal data
  • Privacy notices updated to disclose AI use
  • Data subject rights honored (access, deletion, portability)
  • DPIA completed for high-risk AI processing
  • Vendor DPAs in place with AI providers

HIPAA Requirements

  • BAAs executed with all AI vendors processing PHI
  • PHI only processed by HIPAA-compliant platforms
  • Minimum necessary principle enforced
  • Audit logs maintained for 6 years
  • Workforce training on PHI + AI completed

SOC2 Requirements

  • AI vendors hold current SOC2 Type II reports
  • Change management process for AI deployments
  • Risk assessment includes AI-specific threats
  • Incident response plan covers AI breaches

🚨 AI Incident Response

Incident Categories

Severity Examples Response Time Escalation
P0 - Critical PHI/PII exposed, credential leak, active breach Immediate CISO, Legal, Executive
P1 - High Confidential data exposure, compliance violation < 1 hour Security + AI CoE
P2 - Medium Policy violation, unauthorized tool use < 4 hours AI CoE Lead
P3 - Low Quality issue, minor policy deviation < 24 hours Department Manager

Response Procedure

  1. Detect: Identify incident through monitoring, user report, or audit
  2. Contain: Isolate affected systems, revoke access if needed
  3. Assess: Determine scope, data affected, root cause
  4. Notify: Inform stakeholders per escalation matrix
  5. Remediate: Fix vulnerability, restore services
  6. Report: Document incident, regulatory notifications if required
  7. Review: Post-incident review, update controls
