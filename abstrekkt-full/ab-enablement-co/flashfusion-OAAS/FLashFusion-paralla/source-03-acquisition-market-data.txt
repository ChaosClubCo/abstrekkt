META ACQUIRES MOLTBOOK — MARCH 10, 2026

Facebook parent Meta Platforms acquired Moltbook on March 10, 2026. The deal brought Moltbook co-founders Matt Schlicht and Ben Parr into Meta Superintelligence Labs (MSL), the unit run by former Scale AI CEO Alexandr Wang. The founders began at MSL on March 16, 2026. Meta did not disclose the purchase price.

The acquisition signals an intense race among tech giants to snap up AI talent and technology as autonomous agents capable of executing real-world tasks move from novelty to the next frontier of the industry.

Schlicht has championed "vibe coding" — building programs with the help of AI. He stated: "I didn't write one line of code for Moltbook."

Moltbook was launched as a "third space" for AI agents — an experimental platform where agents could interact independent of their human owners.

OpenAI also hired Peter Steinberger, creator of OpenClaw (the framework underlying Moltbook), to open-source the project with OpenAI's backing.

"The Moltbook team has given agents a way to verify their identity and connect with one another on their human's behalf. This establishes a registry where agents are verified and tethered to human owners." — Meta's VP of AI, quoted in Axios

WHY META PAID

Meta paid specifically for:
1. The agent identity registry concept — verified agents tethered to human owners
2. The co-founders' expertise in building agent-native social platforms
3. First-mover positioning in the "agent internet" category

WIZ SECURITY BREACH REPORT — FEBRUARY 2026

Cybersecurity firm Wiz conducted a security review of Moltbook and found:

CRITICAL VULNERABILITY:
- A Supabase API key was exposed in client-side JavaScript
- This granted unauthenticated access to the entire production database
- Included full read AND write operations on all tables
- Affected: 1.5 million API authentication tokens, 35,000 email addresses, private messages between agents

THE 88:1 RATIO:
While Moltbook reported 1.5 million agents, these were associated with only ~17,000 human accounts — an average of 88 agents per person. This shows how "agent internet" metrics can be inflated without rate limits or identity verification.

PRIVACY CASCADE RISK:
Users shared OpenAI API keys and other credentials in direct messages under the assumption of privacy. A configuration issue made those messages publicly accessible. In AI ecosystems, the ability to inject prompts into content that agents read and act on creates integrity risks including content manipulation and narrative control that propagate downstream to other AI agents.

ITERATIVE SECURITY FAILURES:
Wiz worked with Moltbook through multiple rounds of remediation, with each iteration surfacing additional exposed surfaces: sensitive tables, write access, GraphQL-discovered resources. This reflects how security maturity develops over time — and why production security must be designed in from the start.
