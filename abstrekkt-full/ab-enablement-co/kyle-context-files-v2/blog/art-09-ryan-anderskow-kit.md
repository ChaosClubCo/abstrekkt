## 2. Discovery call prep doc

**Goal of the call:** Determine if there's a real fit. Decide whether to send the proposal. Should run 20 minutes max.

### Structure (20 min)

| Time | Section |
|------|---------|
| 0:00–0:02 | Personal catchup — keep it light, this is a friend |
| 0:02–0:05 | Reframe: "I want to make sure I'm not pitching you on something that doesn't fit. Can I ask 5 questions to figure that out?" |
| 0:05–0:18 | The 5 diagnostic questions (below) |
| 0:18–0:20 | "Based on what you said, here's what I'd recommend." Either: send proposal / not the right fit right now / let me think and circle back |

### The 5 diagnostic questions

1. **What AI tools do you have currently deployed in your work?** *(Listening for: anything beyond ChatGPT-as-an-individual? Any team-level deployment? Any integration with internal systems?)*

2. **What's the gap between what you thought AI would do and what it's actually doing?** *(Listening for: specific friction, not abstract dissatisfaction. Generic complaints = bad fit. Specific failures = good fit.)*

3. **If I solved one specific AI-related problem for you in the next month, what would have the biggest leverage on your work?** *(Listening for: a clear answer. If they need to think about it for >30 seconds, the engagement is premature.)*

4. **Who else is involved in decisions about AI tooling at your company?** *(Listening for: stakeholders. If Ryan can't say yes by himself, the proposal needs to address that.)*

5. **If we did this and it didn't work — what would "didn't work" look like to you?** *(Listening for: their actual success criteria. If they can't articulate failure, they can't recognize success.)*

### Outcomes (decide on the call, don't promise to "follow up")

- **Strong fit** → "I'm going to send you a proposal by end of day. It'll have everything we just talked about, fixed scope, fixed fee. Take a few days, ask whatever questions, and reply by [date]."

- **Weak fit but worth more conversation** → "I think there might be something here but it's not a fit for an Audit specifically. Let me think about what would be useful and I'll send you something within a week."

- **No fit right now** → "Honestly, based on what you said, I don't think you need what I'm selling right now. Here's what I'd watch for that would change that — [signal]." (No fake follow-ups. Close cleanly.)

## 4. Case-study trade arrangement

The discount-for-permission pattern is the only way to honor the personal relationship without breaking the price floor for future customers.

**The math:**
- Standard Audit price: $4,500
- Ryan's price: $3,000
- Implicit value of the case study: $1,500

**What this means for future positioning:**
- Public price stays $4,500
- Ryan engagement is described internally and externally as "case-study-trade pricing"
- Future customers who ask "can I get the friend price?" hear: "the only discounted pricing is the case-study trade. If you want to be a public case study, we can talk about that. Otherwise the price is $4,500."

**What goes in the case study:**
- Problem (Ryan's situation pre-engagement)
- What FlashFusion did
- What changed
- Quote from Ryan (with explicit permission)
- Methodology callout if there was a novel approach
- Pricing context: "engaged via FlashFusion's Audit tier with a case-study trade arrangement" — fully transparent

## 5. After the engagement (regardless of outcome)

Whether Ryan says yes, no, or maybe — capture what was learned:

**If yes:**
- Note the questions he asked during discovery (for the discovery script v2)
- Note any objections and how you handled them (for the objection handling guide)
- Note time-to-close, time-to-deposit, time-to-delivery (for process metrics)
- Update the proposal template if anything you wrote was unclear

**If no:**
- Note the specific reason (price? timing? unclear value? wrong shape entirely?)
- Note whether the no is a "not now" or "not ever" — they're different signals
- Keep the relationship warm. First nos can become later yeses.

**If maybe / dragging:**
- Set a hard date for follow-up — once.
- After one follow-up with no decision, treat as a no and stop chasing.

## Risk patterns for this specific outreach

**Risk: Personal relationship gets weird around money.** Symptom: Ryan feels obligated to say yes, or feels weird saying no. Mitigation: explicitly name the dynamic in the email — "I'd rather it be someone whose work I respect than a stranger" — and make the no easy to say.

**Risk: Discount pricing leaks to other prospects.** Symptom: someone hears about the $3,000 Ryan price and asks for the same. Mitigation: hold the position above. The $3,000 price exists *only* with the case-study trade. Without that trade, it's $4,500.

**Risk: Audit reveals nothing actionable.** Symptom: the readout is generic. Mitigation: invoke the money-back clause unprompted. Don't make Ryan ask for it.

**Risk: Ryan says yes but ghosts on scheduling.** Symptom: signed agreement, no calendar movement. Mitigation: 7-day cadence on follow-up, three attempts. After three, refund the deposit and close cleanly.

---

## Final note on this engagement specifically

This is the first paid audit. The most important thing about it isn't the money. It's that the process from outreach → discovery → proposal → delivery → readout → case study runs end-to-end at least once. Everything Phase 3 depends on (process documentation, repeatability, hand-off readiness) builds on this single transaction working.

Ship it. Don't pre-optimize. The first one is supposed to be rough.
