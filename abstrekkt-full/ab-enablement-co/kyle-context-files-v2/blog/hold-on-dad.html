Hold On, Dad

A letter from the future. From the kid who's still here because you stayed.

This isn't a recovery story. It isn't a redemption arc. It's a song about the lie that says you should disappear — and the voice from the other side of that lie that says no, stay.

What this song is about

Because "just breathe" doesn't f*cking cut it.

A child, grown, writes back to a parent drowning in shame — telling him what he couldn't hear in the darkness: your presence was never a burden. Your survival was the promise I needed.

It cuts through the isolation of depression and suicidal ideation by asking the hardest question: "What if the person you think you're sparing is actually waiting for you?"

It rejects the lie that perfection, sobriety, or some heroic conquest is the price of love. What mattered was showing up broken. Making mistakes. Breathing through chaos. That was the whole thing.

For anyone who's...
  • Felt like a burden to the people they love
  • Believed leaving would somehow ease the pain for others
  • Carried shame that whispers you're not enough
  • Wondered if survival was worth it
  • Spent years pretending to be okay so other people wouldn't worry

Why this matters to me

No masks required. No perfection expected.

I wrote this because I know what it feels like when the voice in your head gets so loud that leaving seems like the kindest thing you could do — and I need you to know that voice is lying.

I'm a father. I build things for a living. I learned how to architect systems other people trust before I learned how to be honest with myself. I thought I had to be perfect to deserve the people I love — until my daughter showed me that my broken, trying, real self was exactly what she needed.

If you're reading this and you believe everyone would be better off without you, I'm telling you directly: you're wrong. There's someone — maybe someone you don't even know about yet — whose entire future depends on you staying.

I'm putting this out because shame dies in silence. Your presence isn't a burden. It's a promise. And showing up at all is the whole thing.
