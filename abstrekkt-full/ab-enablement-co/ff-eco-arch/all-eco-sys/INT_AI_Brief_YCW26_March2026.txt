1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================
Every few months, Y Combinator's latest cohort functions as a forward indicator
— not just of what founders are building, but of what enterprise technology
infrastructure will look like in 24–36 months. The W26 batch contains a clear,
unified signal: the next infrastructure layer is not more AI applications. It is
the operating substrate that autonomous AI agents need to run reliably inside
businesses.

Three structural shifts visible in this batch that carry direct implications
for INT:

  1. Agents are becoming operational employees, not tools. Companies like Tensol
     deploy AI with email addresses, phone numbers, and permissions across 100+
     enterprise systems. This is not automation — it is a new class of worker.

  2. Agent reliability infrastructure is a first-class problem. Moda (agent
     monitoring), Compresr (context optimization), and Polymath (agent
     simulation) exist because production agents fail in non-obvious ways. No
     observability tooling currently exists for these systems in most enterprises.

  3. Regulated industries are next. Fenrock AI (banking compliance), Corelayer
     (production operations), and Hex (cybersecurity) signal that AI automation
     is moving from productivity tools into sectors where accuracy and
     auditability are non-negotiable.

2. HIGH-SIGNAL COMPANIES — INT RELEVANCE ANALYSIS
================================================================================
The following three companies from the W26 batch carry the highest direct
relevance to INT's service lines, client conversations, and platform strategy.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Corelayer — AI On-Call Engineers for Production Systems
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Founders:    Mitch Radhuber, Shipra Jhahirani
  Origin:      Goldman Sachs petabyte-scale data infrastructure
  Core problem: 90% of 5am production incidents had a simple fix — but required
               human escalation
  What they built: AI agents that act as on-call engineers: diagnose, resolve,
               and document common production incidents automatically
  INT relevance: Directly maps to service desk L1/L2 automation opportunity

The Corelayer founding story is directly transferable to INT's service desk
context. Jhahirani and Radhuber built petabyte-scale infrastructure at Goldman
Sachs, where they repeatedly encountered the same pattern: critical-sounding
alerts at inconvenient hours that resolved in minutes once a human looked.

  BEST PRACTICE: Use Corelayer's founding framing when pitching AI service desk
  automation internally: "What percentage of our Tier 1 tickets have a
  resolution that could be documented in 5 steps or fewer?" If the answer is
  above 40%, the automation case is already made.

The relevance to INT is not in adopting Corelayer's product — it is in the
pattern they validated. A YC-backed team with Goldman Sachs credentials chose
to build this because the market signal was undeniable. INT's service desk
handles analogous patterns at a smaller scale with the same structural
opportunity.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 Moda — Monitoring and Debugging for AI Agents
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Founders:    Mohammed Al-Rasheed, Pranav Bedi
  Comparable to: Sentry (error tracking) — but designed for autonomous AI agents
  Core problem: When an AI agent makes a mistake — moving money, updating
               systems, answering customers — understanding why it failed is
               difficult without dedicated tooling
  What they built: Observability and debugging infrastructure specific to
               agentic workflows
  INT relevance: Any agentic workflow INT deploys will need this class of
               tooling before it is production-safe

  RISK FLAG: Deploying agentic workflows — whether in service desk automation,
  client-facing AI, or internal operations — without observability tooling
  creates liability exposure. If an agent takes a wrong action (closes the
  wrong ticket, sends incorrect information to a client, modifies a record
  erroneously), the absence of logs and audit trails makes resolution and
  remediation extremely difficult. This must be a first-class requirement,
  not an afterthought.

The existence of Moda as a YC-funded company confirms what AppSec practitioners
already know: the absence of observability in AI systems is a recognized,
fundable problem. INT should treat agent observability as a non-negotiable
deployment prerequisite — not a roadmap item.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3 Tensol — Deploying AI Employees Inside Companies
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Founders:    Oliviero Pinotti, Pratik Satija
  What they built: Platform for deploying AI agents as persistent employees with
               email, phone, computer access, and permissions across 100+
               enterprise tools
  Onboarding model: AI agents onboard like real hires — with provisioned access,
               tool integrations, and defined scope
  INT relevance: Near-term threat model for MSP business AND strategic anchor
               for INT's platform positioning

Tensol represents the upper bound of what AI agent deployment looks like at
scale. The significance for INT is twofold: it is simultaneously a competitive
threat to traditional MSP service delivery models and a strategic anchor for
positioning INT's platform ahead of client awareness.

  STRATEGIC USE: Present Tensol to leadership not as a vendor recommendation
  but as a market ceiling illustration. When a client asks "where is AI going?"
  this is the answer. INT's role is to be the trusted guide that helps clients
  navigate to that future responsibly — not to be replaced by it.

3. THE STRUCTURAL SIGNAL — WHAT THIS BATCH MEANS
================================================================================

3.1 The Platform Shift Thesis
-------------------------------
The previous generation of AI startups built applications for human users —
better email, faster writing, smarter search. The W26 batch is building
infrastructure for AI agents as users. The shift is analogous to the difference
between building websites and building the servers, CDNs, and databases that
websites run on.

  Layer            Previous Wave (2022–2024)        Emerging Wave (2025+)
  ───────────────  ───────────────────────────────  ─────────────────────────────
  Interface        Chat UI, copilots, assistants    Agent-to-agent APIs
  Users            Human employees                  Autonomous AI agents
  Infrastructure   Prompt engineering, RAG          Agent monitoring, simulation
  Reliability      Human in the loop                Automated observability
  Enterprise risk  Data privacy, accuracy           Auditability, blast radius

3.2 The 12–18 Month Window
----------------------------
INT's enterprise and SMB clients are approximately 12–18 months behind the YC
signal curve. They are currently adopting Tier 1 AI tools — copilots, meeting
summarizers, document assistants. Within 18 months, their SaaS vendors will
begin shipping agentic capabilities into products they already use. Clients will
not have consciously decided to adopt AI agents. The agents will arrive inside
their existing tools.

  THREAT MODEL: The risk is not that clients adopt agentic systems and exclude
  INT from the conversation. The risk is that agentic capabilities ship inside
  existing SaaS platforms (Salesforce, ServiceNow, Microsoft 365) without anyone
  at the client performing a security or compliance review — because no one
  flagged it as a deployment requiring review. INT needs a proactive advisory
  posture, not a reactive support posture, on this class of change.

3.3 INT's Positioning Opportunity
------------------------------------
MSPs that understand agent infrastructure before their clients will be positioned
to offer three new service lines that do not exist in most MSP catalogs today:

  1. Agent Deployment Readiness Assessments — Does the client's security
     posture, data hygiene, and tool stack support safe agentic operations?

  2. Agent Observability & Governance — Ongoing monitoring, audit trail
     management, and incident response for AI agent actions.

  3. Agent Onboarding & Permission Modeling — Defining what a deployed AI agent
     is allowed to access, modify, and act on — analogous to new-hire identity
     provisioning.

  POSITIONING STATEMENT: INT is not just an IT provider. We are the
  infrastructure team for the AI-native enterprise. Clients who trust us with
  their endpoints, identity, and cloud infrastructure will need the same trusted
  partner to manage their agent infrastructure. That positioning must be
  established before the demand arrives.

Appendix B: Glossary — Agent Infrastructure Terms
---------------------------------------------------
  AI Agent
    An autonomous software system that perceives inputs, makes decisions, and
    takes actions — including calling APIs, modifying data, and interacting with
    other systems — without requiring a human to approve each step.

  Agent Observability
    Logging, monitoring, and audit trail tooling designed specifically for AI
    agent actions. Analogous to Sentry for applications, but captures agent
    decisions and their consequences.

  Context Window
    The amount of information an AI model can process in a single operation.
    Larger context = higher cost + potential for model confusion. Context
    optimization (Compresr) manages this.

  Blast Radius
    The scope of potential damage if an AI agent takes an incorrect or
    unauthorized action. Defining blast radius is a prerequisite for safe agent
    deployment.

  Agent Permission Model
    The defined set of systems, data, and actions an AI agent is authorized to
    access and perform. Analogous to role-based access control (RBAC) for human
    employees.

  Agent Simulation
    Running an AI agent in a controlled test environment (Polymath) before
    deploying to production. Equivalent to staging environments in software
    development.

  MCP (Model Context Protocol)
    An open standard for connecting AI models to external tools and data sources.
    Enables agents to use real-world systems as extensions of their capabilities.
