```

My mind was broken by a combination of choices, injury, and circumstance. The standard

built something new: 240+ tools and systems, documented meticulously, that became 
prosthetic memory and scaffolding for a brain that couldn't be trusted to think on its own.

The essay argues that construction—building what never existed—is sometimes more powerful 
than recovery. It's about agency rebuilt, not through healing, but through deliberate 
architecture.

The essay is [WORD COUNT] words. It has appeared in [IF PUBLISHED ELSEWHERE] / is unpublished [IF NOT].

I think your readers—who care about earned authority, systems thinking, and genuine 
complexity—would find this relevant.

[PERSONALIZED PARAGRAPH about why THIS publication specifically]

Best,
[YOUR NAME]
[YOUR EMAIL]
[YOUR WEBSITE]
```

## MASTER CHECKLIST: Before Sending Any Pitch

**Every pitch should:**
- [ ] Have [EDITOR NAME] or [REPORTER NAME] filled in (not generic "Dear Editor")
- [ ] Reference something specific about that publication (recent article, coverage area, house style)
- [ ] Be 200-300 words (not longer)
- [ ] Include word count of essay
- [ ] State whether previously published or unpublished
- [ ] Explain WHY this outlet specifically
- [ ] End with clear call to action (attach essay, interested in discussing, etc.)
- [ ] Include your contact info + website (if you have one)

## CUSTOMIZATION GUIDE

### For Each Outlet:

**Research (15 minutes per outlet):**
1. Read 2-3 recent articles that resemble your essay
2. Find the editor/reporter (not "Dear Editor")
3. Note: What did they publish recently? What's their style? What's their beat?

**Customize (5 minutes per pitch):**
1. Open this template for the relevant ANGLE
2. Fill in [OUTLET-SPECIFIC REASON] with something from your research
3. Adjust tone slightly to match publication (Slate is snarkier, Aeon is philosophical, MIT TR is technical)
4. Personalize the greeting
5. Send

## FINAL TIPS

1. **Send all tier-1 pitches the same week.** Don't wait for rejections. Speed matters.

2. **Track what you sent where.** Save a copy of every pitch you send in a folder (Publication + Date). If you revise based on feedback, you'll want to know what angle you pitched.

3. **Personalization > Generic.** A pitch that shows you read their work gets 3x higher response rate than template pitches.

4. **If they ask for revisions:** They're interested. Revise and resubmit within 48-72 hours. This is good news.

5. **Follow-up timing:**
   - Wait 3 weeks for response
   - Send polite follow-up: "Hi [editor], just checking if you got my pitch for [ESSAY TITLE]? Happy to discuss further."
   - If no response after follow-up (2 weeks), assume reject. Move on.

6. **Keep the same essay.** You're not rewriting the essay for each outlet. You're pitching it differently. The essay itself stays the same.
