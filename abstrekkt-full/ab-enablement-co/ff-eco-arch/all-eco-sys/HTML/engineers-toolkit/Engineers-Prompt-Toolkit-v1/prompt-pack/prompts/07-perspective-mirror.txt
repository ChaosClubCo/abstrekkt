PROMPT 07: THE PERSPECTIVE MIRROR
====================================
See your problem through 3 expert lenses instead of 1.

WHEN TO USE:
- Making a decision with multiple valid approaches
- When you're stuck in one way of thinking
- Architecture decisions, career moves, product strategy, vendor selection

THE PROMPT:
-----------

Analyze [TOPIC/DECISION] from three distinct expert perspectives:

PERSPECTIVE 1 — THE EXPERT PAIR:
You are two domain experts having a conversation about this problem.
Expert A advocates for the most common/conventional approach.
Expert B advocates for an unconventional but defensible alternative.
Show where they agree, where they disagree, and what each would
prioritize differently.

PERSPECTIVE 2 — THE SKEPTIC INVERSION:
You are a skeptic who believes the opposite of the obvious answer.
What would someone argue if they had to make the case AGAINST the
popular approach? What evidence would they cite? What risks do the
optimists ignore?

PERSPECTIVE 3 — OPPOSITE DAY:
If you had to solve this problem with the exact opposite constraints
(unlimited budget but no time, OR unlimited time but zero budget, OR
expert team but legacy stack), how would the answer change? What does
that reveal about which constraints actually matter?

End with: SYNTHESIS — What do all three perspectives agree on?
That's your highest-confidence insight.

WHY IT WORKS:
The biggest risk with AI-assisted decision-making isn't wrong answers —
it's narrow answers. Claude will give you ONE perspective confidently.
This prompt forces three, which reveals the actual decision surface.

The synthesis step is key: when three different lenses agree on something,
that signal is much stronger than any single recommendation.

VARIATIONS:
- For career decisions: "Perspective 1: Your mentor who values stability.
  Perspective 2: Your friend who values growth. Perspective 3: You in 5
  years looking back."
- For product: "Perspective 1: Your power user. Perspective 2: A churned
  user. Perspective 3: Someone who never signed up."
- For hiring: "Perspective 1: The candidate's best advocate. Perspective 2:
  The hiring manager who passed. Perspective 3: The team member who'd work
  with them daily."

PRO TIP:
After the Perspective Mirror, feed the synthesis into the Adversarial
Reality Filter (Prompt 04). This creates a two-stage pipeline:
explore → stress-test. It's the closest thing to having a senior
advisor on speed dial.
