PROMPT 06: THE EXPLANATION LADDER
====================================
Get explanations that actually stick — not jargon dumps.

WHEN TO USE:
- Learning a new concept, technology, or framework
- Preparing to explain something to your team
- When Claude's first explanation was too abstract or too technical

THE PROMPT:
-----------

Explain [TOPIC] using this four-step ladder. Complete every step — don't skip any.

STEP 1 — TL;DR (2 sentences max):
What is this thing and why should I care? If I remember nothing else,
what's the one sentence that matters?

STEP 2 — ANALOGY:
Explain it using a real-world comparison. Use something physical and
familiar — cooking, building a house, running a restaurant, organizing
a warehouse. Make the analogy specific enough to be useful, not just
decorative.

STEP 3 — CONCRETE EXAMPLE:
Show me this concept in action. A real scenario, a real command, a real
before/after. Not hypothetical — something I could run, test, or observe
right now.

STEP 4 — TECHNICAL DEFINITION:
Now give me the precise definition with correct terminology. Include the
jargon, the spec references, the edge cases. This is what I'd put in
documentation or explain to a senior engineer.

WHY IT WORKS:
Research on learning retention shows that concepts stick best when you
move from familiar → concrete → abstract. Most AI explanations start at
Step 4 (jargon) and wonder why you're confused. This prompt reverses the
order so understanding builds progressively.

The analogy isn't just a nice-to-have — it creates a mental model you
can hang the technical details on. Without it, jargon floats unanchored.

VARIATIONS:
- For teaching others: "Add STEP 5 — COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS: What do people
  usually get wrong about this? What looks right but isn't?"
- For decision-making: "Add STEP 5 — WHEN NOT TO USE THIS: When is this
  concept the wrong choice? What are the alternatives?"
- For interview prep: "Add STEP 5 — INTERVIEW ANSWER: How would I explain
  this in 60 seconds to a hiring manager?"

PRO TIP:
Save the analogies Claude generates for concepts you use often. Good
analogies are reusable — they're how you teach your team without
re-explaining from scratch every time.
