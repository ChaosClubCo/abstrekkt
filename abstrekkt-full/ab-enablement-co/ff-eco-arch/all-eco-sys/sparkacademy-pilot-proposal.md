## Success Metrics

**Hard metrics:**
- Team enrollment rate: ≥80% of eligible team members enrolled by Week 2
- Course completion rate: ≥50% of enrolled members complete at least one course in 60 days
- Pre/post survey: ≥20% lift in self-reported AI confidence
- Pilot → paid conversion: Target 1 of 1 (Chris commits to paid Team tier)

**Soft metrics:**
- Qualitative feedback themes (what was useful, what missed)
- Identified content gaps (courses I should build next)
- Champion identification (who on his team became the AI advocate)

**Failure criteria:**
- <30% enrollment after Week 2 = pilot structure is wrong
- <20% completion by Week 6 = content not landing
- Chris declines paid conversion = pricing or value prop misaligned

## Legal Framing (Light)

This pilot is informal — no contract required for free access. But include in the kickoff email:

> "This pilot grants temporary Team-tier access to SparkAcademy for [Company Name] from [start date] to [end date]. Either party may discontinue at any time with written notice. Case study attribution requires explicit written approval before publication. No payment is exchanged. Data collected (enrollment, completion, feedback) is used solely for pilot evaluation and may be aggregated anonymously in future product decisions."

That's it. Heavy contracts kill pilot velocity.

## Why This Beats a Cold Launch

Three reasons:

1. **One paying Team customer = social proof for the next 10.** "Chris's team uses this" is worth more than "we have 200 enrolled users."
2. **Real completion data is unavailable otherwise.** Free-tier users have less skin in the game and lower completion rates.
3. **The interview generates 5+ content gaps I can fix before public launch.** That's how good products get built — by watching real teams use them.

The pilot is cheaper than the alternative: launching publicly without ever stress-testing on a real team.
