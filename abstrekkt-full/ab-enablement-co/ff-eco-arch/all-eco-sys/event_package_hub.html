Attendee Walkup

What to bring · What to expect · Logistics

What to bring

Required Laptop — Mac, PC, or Linux. A tablet will not work.

Required Charger

Required A real workflow problem (see below)

Required A Claude account — free at claude.ai. Make one before you arrive.

Optional Notebook and pen — some people think better on paper first

Optional Your current process written out, even roughly

Your workflow problem

This is the most important thing you bring. A good workflow problem wastes 30+ minutes of your week, involves text, has a clear "done" state, and happens repeatedly.

Good examples:

  • "I summarize service call notes into client reports. 45 min. Same format every time."
  • "I write job posts for every open position from scratch."
  • "I onboard new hires verbally because there's no written process."
  • "I respond to the same 8 customer questions every week."

Not a good fit: "I want to build an app." / "I want to learn AI." / "I'm not sure." (Bring a problem anyway — we'll help you find one.)

What you don't need

  • A computer science degree
  • Prior coding experience
  • A paid Claude subscription (free tier works)
  • A specific AI project idea

What you leave with

  • A personalized CLAUDE.md file — your AI's operating instructions
  • A completed participant worksheet
  • A responsible-use checklist for your team
  • Access to the workshop GitHub repo (all materials, free)
  • One working draft of your workflow problem, solved with Claude

Workshop Presentation Script

15 slides · Speaker notes · 90 minutes

Slide 1Title — up when people walk in

"Claude Code for Practical Builders" / "90 minutes. One real tool. No hype." / FlashFusion logo + date + Kyle Rosebrook. No intro yet — let people settle.

Slide 2Who's in the room

Three columns: "You have a real problem" / "You have a laptop" / "You don't need to code." Say: "That's the entire qualification list. My name is Kyle Rosebrook. I'm a self-taught developer and I run FlashFusion in Lake County. I am not here to sell you anything."

Slide 3What you leave with

Four items with icons. Hold up the physical worksheet. "These exist. They're at your seat. We're going to fill them in."

Slide 4What Claude is / is not

Two-column. IS: capable language model, thought partner, draft generator, pattern recognizer. IS NOT: always right, a replacement for your judgment, magic, infallible on facts. Say: "The most important thing I can tell you is this: it is confidently wrong sometimes. Your job is to catch it. That's not a limitation — that's how tools work."

Slide 5Responsible use — hand out checklist now

Show the 10 items. Walk through items 1, 3, 5, and 8 only — paraphrase, don't read. End: "This goes home with you. If you have a team, put it somewhere visible."

Slide 6What is CLAUDE.md

Screenshot of a minimal CLAUDE.md with labels. "This is where we start. CLAUDE.md gives Claude operating instructions for your specific project. Think of it as the briefing document you'd give a very capable new contractor. Yours is at your seat."

Slide 7Anatomy of CLAUDE.md

Template sections with one-line descriptions. Highlight: 'what this solves' and 'what a good output looks like' matter most for first-timers.

Slide 8Your problem — 3 minutes silent writing

"What is the workflow problem you're solving? Write it in 2 sentences. If you need 3, cut one." Give full silence. Then call on 2 people — don't fix their problem, validate the specificity.

Slide 9The build — split screen demo

Open claude.ai. Start new project. Paste CLAUDE.md content. Write first prompt. Read output. Identify what missed. Refine. They follow with their own problem.

Slide 10Prompt anatomy — labeled example

[Role] + [Context] + [Task] + [Format] + [Constraint]. "Every good prompt has at least 3 of these 5. Ask: does Claude know who it is, what I want, and what a good answer looks like? If yes — run it."

Slide 11When it goes wrong

Four wrong output types + one-line fix each. Hallucination: "only use information I provide." Wrong format: give a concrete example. Too generic: add a constraint. Missed the point: rewrite the task sentence from scratch.

Slide 12Debrief — three questions

"What did you build?" "What broke?" "What would you do differently?" Three people, 30 seconds each. Repeat back what they said — don't judge.

Slide 13What's next

Take your CLAUDE.md home. Run it on one real task this week. Come back next month. GitHub repo QR + NotebookLM link + kyle@flashfusion.co. "The only way this stops working is if you stop."

Slide 14Feedback forms

QR code to form or "Forms at the door." Stand at the door. Collect every form. Do not let people walk past.

Slide 15Thank you / contact

Kyle Rosebrook · kyle@flashfusion.co · flashfusion.co. Next session date or TBD.

RESPONSIBLE AI USE CHECKLIST
Claude Code for Practical Builders | FlashFusion

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BEFORE YOU RUN A PROMPT

[ ] 1. I am not pasting personal data — names, SSNs, account 
       numbers, health info — unless I have authorization.

[ ] 2. I am not using Claude as the sole decision-maker on 
       anything affecting a person's health, safety, employment, 
       or finances.

[ ] 3. I know what a correct output looks like well enough to 
       catch a wrong one.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHEN YOU READ THE OUTPUT

[ ] 4. I read the output before I use it. I did not just 
       copy-paste.

[ ] 5. I checked any specific facts, numbers, or dates Claude 
       stated. If I can't verify it, I remove it or flag it.

[ ] 6. Tone and format match what I asked for. If not, I 
       adjusted the prompt — not just the output.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHEN YOU SHARE THE OUTPUT

[ ] 7. I am not presenting AI-generated work as fully my own 
       when disclosure is appropriate — client work, academic 
       submission, regulated industry output.

[ ] 8. I have not removed information that would make the 
       output misleading.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ONGOING

[ ] 9. If Claude produces something wrong, harmful, or 
       off-target, I stop and revise the instruction before
