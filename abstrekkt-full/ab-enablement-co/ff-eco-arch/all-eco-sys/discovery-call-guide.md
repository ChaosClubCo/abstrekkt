# Discovery Call Guide
### FlashFusion | Pre-Demo Discovery Framework

---

## Purpose

Discovery is not a prerequisite formality before the "real call" (the demo). Discovery **is** the real call. A 30-minute discovery call that surfaces the right pain, urgency, and stakeholders is worth ten demos to unprepared buyers.

**The goal of discovery:** Understand the buyer's situation clearly enough to know whether FlashFusion is the right fit — and if yes, to run a demo that speaks directly to their actual problem.

---

## Call Structure (30 minutes)

| Block | Time | Purpose |
|-------|------|---------|
| Open | 0:00–2:00 | Set agenda, confirm time |
| Context | 2:00–8:00 | Understand their situation |
| Pain | 8:00–18:00 | Identify specific problems and urgency |
| Current state | 18:00–24:00 | What they're doing now |
| Commercial | 24:00–28:00 | Authority, budget, timeline |
| Close | 28:00–30:00 | Agree on next step |

---

## Call Opening Script

**SAY:**

> "Thanks for making time. Here's what I was thinking for the next 30 minutes: I want to understand your situation first — what you're working with, what's on your plate around AI and compliance — and then decide together whether it makes sense to see a demo. Sound good?
>
> Before I start, is there anything specific you wanted to cover?"

*[Wait for their answer. Note anything they raise — it's a buying signal.]*

---

## Context Questions (2:00–8:00)

**Goal:** Understand the company, the person, and what's driving the call.

1. **"Tell me about [Company]'s current AI posture — where are you in terms of adopting AI tools internally?"**
   - Listen for: early adopter vs. cautious, specific tools in use, who owns AI decisions

2. **"What's your role in all of this — are you the one making the call on AI tooling, or is there a committee?"**
   - Listen for: decision-maker vs. evaluator vs. champion; who else is in the room

3. **"What prompted this conversation specifically? What changed recently, or what's coming up?"**
   - Listen for: audit cycle, incident, executive mandate, competitor pressure, hiring

---

## Pain Questions (8:00–18:00)

**Goal:** Get them to articulate the problem in their words. Their words > your words.

4. **"When you think about your AI deployments — Copilot, any internal agents, anything you've built — how are you currently tracking what those systems are doing?"**
   - If they have a system: "Walk me through how that works."
   - If they don't: "What would happen if your auditor asked about that tomorrow?"

5. **"Has compliance or security flagged anything around your AI tools yet? Or is that still ahead of you?"**
   - Listen for: previous finding, near-miss, security team friction, board-level concern

6. **"What's your audit timeline look like? SOC 2, ISO 27001, anything else?"**
   - Listen for: upcoming renewal, specific control area flagged, examiner concern

7. **"If I asked your CISO right now 'what are your AI agents authorized to do and what's your evidence?' — what would they say?"**
   - This is often the question that creates silence. Let the silence sit.

8. **"What's the cost of not solving this? What happens if the compliance question comes up in your next audit and you don't have an answer?"**
   - Listen for: specific dollar amounts, contract risk, certification loss, executive visibility

---

## Pain Signal Ranking

| Signal | Weight | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Upcoming SOC 2 / ISO 27001 renewal in next 6 months | 🔴 HIGH | Active urgency. Time pressure = deal velocity. |
| Previous audit finding related to access control or AI | 🔴 HIGH | Already been burned. Compliance scar tissue = fast buy. |
| Security team is blocking AI adoption | 🟡 MEDIUM | Friction you can solve. Confirm security has veto power. |
| Executive mandate to "get AI under control" | 🟡 MEDIUM | Political urgency. Confirm budget followed the mandate. |
| General awareness of the problem, no timeline | 🟢 LOW | Good for pipeline, but nurture not close. Ask what would accelerate. |

---

## Current State Questions (18:00–24:00)

**Goal:** Understand what they're currently doing and what the switching cost is.

9. **"What AI tooling are you using today? Copilot, custom-built agents, third-party tools?"**
   - Note specifics — they'll come up during demo and objection handling.

10. **"How are you currently handling compliance documentation for those tools? Manual process, nothing yet, something else?"**
    - "Manual" = they know it's a problem. "Nothing yet" = they're pre-problem. "Something else" = understand what and assess fit.

11. **"Have you looked at other solutions for this? What did you evaluate?"**
    - Understand competitive context. Don't argue with their evaluation — understand it.

---

## Commercial Questions (24:00–28:00)

**Goal:** Confirm this person can move. Do not ask these before you've established pain — it feels transactional.

12. **"If this turns out to be a strong fit — who else would need to be involved in a decision? Is it you, or is there a team?"**
    - Map the buying committee. Champion vs. approver vs. end user.

13. **"On budget — is this something that already has allocation, or would we need to create a business case together?"**
    - "Already allocated" = move fast. "Need to create a business case" = you become the budget seller, not the solution seller. Decide if it's worth the effort.

14. **"What does your timeline look like? If we found the right solution, how quickly could something get moving?"**
    - "Next quarter" = live deal. "Eventually" = nurture. "This week" = something specific is driving urgency — find out what.

---

## Call Close (28:00–30:00)

**If qualified — propose demo:**

> "Based on what you've shared, I think the demo would be directly relevant — specifically [the ComplianceReport module / the audit trail / the Builder layer]. It's about 6 minutes, live, no slides. I can do it now if you have time, or we can book a dedicated 30 minutes. What works better?"

**If not qualified yet — propose a follow-up:**

> "I want to make sure we're the right fit before I take up more of your time. Can I send you the one-pager and check back with you in [2 weeks / next quarter]? What would need to be true for this to be a priority?"

**If definitely not qualified:**

> "I appreciate the honesty — it sounds like the timing isn't right. I'll follow up when [the audit cycle / your AI rollout / Q3] comes around. Is it okay if I stay in touch?"

## Discovery Call Scorecard

Use this to rate each discovery call immediately after. If average is below 3, do not proceed to proposal.

| Question | Green (3) | Yellow (2) | Red (1) |
|----------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **Pain identified?** | Specific, urgent problem named | General awareness | No problem found |
| **Timeline confirmed?** | Specific deadline (audit, renewal) | Within 6 months | No timeline |
| **Decision maker in room?** | This person can approve or has direct access | Champion who needs to escalate | Unclear, possibly too junior |
| **Budget signals?** | Allocated or willing to create | Needs internal business case | No budget conversation possible |
| **AI compliance awareness?** | They've been asked about it or been burned | They know it's coming | Unaware |

**Score 3+ on 4 of 5 to proceed to demo proposal.**  
**Score 3 on Pain + Timeline (mandatory) regardless of others.**

## Common Discovery Traps — Avoid These

**Trap 1: Pitching during discovery.**  
You're not selling yet. You're learning. Every time you pitch before you've confirmed pain, you reduce your ability to tailor the demo. Discovery first.

**Trap 2: Accepting "we're fine" at face value.**  
"We're handling it" is not evidence of a solution. Ask: "Can you walk me through what that looks like?" Most of the time, "handling it" means "someone is doing it manually once a year and hoping."

**Trap 3: Over-explaining the product.**  
They asked "what is FlashFusion?" You said eight sentences. They glazed over. One sentence: "We're an enterprise AI agent platform with a native compliance layer — your agents run with full audit trails and generate SOC 2 artifacts automatically." Then stop. Let them ask.

**Trap 4: Not asking the commercial questions.**  
Uncomfortable? Yes. Necessary? Absolutely. A beautiful demo to someone with no budget and no decision authority is a beautiful waste of time.

**Trap 5: Agreeing to send "more information" instead of booking a next step.**  
"Send me some materials" is a soft no. Always push for a concrete next step: a scheduled demo call, a follow-up with a specific person, a decision date. "I'll send you the one-pager — can we book 30 minutes to go through the demo next week?"

## Silverfort Analogy (For Kelan's Reference)

At Silverfort: you discovered that companies had identity surface gaps they didn't know were there. The discovery question wasn't "do you have MFA?" — it was "walk me through how a compromised service account would be detected."

Same motion here. The question isn't "do you have AI compliance?" It's: "Walk me through what happens when your auditor asks what your AI agent did yesterday. What do you show them?"

That question surfaces the gap. The demo fills the gap.
