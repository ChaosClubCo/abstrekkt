# AI Setup Audit — Facilitator Kit

**Engagement:** AI Setup Audit
**Investment:** $4,500
**Duration:** 90-minute live session + 48-hour written summary
**Format:** Video call, screen-share both ways
**For:** FlashFusion (Kyle Rosebrook) — internal use only

---

## What this audit is

A structured 90-minute working session that diagnoses where a team's current AI setup is leaking time, money, or output quality. Produces a written summary with the top three findings and a recommended next move (Sprint, Retainer, or no further engagement).

This is a diagnostic, not a sales call. You finish the call having earned the next conversation. You do not finish the call having pitched anything.

## What this audit is NOT

- A demo of FlashFusion products.
- A free consultation. The $4,500 has been paid before the call starts. No exceptions to this rule on this engagement type.
- A training session. The audit identifies where training is needed; it does not deliver training.
- A vendor recommendation. You may name tools but you are not selling anyone else's product either.

---

## Pre-audit prep (T-48 hours)

1. Confirm payment received. If unpaid, the audit does not run. Reschedule.
2. Read the completed Pre-Audit Intake Form (see kit file 02). Annotate it.
3. Spend 20 minutes on the client's company website, LinkedIn, and any public output (blog, podcast, deck). Look for: stated AI position, tools mentioned publicly, team size, what they sell, who they sell it to.
4. Pre-fill the Live Scoring Rubric (Section 5 below) with hypotheses based on the intake form. You will adjust during the call. Do not pretend you arrived without hypotheses.
5. Send a one-line confirmation 24 hours before: time, link, what they should have open (their AI tools, their workflow doc if they have one, their most recent painful workflow example).

## 90-minute live agenda

| Minute | Block | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8 | Open | Restate scope, what they're getting, what they're not. Confirm intake answers. |
| 8–22 | Profile | Their role, their team, what AI they're responsible for. Listen for who else needs to be in the room next time. |
| 22–37 | Tools | Walk their stack. What they pay for, what they use, what they pay for and don't use. Screen-share their tool list if they have one. |
| 37–52 | Workflow | Pick ONE specific recent workflow they hated. Walk it end to end. This is the highest-signal block. |
| 52–67 | Prompting + output | Look at actual prompts and actual outputs from the last 7 days. Not hypothetical. Real. |
| 67–77 | Integrations | Data movement, handoffs, automations. Where humans copy-paste between tools. |
| 77–87 | Synthesis | Tell them the top three findings out loud. Not the full deliverable — the headline read. |
| 87–90 | Close | Confirm the 48-hour written summary. Set expectation for the recommendations call (separate). |

**Rule:** If you find yourself at minute 60 still in Tools, you mis-prepped. The Workflow block is the highest-leverage; sacrifice depth elsewhere to protect it.

## Discovery question bank

Organized by dimension. Pick 3–5 per block; do not run the whole bank.

### Profile (8–22)
- What's your role, and what part of AI strategy sits on your desk vs. someone else's?
- Who else at the company needs to be in the room for AI decisions but isn't right now?
- What did you try with AI in the last 90 days that didn't work?
- What did you try that DID work — and is anyone else on the team copying it?

### Tools (22–37)
- Walk me through what you pay for. Per-seat costs included.
- Of those tools, which one would you cancel today if you had to cut one?
- Which one are you NOT paying for that your team is using anyway?
- What's the last AI tool you signed up for, and what made you sign up?

### Workflow (37–52)
- Pick one workflow from the last two weeks that took longer than it should have. Walk me through it.
- Where in that workflow did you switch tools?
- Where did you do something you'd rather not do?
- If a junior version of you had to redo this workflow next week, what would you tell them?

### Prompting and output (52–67)
- Pull up the last three prompts you wrote. Read them to me.
- Show me an output you used. Show me one you threw away. What was the difference?
- Do you reuse prompts, or write from scratch each time?
- Does anyone else on the team see your prompts?

### Integrations (67–77)
- Where do you copy-paste between tools?
- What runs on a schedule (Zapier, n8n, Make, cron, anything)?
- Where does AI output go after it's generated? Doc? Slack? Email? Trash?
- Is there a place where AI work disappears — produced but not used?

## Live scoring rubric

Fill in during the call. Each dimension scored 1–5.

| Dimension | 1 (broken) | 3 (working but leaky) | 5 (compounding) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Profile | No clear owner; AI happens or doesn't | One person owns it; no peer | Owner + at least one peer; cross-team handoffs work |
| Tools | Paying for what's not used; missing what is | Stack is roughly right; cost not optimized | Stack matches workflow; quarterly review happens |
| Workflow | Steps copy-paste; format drifts; redo common | Most steps repeatable; some friction | Workflow is documented and held by the team, not one person |
| Prompting + output | Prompts re-written each time; quality varies | Some reusable prompts; quality inconsistent | Prompt library exists; outputs reviewed against a known target |
| Integrations | Manual handoffs everywhere | Some automation; not maintained | Automations documented, monitored, owned |

**Top-three findings rule:** finish the call with three things, not eight. Eight findings = no findings.

## Findings synthesis template (use during minutes 77–87)

For each finding:

**Finding N: [one-sentence description]**
- What you saw: [specific moment from the call]
- Why it matters: [concrete cost — time, money, quality, risk]
- What changes if fixed: [outcome in their language, not yours]

Example:
> **Finding 2:** You're paying for ChatGPT Team, Claude, Perplexity, and Otter. Three of the four overlap. One of them is the actual workhorse.
> - What you saw: When I asked which tool you'd cancel today, you said "honestly I'd cancel two."
> - Why it matters: That's roughly $1,200/year per seat in overlap. At ten seats, $12K/year going to redundant capability.
> - What changes if fixed: One designated default, one specialist for the things the default can't do, and budget freed for what's actually missing (which is, based on what you showed me, integration plumbing).

## Continuation paths

After the audit, there are three honest next steps. Recommend the one that fits — do not default to Sprint.

| Path | When to recommend | Investment |
|---|---|---|
| **Enablement Sprint** | Findings cluster in Workflow or Prompting+Output and they have one specific recurring workflow worth fixing. | $5,000 fixed, 3 weeks |
| **Managed Retainer** | Findings are everywhere; they need a thinking partner for 6–12 months, not a one-shot fix. | $2,000/mo |
| **No further engagement** | Findings are mostly Profile (wrong owner) or Tools (wrong stack but they know it). They need to do internal work first, not buy more from you. | $0 — and the audit is still complete |

The "no further engagement" path is the highest-trust move you can make. Use it when it's true. It also keeps the door open for a real engagement later.

## Boundary rules (do not violate)

- **Price floor.** $4,500 is the audit floor. No discounts on this engagement type. The Ryan Anderskow $3,000 case-study trade is isolated and does not apply to other prospects.
- **No employer reference.** Never mention "a managed IT firm," current job, hire year, or role title. FlashFusion is the brand. Period.
- **No inflated claims.** Tool count is ~30 used well, not 240. Job title is not Staff Engineer. AI SME is self-initiated, not employer-given. None of those belong in the audit conversation anyway.
- **No fictional case studies.** When asked "who else have you done this for," the honest answer is whatever the honest answer is. Do not name engagements that didn't happen.
- **The deliverable goes out within 48 hours.** Late delivery breaks the engagement contract.

## Acceptance checklist (call is complete when…)

Walk this list with yourself within 30 minutes of the call ending:

- [ ] Top three findings written down before the next call on your calendar
- [ ] Scoring rubric completed across all five dimensions
- [ ] Continuation path identified and noted (Sprint, Retainer, or none)
- [ ] One direct client quote captured (verbatim, with timestamp if possible)
- [ ] At least one specific moment from the call that becomes the "what you saw" line in the deliverable
- [ ] Calendar block set for writing the 48-hour deliverable (minimum 2 hours)
- [ ] Boundary rules above all held — no inflation, no false reference, no price compromise

If any box is unchecked, the call is not complete.
