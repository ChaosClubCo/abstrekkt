# Week 12 — Capstone + Demo 🏃🎓

**Phase:** Run (final gate) · **Survey gap:** the whole point — turn beginners into shippers
**Goal:** Ship your own app and defend it in a 5-minute demo.

## TL;DR
Your capstone is a real app you conceived, built, deployed, and can defend. It doesn't have to be big. It has to be **yours, working, live, and understood.** This is the difference between "took a course" and "can do the job."

## Analogy
Everything so far was practice service. This is **opening night with the critic in the room.** You plate your own dish and stand next to it while someone tastes it and asks how you made it. That's the capstone: ship it, then defend it.

## What makes a capstone pass
Not scope. Not polish. Three things:
1. **It works.** Deployed, live, does what you said it does.
2. **You own it.** You can explain every part, including anything AI helped with.
3. **You can defend it.** When asked "why did you do it this way?" or "what happens if X?", you have real answers.

A tiny app you fully understand and can defend **beats** an ambitious app you can't explain. Every time. The "defend it" bar is what employers and the Army actually test.

## Scope guidance (keep it finishable)
Good capstones are Week-10-sized or slightly bigger, with one thing that's *yours*:
- A tool that solves a real problem you have.
- Something with an agentic step (an AI doing a real multi-step task you supervise).
- Something you'd actually use or show someone.

Bad capstones: a clone of a huge app you can't finish; anything you can't explain; anything not deployed.

## The demo (5 minutes, structured)
1. **30s** — What it does and who it's for.
2. **2min** — Live walkthrough. Use the real deployed URL. Show it working.
3. **1min** — One hard thing: a bug you fixed, a design decision, where you kept a human in the loop.
4. **30s** — One thing you'd do next.
5. **1min** — Q&A / defense. Expect: "Why this approach?" "What breaks if...?" "Show me the code for X and explain it."

## Defending AI-assisted work honestly
You're *expected* to have used AI — that's the course. The bar isn't "did you write every line." It's "do you understand every line and can you fix it." Say clearly what the AI did and prove you understood it. That honesty + understanding is exactly the "direct and verify" mindset from Week 1, now second nature.

## Technical definition
A **capstone** is an integrative project demonstrating cumulative competency: independent
design, implementation, deployment, and defense of a working system.
